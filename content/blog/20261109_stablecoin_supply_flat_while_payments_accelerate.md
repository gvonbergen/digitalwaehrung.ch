## Stablecoin Supply Flat While Payments Accelerate

Stablecoin circulation grew only 0.4% to $305 billion from January through September 2026, while payment volume surged 45% to $482 billion. The divergence points to higher capital turnover, with existing tokens circulating more frequently in consumer and cross-border transactions, a trend accelerated by AI agents. A cyclical decline in crypto trading demand reduced the need for stablecoins on exchanges, while the high opportunity cost of holding non-interest-bearing tokens also weighed on supply, rather than a structural lack of adoption. Analysts stress the need to separate genuine real-world payment usage from total issuance and overall on-chain transaction volume. Standard bank velocity estimates have doubled to about 30 transfers per month, underscoring the shift from trading-centric liquidity toward utility-driven circulation.

## Hammack Flags Offshore Redemption Gap Under GENIUS Act

Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack argued that the GENIUS Act’s redemption guarantees are largely inaccessible to offshore stablecoin users who lack U.S. dollar bank accounts. She highlighted that major issuers such as Tether and Circle restrict direct redemptions to high-net-worth institutions, forcing retail users to rely on exchanges that may not honor the statutory guarantee. Consequently, Hammack suggested that compliant stablecoins will likely remain a domestic product, while noncompliant tokens continue to dominate international markets. Her remarks expose a practical gap between U.S. regulatory intent and global stablecoin distribution, where legal protections may not reach users outside formal banking rails.

## Samsung Wallet Adds Stripe Onramp for USDC

Samsung Wallet has integrated Stripe’s Crypto Onramp, enabling eligible U.S. Galaxy users to purchase the USDC stablecoin directly within their mobile app using Samsung Pay, credit cards, debit cards, or ACH bank payments. The initiative aims to simplify access to digital dollars and facilitate international remittances by leveraging an existing consumer wallet. Account balances are not insured by FDIC, NCUA, or SIPC. The rollout represents a significant distribution channel for stablecoin adoption, with success dependent on whether users engage in repeat transactions rather than just initial funding.

## BNY Calls for 24/7 Stablecoin Reserve Operations

On June 22, 2026, BNY executive Weinberg advocated for stablecoin reserves to operate 24/7, arguing that continuous operations are a structural necessity to match the real-time settlement cadence of stablecoins. The call highlights the gap between traditional banking hours and the perpetual nature of digital assets, emphasizing the need for infrastructure that supports immediate legal finality, collateral mobility, and continuous verification. The statement serves as a public position from a major custody bank rather than an announcement of a specific product, underscoring unresolved technical and regulatory hurdles facing the industry.

## Clarifying Stablecoin Supply, Market Cap, and Share

The article clarifies that a stablecoin’s circulating supply is altered only by issuance and redemptions, not by token transfers. It further distinguishes between supply changes and price fluctuations, noting that market capitalization can shift due to price movements even when supply remains constant. Finally, it defines market share as the ratio of an individual stablecoin’s market capitalization to the total market capitalization of all stablecoins. These definitions matter for interpreting growth, dominance, and liquidity metrics across issuers, especially when supply stagnation, peg drift, and secondary-market activity can create misleading impressions about underlying adoption.

## GENIUS Act Reserves and Dollar Dominance

Under the GENIUS Act, U.S. stablecoin regulations effective January 18, 2027, mandate that issuers maintain 1:1 reserves in cash, bank deposits, and short-term U.S. Treasuries. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent projects a $2-3 trillion market, positioning stablecoins as structural buyers of T-bills amid rising national debt. The rules are expected to accelerate global dollar dominance by encouraging adoption in high-inflation countries and channeling international savings into U.S. government debt. The framework links monetary-policy objectives, fiscal-financing needs, and stablecoin regulation, making compliance, reserve quality, and redemption access central to the sector’s long-term viability.

## Buterin Reframes ETH as Store of Value

Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin stated that the network’s fee burn mechanism was never intended to serve as a price floor, shifting the narrative toward ETH’s role as a store of value. Despite this clarification, Ethereum faces significant pressure from shrinking stablecoin liquidity and substantial outflows from spot ETFs, which have removed key sources of buying demand. The token remains supported near $2,200 by its relative strength against other cryptocurrencies and its dominance in decentralized finance, but it lacks strong fundamental support as supply inflation continues due to low transaction fees.

## Nigeria Imposes Licensing and Capital Thresholds

The Central Bank of Nigeria introduced Non-Security Virtual Asset rules effective November 5, 2026, giving stablecoin issuers, digital wallet providers, exchanges, and payment providers 180 days to obtain authorization, with registration required within 30 days. Foreign-currency-pegged Class 2 issuers need about $11.12-$11.16 million, equivalent to ₦15 billion, in paid-up capital, while naira operators need ₦10 billion, about $7.41 million. Fines start at ₦100 million. All virtual asset service providers targeting Nigerian customers must register locally, obtain approval before offering tokens, report supervisory information, and verify peer-to-peer traders by matching naira accounts to Bank Verification Numbers. Algorithmic stablecoins and interest payments are prohibited. Securities remain under the jurisdiction of the Securities and Exchange Commission. The measures aim to enhance oversight of digital asset transactions and protect the financial system.

## Nigeria Sets Reserves, Liquidity, and Consumer Protections

The CBN framework is currently in effect and requires stablecoin issuers to fully back tokens with reserve assets, including a 2% buffer, and to keep at least 40% of reserves in cash or highly liquid assets. Customer funds must be segregated, redemption rights guaranteed, and complaint-resolution timelines defined. Enhanced supervision applies to systemically important stablecoins identified by high circulation values or user bases. Experts say the rules strengthen accountability and public confidence, but warn that high capital thresholds may exclude smaller indigenous fintechs and emerging players, urging a flexible, risk-based licensing approach. They caution that overly restrictive requirements could force consolidation, relocation, or migration into informal channels.

## IMF Examines Stablecoins and Currency Competition

The International Monetary Fund held a discussion led by Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva to examine how stablecoins may influence currency competition and cooperation. The session took place amid geopolitical shifts affecting the international monetary system. Researchers from various fields participated to analyze these dynamics in global finance. The discussion underscores rising policy attention to stablecoins as potential channels for dollarization, cross-border settlement, and monetary-sovereignty challenges, especially where local currencies face inflation, capital controls, or limited access to payment infrastructure. It signals that multilateral institutions are monitoring the monetary implications.

## Blockaid Finds Blacklist Recovery Failure

Blockaid’s 2026 report reveals that stablecoin issuers recovered zero funds through blacklists across 60 major exploits, highlighting a critical gap between technical safeguards and rapid attacker movements. Hackers typically convert stolen stablecoins into other assets via decentralized exchanges within 18 minutes, rendering issuer interventions ineffective due to slow manual coordination processes. This underscores the urgent need for automated notification systems and real-time cooperation among security firms, exchanges, and issuers to enhance recovery mechanisms in decentralized finance. Without faster response, blacklist mechanisms offer little practical deterrence for large-scale theft.

## Weekly Stablecoin Supply Drops to $292.31 Billion

Total stablecoin supply contracted by $6.66 billion to $292.31 billion in the week ending November 7, 2026, marking the steepest weekly decline since the Terra UST collapse in 2022. The reduction was primarily driven by redemptions of USDT and USDC, which maintained their pegs at $0.99892 and $0.99945 respectively, indicating a liquidity drain rather than a loss of confidence in collateral. Despite Tether’s reserves covering 108.2% of its tokens, the shrinking cash leg of crypto positions has reduced market liquidity, with analysts monitoring whether supply can recover above $300 billion to signal the end of the downtrend.

## JINGDONG Coinlink Excluded from Hong Kong Licenses

The website of JINGDONG Coinlink, JD.com’s stablecoin issuance entity, has gone offline, coinciding with the company’s exclusion from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority’s first batch of stablecoin licenses. Although the firm was selected for the HKMA’s sandbox in July 2024 and planned to issue a Hong Kong dollar-pegged stablecoin, it did not receive approval in April 2026. The company remains registered in corporate records, while Standard Chartered-led Anchorpoint and HSBC were the sole recipients of the initial licenses. The episode shows sandbox participation does not guarantee regulatory clearance and may signal strategic retreat.

## Visa Expands Stablecoin Rails and Earnings Narrative

Visa has significantly expanded its stablecoin infrastructure, launching a dedicated platform and joining the Open USD consortium to increase settlement run rates across nine blockchains. The company reported Q4 2026 revenue of $12.139 billion, supporting bullish narratives that emphasize digital adoption and new earnings engines like stablecoins and AI agents. Despite this growth, bearish perspectives highlight ongoing regulatory threats, such as the Credit Card Competition Act, which pose risks to Visa’s traditional card economics. The expansion positions Visa as both a payment network and a blockchain-enabled settlement participant, linking stablecoin flows to its core merchant, issuer, and cross-border business.

## Overall Outlook

Stablecoin markets are entering a phase where regulation, distribution, and settlement speed matter more than raw issuance growth. Strong payment volumes and institutional rails contrast with flat supply, ETF-driven liquidity pressure, and security gaps. The next phase will likely be shaped by GENIUS Act compliance, Nigeria’s licensing enforcement, Hong Kong’s selective approvals, and whether weekly supply can recover above $300 billion. Adoption appears real, but fragmented, and the sector’s resilience depends on reserve transparency, automated risk controls, and access for users beyond regulated U.S. corridors.
