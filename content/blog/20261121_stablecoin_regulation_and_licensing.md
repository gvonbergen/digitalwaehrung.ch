## Stablecoin Regulation and Licensing

FDIC and Treasury’s FinCEN submitted final prudential and anti-money-laundering rules to the White House, completing the GENIUS Act framework for stablecoins. Effective January 18, 2027, the regulations impose strict reserve requirements and bar issuers from paying interest or yield to holders, a restriction that has drawn strong industry resistance. The rules create a licensing regime that limits stablecoin issuance to permitted entities, aiming to bring clarity, compliance, and consumer-protection standards to the market before implementation. The submission marks the final step toward operationalizing federal stablecoin supervision.

Payoneer received conditional approval from the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish PAYO Digital Bank, a national trust bank supporting stablecoin services for global businesses. The proposed bank aims to issue PAYO-USD, a fully reserved stablecoin compliant with the GENIUS Act, enabling conversions between digital assets and local fiat currencies inside Payoneer’s cross-border payment network. This preliminary authorization permits organizational preparation, including systems and governance work, but final OCC approval remains necessary before the bank can issue stablecoins and open for business, underscoring the phased federal licensing path.

MoonPay applied to the OCC for a national trust bank charter to create MoonPay National Trust Bank, offering fiduciary custody and comprehensive stablecoin services, including issuance, redemption, and reserve management. The application comes before the GENIUS Act’s January 2027 effective date, which requires payment stablecoin issuers to obtain approval under a new federal framework. If approved, the non-depository bank would operate as a Federal Reserve member without federal deposit insurance, aligning MoonPay with firms such as Circle and Coinbase that have pursued comparable charters to strengthen regulated stablecoin infrastructure.

The United States continues debating federal and state regulatory boundaries for stablecoin issuers, especially the implementation of safe harbours under the GENIUS Act. Proposed transitional safe harbours are not final rules, so announcements should not be treated as established facts without primary-source verification. The debate stresses the need to distinguish changes in market access from actual ownership transfers. Key indicators, including the ten-billion-dollar ceiling and substantial-similarity certification, require precise dating because timing determines how far their effects extend across issuers, states, and future compliance decisions.

Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission proposed a daily cap of 5 million baht on foreign stablecoin transfers to reduce money-laundering risks and prevent circumvention of cross-border regulations. Under the draft, such transfers are limited to accounts held by the same person, while domestic Thai baht-backed stablecoins developed under the central bank’s framework are exempt from the limit. The SEC also plans to require baht-stablecoin trades to occur on exchange trading engines rather than over-the-counter desks, improving market surveillance. Public consultation on the guidelines is scheduled to close in November 2026.

Germany’s Bundestag passed customs reform allowing authorities to freeze and confiscate high-value digital assets of doubtful origin without initiating criminal proceedings. The measure targets holdings above €100,000 and takes effect January 1, 2027, strengthening enforcement tools against illicit flows. It expands customs powers beyond traditional goods, reflecting concerns that digital assets can facilitate cross-border laundering and sanctions evasion. The reform is expected to increase compliance pressure on exchanges, custodians, and businesses handling large cryptocurrency balances, while raising legal questions about due process, asset provenance, and the evidentiary standards required for seizure.

Brazilian central bank officials warned that certain stablecoin transactions constitute money laundering, reinforcing recent regulations that prohibit electronic foreign-exchange providers from using stablecoins to settle payments with foreign counterparties. The statement signals stricter supervisory scrutiny of cross-border crypto rails that can bypass conventional foreign-exchange reporting. For providers, the warning raises compliance costs around transaction monitoring, counterparty screening, and settlement documentation, particularly where stablecoins substitute for wire transfers. The episode highlights a broader pattern: regulators increasingly treat stablecoin settlement not as neutral technology, but as a regulated payment channel subject to anti-money-laundering controls.

## Stablecoin Market Flows and Liquidity

Santiment data show the balances of the 100 largest USDT and USDC wallets on Ethereum fell by a combined $21.86 billion since the start of the year. USDT balances declined $15.14 billion, or 21.1%, while USDC balances dropped $6.72 billion, or 23.7%. Because stablecoin holdings often represent available buying power for crypto assets, the contraction attracted attention. The report cautions that the decline does not necessarily indicate market exit, since funds may have been used for purchases, converted to fiat, moved to untracked addresses, deployed into yield strategies, or shifted across chains.

Aggregate stablecoin market capitalization declined by approximately $4.56 billion over the past week, falling 1.6% to roughly $284.17 billion. USDS, USD Coin, and Tether recorded the largest outflows during the period, pushing total market value below its level from the previous week. Specific outflow amounts for the three tokens were not disclosed. The weekly contraction fits a broader trend of faster stablecoin supply reduction, raising questions about liquidity availability, dealer inventory capacity, and the speed with which capital can move between crypto markets and traditional finance.

Securitize and Ethena launched a new liquidity facility that lets investors exchange tokenized fund shares for stablecoins, providing immediate trading capital that can support Bitcoin purchases. The facility arrives as aggregate stablecoin supply is contracting at its fastest pace since June 2022, prompting questions about dealer inventory capacity for over-the-counter trades. Under the mechanism, market makers can use their own stablecoins to facilitate transactions while clients settle later by redeeming tokenized assets. This bridges the gap between asset conversion and trade execution, giving investors faster access to capital and creating a more flexible liquidity channel between tokenized securities and crypto markets.

ECB Governing Council member Olaf Sleijpen warned that a large-scale run on the growing $300 billion stablecoin market, especially dollar-backed tokens, could transmit stress to traditional finance and force the European Central Bank to reconsider its interest-rate stance. Mass redemptions would require issuers to liquidate substantial US Treasury holdings and withdraw bank deposits, creating systemic risks beyond crypto markets. He said financial-stability tools would be the primary response, but severe market stress affecting inflation and economic activity across Europe could require monetary-policy adjustments. The ECB has increasingly focused on dollar-token dominance’s impact on bank funding, lending conditions, and policy transmission.

A study analyzing six euro-linked stablecoins from 2022 to 2025 found little evidence of persistent speculative bubbles, indicating general price resilience among tokens such as EURC and EURR. The authors emphasize that price stability alone is insufficient: true utility depends on reliable redemption mechanisms, deep liquidity, and seamless integration with existing banking infrastructure. Future adoption therefore hinges less on maintaining the peg and more on operational reliability, counterparty risk management, and market depth. The research positions regulatory frameworks such as MiCA as a foundational, though not causally proven, layer of stability that can support confidence when paired with robust redemption and settlement systems.

## Corporate Stablecoin Infrastructure and Payments

Pulsar integrated TRC-20 USDT deposits into its virtual card service, enabling users to fund accounts and make online payments directly with stablecoins on the TRON network. While Pulsar advertises zero deposit fees, users may still incur blockchain network charges and third-party costs, and the company emphasizes that the feature does not guarantee increased spending volumes. The launch leverages TRON’s substantial stablecoin ecosystem, which reported $2.21 trillion in settlement volume and more than $94 billion in circulating USDT in Q3 2026, to expand access to stablecoin-funded transactions and support everyday payments.

Stablecoin FX platform Codex raised $30 million in a Series A round led by Pantera, valuing the company at $130 million post-money. The capital will scale its automated FX facility, which enables businesses to conduct cross-border transactions using stablecoins such as USDC and USDT to replace costly correspondent-banking rails. The funding follows a tenfold increase in annualized transaction volume to approximately $25 billion in 2026, driven by growing adoption of stablecoin infrastructure for global payments, especially in emerging markets. Codex positions the facility as a liquidity-efficient bridge for exporters, importers, and multinationals seeking faster settlement and reduced intermediary costs.

Western Union launched its Digital Asset Network, enabling cash-out services for the Solana-based USDPT stablecoin across 180 countries and 130 currencies. The network integrates with selected exchanges and wallets, including Bitso, Bybit, and Kraken, to facilitate conversion of USDPT into physical cash at Western Union locations. The initiative supports Western Union’s strategy to use USDPT as an always-on settlement asset for cross-border payments, complementing its existing financial infrastructure. It connects crypto-native liquidity with legacy remittance rails, giving users a regulated cash-access channel while expanding the practical utility of stablecoins for global transfers.

Visa expanded its stablecoin-powered prefunding capability into the Asia-Pacific region through Visa Direct, piloting the initiative with Singapore-based dtcpay. Eligible clients use supported stablecoins to fund cross-border payouts, while recipients continue receiving conventional fiat through bank accounts, cards, or digital wallets. Infrastructure provider Triple-A supports the expansion by handling stablecoin processing, currency conversion, compliance screening, and settlement. The rollout follows successful pilots and commercial launches in other regions, including the United States in October 2026, positioning stablecoins as a bridge between traditional financial infrastructure and onchain systems.

As traditional banks adopt stablecoin rails for settlement, decentralized protocol USDD argues that crypto-native dollars offer superior user freedom through permissionless self-custody, 24/7 global transfers, and transparent onchain reserves. The protocol highlights multichain utility across TRON, Ethereum, and BNB Chain, featuring GasFree zero-fee transfers and automated yield via its Smart Allocator system. USDD positions these structural advantages—bypassing banking hours and providing verifiable transparency—as key differentiators that exceed bank-issued digital dollar alternatives. The framing reflects a competition between bank-led compliance rails and protocol-led self-custody models for control over digital dollar usage.

## Institutional Adoption, Tokenization, and CBDC Pilots

DBS is integrating stablecoin and tokenized deposit services into its transaction banking offerings, allowing corporate clients to convert deposits into USDC via Circle and execute cross-border payments through a link with JPMorgan. The strategy aims to retain clients who might otherwise migrate to crypto exchanges for faster settlement, protecting the bank’s record $208 million quarterly transaction-services fee revenue. DBS also participated in the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s wholesale central bank digital currency trials by purchasing government bills, signaling broader institutional adoption of digital currencies and linking regulated stablecoin rails with central-bank settlement experiments.

Maybank completed a tokenized bond pilot with insurer Etiqa Life, using SettleMint’s Digital Asset Lifecycle Platform to settle bonds against tokenized deposits on a permissioned blockchain. The transaction demonstrated a delivery-versus-payment workflow that integrates tokenized securities, deposits, and custody processes across on-chain and conventional infrastructure. The initiative is part of Maybank’s broader tokenization programme, which includes recent pilots for ringgit tokenized deposits and cross-border payments under Bank Negara Malaysia’s Digital Asset Innovation Hub. It shows how banks can test institutional settlement efficiency while keeping core financial operations compatible with existing market and regulatory environments.

At the Singapore FinTech Festival 2026, leaders from HSBC, Paxos, and Partior debated stablecoins and tokenized deposits as tools for addressing trapped liquidity in multinational businesses. HSBC announced expansion of its tokenized deposit services to eight markets and highlighted the launch of the RedCoin stablecoin. Paxos emphasized the commercial viability of stablecoins for cross-border B2B payments outside conventional banking hours. Partior’s CEO argued that faster settlement alone is insufficient, stressing the need for interoperability across separate banking networks to unlock corporate cash held in multiple institutions. The discussion framed adoption as a coordination problem, not just a technology upgrade.

Singapore’s Monetary Authority announced the Know Your Agent protocol to secure AI-agent interactions with financial accounts, advising consumers against sharing login credentials while developing a framework for verifying agent identity and authority. MAS also repositioned its wholesale CBDC test network as the operational SG Digital Ledger, supporting native tokenized MAS Bills settled directly in central bank digital currency. It released the Guardian Tokenised Collateral Framework to standardize legal and operational requirements for using tokenized assets as collateral, addressing the need for tokens to replicate the legal rights of their underlying securities. The package links AI, settlement, and collateral policy.

The Monetary Authority of Singapore is collaborating with financial institutions to establish common standards for digital money, emphasizing that interoperability and trust are essential for cross-border adoption of stablecoins and tokenized bank deposits. MAS Deputy Managing Director Leong Sing Chiong identified business-to-business transactions and emerging agentic payments as key growth areas, while noting that forming bank consortia does not automatically guarantee increased adoption. The discussion included perspectives from the European Central Bank and the Reserve Bank of India on the need for resilient public payment infrastructure and clear regulatory responsibility as digital payment ecosystems expand.

The Bundesbank and Singapore’s MAS completed Project Simbolon, a successful test using wholesale central bank digital currencies to enable cross-border settlement of euro–Singapore dollar trades. The pilot demonstrated that tokenized settlement infrastructure can connect major currencies across jurisdictions, reducing reliance on intermediated correspondent channels. It adds to the growing set of central-bank experiments linking tokenized deposits, government bills, and stablecoin rails to cross-border payment efficiency. The result supports the broader institutional narrative that digital currencies can improve settlement speed and transparency, while leaving unresolved questions about governance, privacy, and long-term operational integration.

## Quantum, AI, and Emerging Digital-Money Standards

Research by Visa and Circle identified an authority-liability gap in the stablecoin market, where institutions financially exposed to quantum-computing threats lack the control needed to upgrade critical cryptographic infrastructure. The structural weakness creates coordination challenges for banks, custodians, and issuers, because responsibility for security is distributed across decentralized networks that no single entity can fully secure. Consequently, the transition to quantum-resistant cryptography requires complex collaboration to align protection costs with the financial risks borne by users of these digital assets. The finding highlights a governance problem that could become urgent as quantum capabilities advance.

## Stablecoin-Linked Equity Exposure

Stablecoin Development Corporation shares fell 10% to $0.50, extending a broader 64% decline over the past six months without company announcements explaining the drop. The micro-cap firm stakes SKY tokens from the Sky Protocol and reported quarterly revenue of $2.1 million and net income of $50.4 million, although the latter largely reflects asset-value changes rather than operational earnings. Despite holding $7.2 million in cash, the company faces ownership dilution risks from a recent prospectus supplement, while its stock value remains highly sensitive to fluctuations in token prices. The episode underscores how thinly traded crypto proxies can amplify market volatility.

## Overall Outlook

The period points to a maturing digital-currency landscape where regulation, institutional infrastructure, and liquidity dynamics are increasingly intertwined. Stablecoin rules are moving from policy design toward enforcement, while banks, payment firms, and tokenization platforms test real-world settlement paths. Market contractions, systemic-risk warnings, and emerging standards for interoperability, collateral, AI agents, and quantum resilience suggest that the next phase will hinge less on peg maintenance and more on operational reliability, governance, and cross-border integration.
