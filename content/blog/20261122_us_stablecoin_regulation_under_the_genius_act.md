## US Stablecoin Regulation Under the GENIUS Act

The US Treasury Department proposed rules to implement key provisions of the GENIUS Act, creating licensing requirements for stablecoin issuers operating in the United States. The framework defines when digital assets are considered US-issued and sets conditions for offering foreign-issued payment stablecoins, including compliance with US lawful orders and reciprocal jurisdictional agreements. The regulations aim to clarify market-access rules for issuers and digital asset service providers. A public comment period is underway ahead of the law’s scheduled January 2027 effective date, signaling a move toward a more formal US stablecoin regime.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued a final rule implementing the GENIUS Act for payment stablecoin issuers under its jurisdiction. Effective January 18, 2027, it imposes strict requirements on reserve assets, capital, risk management, and redemption. Issuers must maintain at least 5% of reserves in demand deposits and limit single-counterparty exposure to 40%. The rule prohibits paying interest or yield on stablecoins, sets a minimum $5 million capital requirement, and requires an operational backstop to support stability.

## Chainflip Suffers Second Exploit

Chainflip, a cross-chain decentralized exchange, paused its protocol after suffering its second security exploit in ten weeks. On November 21, an attacker drained about $4.86 million from its Ethereum vault, exploiting a reportedly compromised aggregate signing key. Stablecoins, including USDC and USDT, accounted for more than $4 million of the loss, alongside Ethereum assets and over 122 Bitcoin. The theft took less than 10 minutes, and funds were rapidly converted and routed through Tornado Cash, bypassing Circle and Tether freeze tools. Chainflip offered the attacker a 10% bounty to return assets by November 23, while FLIP fell about 31%-34% to near $0.33 and services remained offline with no reimbursement plan announced. This follows prior use of surplus USDC to reimburse an earlier Tron exploit.

## Banks Adopt Tokenized Money Slowly

Fireblocks Senior Director Tim Way said major global banks, which stand to gain most from tokenized money, are adopting it slowly because of core banking system complexities and a preference for minimizing risk to established business lines. Adoption is currently driven by friction reduction in emerging markets and wholesale settings. Banks are treating tokenized assets as new subledgers that require unified security and control frameworks, rather than entirely new systems. Way identified regulatory clarity as the primary gatekeeper for production use. He noted digital assets are shifting from speculative trading to practical value transfer, with potential to expand regional currency settlement in Asia through lower transaction costs and faster settlement times.

## China-Linked Stablecoin Payments Surge for Restricted AI Access

Chainalysis reported a 55% surge in stablecoin payments from China-linked wallets within three days of major US AI model releases, indicating suspected Chinese users increasingly use stablecoins to pay proxy services that provide access to restricted US frontier AI models. About 40% of surveyed proxy services accept stablecoin payments, creating a specialized ecosystem that helps users bypass export controls and access advanced capabilities cost-effectively. The report also cites $104.1 billion in self-custodied stablecoin transfers from China-linked wallets between July 2025 and June 2026, underscoring stablecoins’ growing role in circumventing domestic restrictions on crypto trading. The trend is expected to trigger heightened regulatory scrutiny from US authorities concerned about national-security risks.

## Ethereum Whale Stablecoin Holdings Decline

Santiment data showed the combined USDT and USDC holdings of the 100 largest wallets on Ethereum fell by $21.86 billion in 2026, with USDT balances down 21.1% and USDC balances down 23.7%. The decline points to reduced readily available purchasing power among major cryptocurrency investors and raised concerns about weaker buying power for Bitcoin and altcoins. Santiment cautioned that the figures do not necessarily indicate an overall contraction in the stablecoin market. Capital may have been deployed into Bitcoin, altcoins, redeemed, held in exchange reserves, moved to fiat currencies, or shifted to non-Ethereum chains, meaning the decline reflects changing liquidity placement rather than a simple loss of market depth.

## Singapore Tests AI-Agent Stablecoin Payments

Singapore’s Monetary Authority plans to develop a common protocol for verifying the identity and delegated authority of AI agents conducting financial transactions, and will run a regulatory sandbox to test safeguards before stablecoin payments are executed. The initiative addresses the challenge of confirming that delegated permissions are valid before settlement, because stablecoin transactions are irreversible and lack the recovery mechanisms available in traditional card networks. The sandbox will focus on identity and delegation standards for agentic commerce, payments, and corporate treasury management using digital money, citing stablecoins and tokenized deposits as examples. Launch dates, participating institutions, and the stablecoins in scope remain unconfirmed, and accountability remains unresolved when AI agents make errors or exceed their mandates.

## Ethena USDe Moves to Third Stablecoin Ranking

Ethena USDe moved into third place on DefiLlama’s stablecoin rankings with a market cap of about $4.888 billion, overtaking Sky’s USDS, which fell to around $3.2 billion. The shift was driven primarily by a sharp decline in USDS supply, not by a significant increase in Ethena’s token issuance. Sky said more than $1.5 billion in USDC was withdrawn from its Peg Stability Module, reducing USDS backing capacity. Sky confirmed its USDC balance remained above $800 million and collateral levels were near $9.3 billion, emphasizing that the ranking change reflects supply fluctuations rather than an assessment of safety. The episode highlights how stablecoin rankings can shift rapidly when collateral flows move between protocols.

## Flintrwa Links USDC to Euro Real Estate Bonds

Flintrwa launched a stablecoin-based investment product that converts USDC into exposure to euro-denominated real estate-backed bonds through a single vault. The platform offers a fixed 10% yield with weekly distributions, emphasizing transparency, audited contracts, and a compliance-first structure without token emissions or leverage. The design aims to integrate real-world assets into decentralized finance by giving users predictable cash flows and a streamlined experience. By using USDC as the entry asset, the product connects stablecoin holders to euro-area credit markets while preserving a relatively simple on-ramp. The launch adds to growing competition among tokenized RWA products seeking yield beyond traditional stablecoin returns.

## TRON Stablecoin Supply Reaches $93.7 Billion

TRON DAO reported that stablecoin supply on its network reached $93.7 billion, equal to 33.1% of the global stablecoin market. That represents a $14.3 billion increase over the past year, allowing TRON to gain market share even as overall global stablecoin circulation declined by 5.4%. The growth underscores TRON’s strengthening position in digital dollar infrastructure and suggests users are moving toward networks offering cost-efficient transfers and established liquidity. The data reinforces TRON’s role as a major settlement layer for stablecoins, particularly for low-fee payments, exchange activity, and applications where users prioritize cheap, fast US dollar-denominated transfers over Ethereum-based alternatives.

## Bitcoin ETF Outflows and Stablecoin Liquidity Decline

Bitcoin ETFs recorded $3.47 billion in net outflows in November, including a record single-day exit of $541.1 million. The selling pressure coincided with a $16 billion decline in the stablecoin market since mid-October, with stablecoin supply reported at $14.9 billion by November 12, marking the fastest 30-day decrease since June 2022. The combined reduction in ETF demand and stablecoin liquidity signals significant capital withdrawal from the crypto sector. These flows contributed to Bitcoin’s price falling to $55,300 and raised concerns about the sustainability of current support levels. The episode shows how shrinking stablecoin balances can amplify price weakness when large spot products lose inflows.

## Pulsar Money Adds TRC-20 USDT Card Funding

Pulsar Money integrated TRC-20 USDT deposits, enabling users on the TRON network to fund virtual card accounts and make direct payments without traditional bank transfers. The feature supports zero deposit fees from Pulsar, although users may still incur blockchain network and third-party charges. The launch leverages TRON’s extensive stablecoin ecosystem, reported at $94 billion in circulating USDT and $2.21 trillion in Q3 2026 settlement volume, to expand access to stablecoin-funded spending. By connecting stablecoin balances directly to card rails, Pulsar reduces reliance on fiat banking intermediaries and broadens real-world use cases for stablecoins, especially for users seeking faster, cheaper, and more accessible payment options.

## Pakistan Explores Stablecoins for Remittances

Pakistan’s Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority is exploring the use of regulated stablecoins to reduce remittance costs, potentially saving families hundreds of millions of dollars annually by bypassing traditional SWIFT-based intermediaries. Industry experts, including JazzCash and Bitget, emphasize that realizing these savings depends on the State Bank of Pakistan and commercial banks recognizing stablecoins as equivalent settlement instruments, avoiding liquidity constraints and conversion costs. Successful implementation requires clear regulatory guidelines, integration with local systems such as Raast, and robust compliance measures to ensure stablecoins can be efficiently converted into spendable rupees for recipients. The initiative positions Pakistan among countries testing state-adjacent stablecoin rails for cross-border payments.

## ECB Invites Experiments Under Appia Initiative

The European Central Bank invited industry groups to participate in experiments under its Appia initiative, focusing on tokenized deposits, securities, and shared network governance to build an integrated European financial ecosystem using blockchain. Applications for the first experimental exercises are due by December 11, with proposals evaluated based on technical readiness and expertise, while later submissions will be reviewed on a rolling basis. The effort aims to establish a unified framework anchored in central bank money, with the ECB planning to publish a blueprint outlining findings and proposed standards in 2028. The initiative extends Europe’s digital finance agenda beyond pilot projects toward interoperable tokenized infrastructure.

## Overall Outlook

Regulators are moving faster on stablecoins than markets, with the United States formalizing issuer rules, Singapore testing AI-agent payment controls, Europe piloting tokenized infrastructure, and emerging markets targeting remittance savings. Security incidents, whale stablecoin withdrawals, and ETF outflows show liquidity remains fragile, while TRON’s growth and stablecoin-funded cards suggest continued real-world adoption. The next phase will depend on whether regulation, bank integration, and interoperability can convert stablecoin scale into durable settlement rails.
