## Stablecore Helps Banks Integrate Stablecoins

Stablecore co-founder and COO Nick Elledge said the Dallas-based firm is helping regulated U.S. banks and credit unions add stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and instant payments inside existing banking environments. Its platform is centered on compliance, using a policy engine, blockchain screening, and AML/BSA integrations so stablecoins can operate like native account balances rather than separate crypto products. Elledge said the goal is to help banks retain and attract deposits, earn transaction-fee income, and manage multiple payment rails, including stablecoins, FedNow, and RTP, through a single interface. The approach is aimed at making digital money feel familiar, safer, and easier to distribute within established customer relationships.

## Canada Advances Tokenized Money Rails

Canadian banks and the Bank of Canada are advancing tokenized deposit and stablecoin infrastructure. Major banks are jointly exploring Canadian-dollar tokenized deposits and participating in BIS Project Agorá with other global institutions. OSFI guidance, the Stablecoin Act, the launch of a regulated Canadian-dollar stablecoin, and Payments Canada’s Real-Time Rail are creating regulatory and settlement foundations for 24/7 programmable payments using tokenized deposits and central bank money. Banks are prioritizing tokenized deposits, stablecoin foreign exchange, and tokenized collateral while designing rail-independent custody, policy, and reconciliation controls. Those controls are intended to connect core banking systems to multiple networks, giving financial institutions a flexible way to support future digital-money rails without tying products to one blockchain or settlement path.

## Wells Fargo Prepares for Stablecoins

Wells Fargo and 20 other financial institutions plan to create a stablecoin company, targeting a first-half 2027 launch with an initial U.S. dollar token for cross-border payments and digital asset settlements. The bank separately launched tokenized deposits for corporate and commercial clients in September, enabling round-the-clock fund transfers and expanding to more customers, currencies, and blockchain connections. CEO Charlie Scharf said U.S. stablecoin use cases remain unclear as the bank prepares for potential opportunities. Wells Fargo reported third-quarter net income of $5.95 billion. The combined push shows a large U.S. bank is hedging across both private stablecoins and bank-issued tokenized deposits, even while senior management is cautious about the commercial case for domestic stablecoin usage.

## Citi Favors Tokenized Deposits

Citi CEO Jane Fraser said institutional clients favor tokenized bank deposits over stablecoins because they allow blockchain-based transfers while keeping funds inside the regulated banking system and reducing accounting, tax, and compliance complications. Citi’s third-quarter results showed 16% year-over-year growth in Treasury and Trade Solutions revenue and 17% growth in average TTS deposits, underscoring the growing value of corporate treasury to banks. The bank’s tokenized deposit capability now operates in seven markets and supports 24/7 clearing, giving it a competitive edge in corporate payments versus stablecoin platforms. Fraser’s comments align with other bank executives who see tokenized deposits as a way to meet institutional demand for faster settlement without moving client funds into private stablecoin ecosystems.

## BIS Details Aurum Privacy Design

The Bank for International Settlements said CBDCs can deliver cash-like privacy, with Project Aurum Phase II presenting a digital cash prototype developed with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and other institutions. The design uses encryption and zero-knowledge proofs to verify payments without revealing users’ names or transaction amounts. Commercial banks would register customers, verify identities, and keep records, while the central bank would not access that data; personal details would be disclosed only when legally required. The BIS found that compromising one bank could expose that bank’s customers without revealing other counterparties’ identities, while a validator-level compromise alone would not expose end users. The report also warned that stronger privacy can slow transactions, complicate programmable features, make lost-wallet recovery harder, and leave bank data-leak risks unresolved before commercialization.

## Rain Launches Embedded Stablecoin Wallets

Rain, a stablecoin payments platform, launched the Rain Wallet, an embedded non-custodial wallet that partners can integrate into their applications. It lets users receive, save, spend, and transfer funds while connecting stablecoin balances to Rain’s card, rewards, payouts, and sending capabilities, so funds can be used immediately upon arrival. The wallet supports 12 blockchains, including Arbitrum, Ethereum, Solana, and Optimism, and links to virtual accounts, deposit and withdrawal services, and global payment options. Rain expects to add custodial wallets and balance-earning features by the end of the year. The company says the design helps turn stablecoins into practical money for everyday payments and cross-border transfers, while giving partner platforms a central account structure rather than a standalone crypto wallet.

## Stablecoin Startups Expand Payment Networks

Fintech startups are using stablecoins to build modern payment and payout networks, positioning themselves against legacy providers and attracting venture investment. Rain has grown by adding corporate clients including Western Union, MoneyGram, and Live Nation, launching consumer stablecoin wallets, and applying for an OCC national trust charter for custody and related services. MassPay is expanding stablecoin-enabled payouts for gig workers, marketplaces, and gaming clients, including a new integration with Block’s Cash App. Executives and investors cite regulatory clarity from laws such as the Genius Act as a driver of adoption. The development matters because startups are not only moving value but are also seeking bank-like custody, payout infrastructure, and enterprise relationships that could make stablecoins a mainstream commercial payments layer.

## Tether Co-Founder Warns on Deposit Competition

Tether co-founder Reeve Collins warned that traditional banks could face greater competition for low-cost deposits if stablecoins and digital wallets become the main hub for consumers’ cash, payments, and borrowing. He said banks may have to earn customer relationships again because stablecoins allow money to move across blockchain networks with less reliance on traditional payment infrastructure. For major U.S. banks, the key risks would be deposit outflows, higher funding costs, and weaker profitability if stablecoin adoption shifts customer balances. Collins added that consumers consolidating cash, borrowing, and payments in a single wallet could force banks to offer more attractive rates or seek alternative funding. Key indicators for banks and issuers such as Circle include deposit growth, funding costs, USDC adoption, usage, and reserve-income sensitivity.

## Bakkt and Thredd Build Stablecoin Credit Cards

Bakkt has partnered with Thredd to build a U.S. credit card-as-a-service program that will settle in stablecoins for consumer credit issued by partner banks. Thredd will provide card processing, integrated credit infrastructure, tokenization, digital wallet integration, fraud and risk management, and loyalty services, while Bakkt contributes its regulated stablecoin settlement and cross-border payment rails. The collaboration is designed to simplify integration for banks and potentially improve the economics of consumer credit by reducing settlement friction. An initial proof of concept is targeted for the first half of 2027. The arrangement is notable because it places stablecoin settlement inside a familiar consumer-lending product, linking credit issuance, card operations, and digital-money clearing in one stack.

## Latin America Faces Liquidity Concentration Risk

Varys Capital and Verda Ventures warned that Latin America’s stablecoin payments market could be disrupted if a limited group of wholesale liquidity providers loses access to banking services. They found that only 16 of 494 regional companies are primarily focused on wholesale stablecoin-to-fiat liquidity, corporate treasury, and credit. Even so, stablecoins represented 32.1% of cross-border crypto transaction value, 22.1% of domestic peer-to-peer activity, and 17.6% of personal wallet balances by June 2026. The firms said clearer licensing rules, stronger banking access, local-currency stablecoins, and multiple independent, well-capitalized providers with separate banking relationships would reduce conversion spreads, payment delays, and stranded-funds risks. The finding highlights how concentrated wholesale liquidity can become a regional operational and financial-stability issue.

## BIS Finds Stablecoins Partially Substitute Deposits

A Bank for International Settlements analysis of weekly U.S. data through June 2025 finds that reserve-backed stablecoins and bank deposits are partial substitutes. Higher deposit rates are associated with slower growth in stablecoin market capitalization, suggesting that bank deposit pricing may influence stablecoin demand. The effect is stronger for USDC than for USDT and is not found for non-stablecoin cryptoassets. The BIS says the finding implies that stablecoins can affect bank funding conditions and the transmission of monetary policy, even if they remain a relatively small share of the payments system. For issuers and banks, the result supports the view that stablecoin economics are sensitive to interest-rate environments, reserve yields, and the relative convenience of deposit and stablecoin balances.

## FASB Proposes Stablecoin Cash-Equivalent Rules

FASB's August proposal would let companies classify certain dollar-backed stablecoins as cash equivalents if they are redeemable for cash on demand, have a known redemption amount directly from the issuer, and are backed by segregated short-term highly liquid reserves of at least 100% of outstanding tokens. The proposal would not automatically classify all dollar-pegged tokens, but it would clarify eligibility and require disclosure of cash-equivalent components, including stablecoins, Treasury bills, and money market funds. Firms may elect to present qualifying stablecoins as cash equivalents, potentially improving corporate liquidity reporting, loan-covenant compliance, and treasury operations. Accounting treatment depends on contractual relationships rather than the token itself, as shown by Coinbase’s classification of USDC based on its arrangement with Circle. It would complement the GENIUS Act; comments close November 19, 2026.

## Stablecoin Market Data Show Large Scale

Total stablecoin market capitalization is $307 billion, representing 10.57% stablecoin dominance in the broader crypto market. Twenty-four-hour stablecoin trading volume reached $84.2 billion, indicating high liquidity and active usage across exchanges and off-ramp channels. The figures place stablecoins as a major crypto asset category, though well below total market size measured by other digital assets. The combination of large market cap and high daily volume suggests that stablecoins are being used not merely as speculative stores of value but as payment and settlement assets. For banks, fintechs, and regulators, the scale reinforces why accounting, custody, licensing, and market-structure questions are becoming central to digital-money policy.

## FSB Chair Outlines AI and Stablecoin Work

The Financial Stability Board’s chair outlined FSB work on artificial intelligence and stablecoins, emphasizing regulatory efforts to address risks from digital money and systemically important stablecoins. The FSB’s stablecoin agenda focuses on oversight of major private digital payment issuers, cross-border regulatory coordination, and the financial-stability implications of token-based payments. The reference to AI is also important, as digital-money infrastructure is increasingly intertwined with model-driven risk management, automation, and customer-facing services. The agenda suggests that regulators are trying to coordinate supervision before large stablecoin issuers become too interconnected to manage effectively. It places stablecoins alongside AI as a key digital-economy risk area for global policymakers.

## FSB Flags Global Stablecoin Oversight Gaps

The Financial Stability Board’s G20 report says global stablecoin and national regulatory frameworks remain untested in severe stress and insolvency, with roughly $300 billion in circulation and 99% pegged to the U.S. dollar. It warns that cross-border structures create gaps because issuers may not know all holders, reserves and redemptions span multiple jurisdictions, and supervisory data often relies on inconsistent private blockchain analytics. The FSB also flags regulatory arbitrage and redemption risks, while noting that Europe’s MiCA framework already requires authorized issuance, segregated reserves, and par-value redemption for many stablecoins. The report’s emphasis is on practical supervisory weakness rather than abstract ideology: cross-border stablecoins can outpace national data, licensing, and crisis-response tools if issuers, holders, and reserve custodians are scattered.

## Singapore Considers Stablecoin Labeling Rules

Singapore’s proposed stablecoin framework would allow only licensed issuers to label assets as “MAS-regulated stablecoins,” while permitting recognition of comparable foreign regimes and jointly issued coins. The proposal matters because issuer location, reserve, redemption, and disclosure arrangements can differ among similar dollar-pegged stablecoins. Trading platforms may therefore add regulatory status to price and market-cap data, helping users distinguish authorized issuers from unregulated or foreign-structured products. The Monetary Authority of Singapore is consulting until October 16, 2026, as stablecoins expand into payments, settlement, and tokenized markets. The framework could influence listing standards, custody decisions, and institutional adoption by making regulatory status a visible market differentiator.

## Overall Outlook

Overall, digital money is moving from pilot rails into production banking, corporate treasury, and consumer payment products. Banks are competing on tokenized deposits and stablecoin access, regulators are tightening accounting and cross-border supervision, and startups are building the wallets, custody, and payout channels that make adoption practical. The near-term watchpoints are deposit migration, stablecoin accounting rules, privacy-preserving CBDC design, liquidity concentration in emerging markets, and whether regulatory clarity can keep pace with product innovation.
