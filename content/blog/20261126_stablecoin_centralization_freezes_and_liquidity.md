## Stablecoin Centralization, Freezes, and Liquidity

Stablecoin issuer Tether reportedly advised a client that it charges a 20% "reissuance fee" to return frozen USDT stolen from exchanges, underscoring the opaque, centralized recovery processes in crypto. The disclosure arrives during a record year for stablecoin freezes, with Tether and Circle freezing approximately $9 billion in 2026, largely tied to high-profile hacks attributed to North Korean actors. These interventions highlight growing tensions over centralization, as issuers exercise bank-like authority to halt transactions and control token movement. The practice has contributed to a recent $17 billion decline in the overall stablecoin market cap, reinforcing concerns about issuer discretion, transparency, and systemic trust.

Cryptocurrency markets recorded approximately $12 billion in net stablecoin redemptions during the 30 days ending November 13, 2026, across the 11 major stablecoins tracked by Kaiko. Shrinking dollar liquidity and withdrawals from U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs drove the contraction, mirroring patterns observed during the 2022 bear market. Major stablecoins Tether’s USDT and Circle’s USDC each lost about 4% of circulating supply, reducing liquidity available for trading digital assets. Capital primarily left the crypto ecosystem rather than rotating within it, coinciding with expectations of tighter Federal Reserve policy. Although redemption pace moderated since mid-November with a modest USDC recovery, stablecoin market capitalization remained down nearly 6% over the period.

Stablecoin supply contracted by $14.2 billion over the 30 days ending November 9, 2026, marking the fastest decline since June 2022 and representing approximately 4% of the total market. Circle’s USDC absorbed a disproportionate share of these redemptions compared with Tether’s USDT, with data indicating the capital primarily exited to fiat rather than rotating into other crypto assets such as Bitcoin. While the absolute drop echoes the 2022 bear market, analysts note the current contraction is driven by regulatory pressures and macroeconomic de-risking, not systemic protocol failure. Major issuers maintained sufficient reserves to honor redemptions at par, preserving confidence in redemption mechanics.

## Merchant Adoption, Payments Networks, and Commerce Data

Glassnode’s “Stablecoins in Commerce” report reveals that on-chain stablecoin transfer volumes do not accurately reflect where merchant payments occur. USDT on Tron and BNB Chain accounted for 75% of NOWPayments pay-ins despite representing only 14% of total on-chain volume. The study indicates that tracking active addresses aligned more closely with actual payment patterns than raw transfer data, highlighting divergence between trading activity and real-world commercial usage. These findings suggest measuring stablecoin adoption by volume alone may misrepresent utility, because payments concentrate on specific networks chosen for cost and accessibility rather than the most heavily traded chains.

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller confirmed that stablecoins are already being used behind the scenes to settle obligations within card networks, with Visa’s stablecoin settlement run rate exceeding $25 billion annually and Mastercard acquiring infrastructure provider BVNK for up to $1.8 billion. Despite this integration, stablecoin volumes remain negligible compared with traditional payment rails, representing only about 0.1% of Visa’s annualized transaction volume. The analysis concludes that stablecoins are not a threat to Visa and Mastercard, because these incumbents leverage entrenched network effects and a history of technological adaptation to absorb innovation rather than be displaced by it. The finding reframes stablecoin adoption as incremental infrastructure integration.

Noah, a UK-based stablecoin payments infrastructure provider, is powering Currency Accounts for Tria, a self-custodial neobank, enabling users to transfer euros from traditional bank accounts directly into USDC in their wallets. This service leverages Noah’s regulated infrastructure to automate SEPA and SWIFT transfers and handle compliance, eliminating the need for centralized crypto exchanges or separate conversion steps. The initiative aims to simplify funding self-custodial wallets by making bank transfers seamless, with plans to expand support to USD and automated local currency payouts. It demonstrates how regulated fiat-to-stablecoin rails can reduce friction for users seeking direct access to on-chain balances without relying on exchange custody.

## Stablecoin Market Stress and Exchange Dynamics

The dollar-pegged stablecoin USDS surged to approximately four times its global price on South Korean exchanges, driven by deposit blocks at Upbit, Bithumb, and Coinone that severed arbitrage channels. This “fishbowl market” emerged after the exchanges designated USDS as a caution-listed asset following withdrawal disruptions in Sky Protocol’s lending product, where significant debt was concentrated among top borrowers. Experts warn that the inflated premium is unsustainable and will likely vanish rapidly once normal deposits resume, exposing late buyers to substantial losses as the token reverts to its $1 peg. The episode illustrates exchange listing decisions, liquidity fragmentation, and lending stress can distort stablecoin prices.

## Stablecoin Issuance Platforms, Partnerships, and Treasury Vehicles

The Cardano Foundation confirmed the launch of Brale, a service enabling businesses to issue custom USD stablecoins on the Cardano blockchain across 30 networks. Powered by CIP-113, the platform allows issuers to attach specific rules to tokens, including transfer restrictions and freezing capabilities, supporting regulated environments that need compliance controls. Despite the infrastructure launch, the Foundation has not disclosed initial issuing partners or expected volume. The announcement coincides with a period where ADA’s price remains in an oversold technical zone, highlighting the gap between technical readiness and immediate commercial adoption. Brale positions Cardano as a programmable settlement layer for asset issuers seeking bespoke stablecoin rules.

Ant International and BNY signed a memorandum of understanding to explore next-generation treasury, 24/7 foreign exchange, and global payments solutions, with a specific focus on tokenized deposits and money market funds. The collaboration aims to enhance interoperability between tokenized and traditional financial instruments to support international commerce, subject to regulatory approvals. It builds on Ant International’s recent AI-driven initiatives to improve liquidity management and cross-border settlement capabilities. By combining BNY’s institutional cash-management expertise with Ant’s real-time treasury infrastructure, the partnership targets corporate clients seeking faster liquidity, reduced settlement friction, and greater access to tokenized financial products.

Stablecoin Development Corporation shares fell 9% to $0.47 amid broader weakness in financial stocks, with no company-specific news explaining the decline. The company, which holds digital assets such as the SKY token to earn staking rewards, faces ongoing risks from potential share dilution due to recent shelf registrations. SDEV has experienced a 64% decline over the past six months, reaching new 52-week lows despite reported net income of $50.4 million. The episode illustrates how treasury-style crypto firms can trade on macro sentiment, balance-sheet opacity, and financing risk rather than earnings, while stablecoin-linked strategies remain exposed to token-price volatility and dilution concerns.

Japanese financial giants SBI Holdings and Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group launched a joint venture with the Solana Foundation, named SBI Solana Global, to develop stablecoin payment infrastructure and tokenize financial assets. The venture aims to expand onchain financial services across Asia by supporting domestic and cross-border remittances, real-world asset tokenization, and AI-agent micropayments. This initiative builds on SBI’s existing involvement in dollar- and yen-denominated stablecoins, including USDC trading and the JPYSC stablecoin. By pairing Solana’s high-throughput network with Japanese banking and capital-markets expertise, the partnership seeks to create regulated rails for payments, asset issuance, and automated financial workflows, extending tokenized finance beyond trading venues.

DBS has partnered with Circle to enable eligible institutional clients in Asia to mint and redeem USDC directly through the bank, offering 24/7 USD-USDC settlement. The collaboration leverages Circle’s regulated stablecoin infrastructure and DBS’s digital asset capabilities to provide institutions with a secure bridge between traditional banking and blockchain-based financial systems. The initiative aims to reduce reliance on secondary market liquidity and enhance treasury management efficiency. It builds on prior participation in Project BLOOM to support interoperability and digital asset innovation in Singapore, giving corporates direct access to issuance and redemption rails that can streamline cash management, settlement, and tokenized treasury operations.

## Institutional Reconciliation and Tokenized Deposit Infrastructure

Utila and Cordant launched a design-partner program in Q4 2026 that enables regulated institutions to reconcile stablecoin and tokenized deposit activity with existing banking and treasury systems. The solution links data on a read-only basis to identify funds in transit and unify compliance signals, addressing discrepancies between blockchain, ledger, and bank records without holding funds or keys. The initiative aims to streamline institutional reconciliation as stablecoin payments reach approximately USD 390 billion annually. By providing visibility into movements across multiple systems, the partnership targets operational inefficiencies, audit gaps, and settlement delays that arise when regulated entities adopt programmable money alongside conventional banking rails.

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority launched the CMU Digital Asset Platform, a private distributed-ledger extension of the Central Moneymarkets Unit operated by CMU OmniClear. It enables round-the-clock, multi-currency settlement of tokenized bonds using cash tokens and atomic delivery-versus-payment, reducing settlement risk and improving efficiency. Ten bank participants are listed initially, and the platform integrates with central bank digital currency and Real-Time Gross Settlement systems. The initiative follows the expansion of Hong Kong’s Project Ensemble pilot, which upgraded interbank settlement for tokenized deposits using tokenized central bank money. Future phases plan to support tokenized deposits, regulated stablecoins, funds, and real-world assets, broadening the infrastructure beyond debt securities.

The Monetary Authority of Singapore issued SGD 100 million, equivalent to USD 77 million, of tokenized MAS Bills on its Singapore Dollar Test Network, settling the transaction using wholesale central bank digital currency. The native issuance involved five primary dealers and received oversubscribed applications, testing distributed-ledger infrastructure while eliminating reconciliation needs and intermediaries. MAS plans to expand the use of these tokenized bills for collateral management and secured lending in future live trials scheduled for 2027. The pilot demonstrates how sovereign short-term debt can be issued, settled, and used as collateral on-chain, providing a template for institutional tokenized finance and supporting Singapore’s interoperability standards for stablecoins and tokenized deposits.

The Bank of Korea is expanding its Project Hangang central bank digital currency pilot to include deposit tokens for automated subsidy payments and plans to launch a government bond tokenization pilot in 2027 linked to its institutional CBDC. Officials emphasized that these tokens, distinct from stablecoins, offer instant settlement and legal finality for inter-institutional transactions. The bond-tokenization initiative is aimed at enhancing collateral mobility and reducing funding costs in the repo market. The central bank intends to integrate these functions into private jurisdictional ledgers rather than public blockchains, while also advancing cross-border payment initiatives through Project Agora. The approach prioritizes privacy, control, and compatibility with regulated financial infrastructure.

Qivalis, a consortium of 37 European banks, is launching a euro-denominated stablecoin designed to provide institutional-scale liquidity for 24/7 cross-border settlements and tokenized asset trading. The project aims to complement existing digital money forms, such as the ECB’s Digital Euro and tokenized deposits, by offering a programmable, interoperable settlement layer that reduces reliance on non-European currencies. By enabling instant, conditional payments backed by euro reserves, Qivalis seeks to enhance Europe’s financial autonomy and support migration of value to on-chain infrastructure. The initiative positions a bank-backed euro stablecoin as a bridge between traditional banking settlement and programmable finance, while preserving monetary sovereignty within the European monetary system.

Switzerland’s National Bank and major financial institutions are exploring the launch of the Swiss Financial Ledger, a permissioned blockchain infrastructure designed to facilitate seamless exchange of tokenized central bank money, commercial bank deposits, and securities. The initiative aims to maintain monetary sovereignty and financial stability by providing a controlled alternative to widely adopted U.S. stablecoins, which are increasingly gaining popularity in global markets. The project builds on international efforts such as the BIS’s Project Agora, seeking to create a national ledger that supports efficient, 24/7 financial interactions while preserving the existing two-tier banking system. It reflects a broader European effort to keep settlement infrastructure domestic and regulated.

The Clearing House’s On-Chain Money Initiative aims to bridge tokenized deposits with existing payment rails, enabling programmable, 24/7 settlement for regulated commercial bank money. Unlike stablecoins, these deposits retain their status as bank liabilities while adding blockchain-based automation, with interoperability across institutions identified as a critical requirement for scaling. By partnering with Quant to synchronize on-chain transactions with fiat movements over networks such as RTP and CHIPS, the initiative seeks to integrate digital capabilities into broader banking infrastructure. A planned launch in the first half of 2027 would give U.S. banks a tokenized settlement layer that preserves liability status, regulatory clarity, and integration with legacy payment systems.

## Regulatory Proposals and Supervisory Actions

Taiwan’s Central Bank proposed draft regulations requiring stablecoin issuers to deposit approximately 11% of their backing assets with the central bank. The reserve requirement, set at 10.775%, applies to issuers minting stablecoins denominated in both domestic and foreign currencies. The proposal aims to mirror reserve ratios applied to traditional financial institutions, strengthening stability and oversight in the digital asset sector. By imposing a central-bank deposit obligation, Taiwan seeks to reduce issuer discretion over collateral and limit systemic risk from rapid redemptions. The move signals a broader shift toward treating stablecoins as quasi-banking products, with implications for cross-border issuers serving Taiwanese users.

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission introduced the Digital Asset Industry Act, permitting issuance of won-denominated stablecoins exclusively by consortia in which banks hold a majority stake. Issuers require FSC approval, strict equity minimums, 100% liquid asset reserves, cap exchange shareholding at 20%, require exchange licensing, and meet oversight requirements for systemic stablecoins exceeding one trillion won. The Bank of Korea retains influence through a joint committee overseeing aggregate issuance, reserve composition, and data access, though it lacks an explicit supply veto. The bill legalizes initial coin offerings with prospectus-like disclosures, and implementation of a 22% crypto tax appears likely delayed until 2029. Approval is expected in 2027, full implementation likely 2028. It targets cost reductions in remittances and merchant fees, challenging dollar stablecoins controlling nearly 99% of the global market.

The European Banking Authority warned that retail investors channeling stablecoins into decentralized finance platforms face significant risks of total asset loss due to weak disclosures, excessive leverage, and operational failures. The regulator urged Brussels to impose stricter rules on crypto-asset service providers that facilitate access to lending protocols, citing concerns that fully decentralized arrangements allow firms to bypass MiCA regulations. The EBA report highlights growing consumer-protection risks and potential regulatory arbitrage as CASPs and AI tools make DeFi easier for EU consumers. It calls for enhanced protections, including suitability checks and leverage limits, within the upcoming MiCA review, reflecting stablecoins’ central role in DeFi liquidity, collateral, and settlement.

The European Securities and Markets Authority issued an opinion directing licensed crypto-asset service providers to cease all services, explicitly including custody, for stablecoins that do not comply with MiCA. Licensed exchanges must remediate client exposures to non-compliant tokens such as USDT by January 8, 2027, allowing only sell-only, conversion, transfer, or withdrawal functions during the wind-down period. The ruling closes the custody loophole previously used by EU exchanges to hold USDT for clients, forcing conversions to compliant assets such as USDC or requiring users to move tokens to self-custody wallets. It marks a decisive step in enforcing MiCA on stablecoin availability and custody.

Singapore’s central bank is establishing common standards for interoperable stablecoins and tokenized deposits to facilitate cross-border payments, following the successful issuance of SGD 100 million in tokenized bills settled via wholesale CBDC. Concurrently, Ukraine’s central bank and securities regulator agreed on a revised draft law to regulate digital assets, aiming to align the country’s crypto market with the EU’s MiCA framework by July 2027. These developments highlight diverging regulatory maturity, with Singapore advancing technical infrastructure for tokenized finance while Ukraine moves to establish basic legal supervision for its large digital asset market. Both aim to reduce fragmentation and strengthen investor protection.

## Overall Outlook

The week points to a bifurcated stablecoin market: liquidity is contracting amid macro tightening and regulatory uncertainty, while institutional infrastructure is expanding through tokenized deposits, sovereign ledgers, and bank-backed stablecoin rails. Centralized freezes, MiCA enforcement, and reserve requirements show regulators and issuers asserting more control, increasing transparency demands. Competition is shifting from simple issuance toward interoperability, settlement efficiency, and compliance. Stablecoins are becoming embedded in payments and treasury systems, not displacing incumbents outright, while sovereign projects seek alternatives to dominant dollar coins. The key 2027 inflection points are regulatory clarity, redemption confidence, and scalable institutional adoption.
