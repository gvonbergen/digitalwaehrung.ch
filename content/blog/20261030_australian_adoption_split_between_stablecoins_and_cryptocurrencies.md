## Australian adoption split between stablecoins and cryptocurrencies

Australian financial institutions are increasingly preferring stablecoins, while consumers continue favoring cryptocurrencies, creating a notable adoption split. The divergence shows institutional demand for predictable, low-volatility settlement assets, often used for payments, liquidity management, and treasury operations, contrasted with retail appetite for speculative and decentralised digital assets. The trend suggests Australia’s digital-asset market is developing along two tracks: regulated financial entities building use cases around price-stable tokens, and consumers engaging with broader crypto markets. This split may shape future licensing, consumer-protection, and payment-system reforms as policymakers seek to reconcile institutional stability preferences with retail crypto participation.

## Circle seeks SEC treatment of stablecoins as cash

Circle has asked the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission to treat GENIUS-compliant payment stablecoins as cash under proposed crypto fundraising rules. The issuer wants the existing 2% broker-dealer capital haircut for such assets reduced to zero, arguing stablecoin payments should not impose extra capital burdens on companies raising funds. The proposal fits Circle’s push for technology-neutral regulation that clarifies how stablecoins function in onchain capital formation and settlement. If accepted, the change could lower friction for tokenised fundraising, encourage compliant stablecoin use in securities transactions, and strengthen the case that regulated payment stablecoins are settlement instruments rather than securities-like assets.

## Coinbase reports resilient fourth-quarter earnings

Coinbase reported fourth-quarter fiscal-year 2025 revenue of $1.216 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $333 million, with stablecoin and subscription revenues helping diversify results amid soft crypto markets. Its trading share rose for a fourth consecutive quarter, showing resilience in the core exchange business despite volatile asset prices. The company also narrowed its expense guidance for fiscal 2026, signalling greater cost discipline after a period of market weakness. The mix of stablecoin-linked income, subscriptions, and recurring services underscores Coinbase’s effort to reduce dependence on spot trading cycles and position itself as a broader digital-asset platform with payments, custody, and institutional infrastructure revenue streams.

## Stablecoin Development Corporation posts SKY-driven profit

Stablecoin Development Corporation posted third-quarter 2026 net income of $50.4 million, driven by a $58.8 million unrealized gain on its SKY token holdings and $2.1 million in staking revenue. The company now holds about 2.32 billion SKY tokens, roughly 10% of total supply, valued at $180.2 million and representing 96% of total assets. It has no debt and raised $45.9 million through an at-the-market offering to deepen public-market access to the Sky Protocol stablecoin ecosystem. The result reflects capital deployed from a January 2026 private placement, including stablecoins and SKY tokens, followed by open-market purchases, highlighting a concentrated on-chain treasury strategy.

## Sky Frontier Foundation discloses near-10% stake

Sky Frontier Foundation disclosed deemed beneficial ownership of approximately 9.99% of Stablecoin Development Corporation’s common stock, equal to 8,115,087 shares as of October 21, 2026. The calculation includes shares issuable upon warrant exercise, subject to a Beneficial Ownership Limitation that caps the foundation below the 10% threshold. The foundation has not exercised any warrants, so the stake remains a potential economic interest tied to future conversion. Its nominee, David Garcia Rios, was appointed to the board with a term ending at the 2027 annual meeting. The disclosure signals continued strategic alignment between the foundation and the public vehicle pursuing exposure to the Sky Protocol stablecoin ecosystem.

## Moonshot conference frames stablecoins as payment infrastructure

At Moonshot 2026, industry leaders argued that stablecoins should optimise financial systems by improving cross-border payment efficiency and liquidity, especially across Africa, rather than replace traditional banking. Speakers from Flutterwave and Paga stressed the need for shared infrastructure, deeper liquidity, and cooperation among fintechs, regulators, and policymakers to reach mainstream adoption. They pointed to a positive shift in regulatory engagement in Nigeria, suggesting that constructive dialogue and responsible implementation are essential to embedding stablecoins as core payment rails. The discussion framed stablecoins as complementary tools for reducing frictions in remittances and commerce, while underscoring that interoperability, supervision, and local policy alignment remain central to scaling them responsibly.

## DBS survey shows enterprise tokenized-money pilots

A DBS survey found that 88% of large businesses are deploying or piloting tokenized money, with stablecoins the most adopted instrument at 63%, followed by tokenized deposits at 53%. Firms cite faster payments and improved liquidity management as primary drivers, helped by regulatory developments in the United States and Singapore and the arrival of bank deposit tokens such as JPMorgan’s JPMD. Despite broad experimentation, mainstream use remains uncertain because many projects are still pilots and stablecoin activity has not shown sustained growth. The findings suggest enterprises are testing digital settlement options for efficiency gains, while full-scale adoption depends on regulatory clarity, interoperability, and demonstrable benefits beyond proof-of-concept deployments.

## HSBC embeds RedCoin in retail payments

HSBC launched RedCoin, a licensed Hong Kong dollar-backed stablecoin, inside its PayMe app on October 23, 2026, reaching more than 3.3 million users for instant retail payments. Operating under Hong Kong Monetary Authority approval, the bank plans to expand the token into tokenized investment products and wholesale applications, embedding it within a broader digital-assets ecosystem. Authorities in Hong Kong and the European Union clarified that such tokens are not protected by statutory deposit-guarantee schemes, including the €100,000 safeguard, because they represent contractual redemption claims against the issuer. The initiative marks a strategic move to normalise stablecoins as integrated money inside familiar payment channels, while making reserve backing and redemption terms central to user risk assessment.

## OpenTrade appoints former Swift chief as chair

Former Swift chief executive Lazaro Campos has been appointed chairman of OpenTrade, a London-based provider of yield infrastructure for stablecoin balances, as the company moves from early growth toward scaling. Campos brings experience across global financial networks and regulatory bodies, supporting OpenTrade’s expansion with fintechs, neobanks, and decentralised finance participants. The firm says it has processed more than $1 billion in transaction volume and holds about $300 million in total value locked, following a recent $17 million funding round. The appointment highlights the growing appeal of stablecoin yield products to traditional finance expertise, positioning OpenTrade to bridge banking systems with tokenized dollar and euro assets through permissioned and permissionless rails.

## Swiss banks call for CHF stablecoin framework

Swiss banking associations have called for a clear regulatory framework enabling domestic institutions to issue Swiss franc stablecoins, arguing banks should be able to do so under existing banking licences rather than through separate subsidiaries. The associations say this would keep value creation and supervision in Switzerland, support cross-border payment efficiency, and prevent foreign providers from dominating, especially where competitors benefit from the EU’s MiCAR, the U.S. GENIUS Act, and German-led initiatives. They note benefits for B2B payments and tokenized markets, while warning emission volumes above CHF 5 billion could affect bank funding and deposit-outflow risks must be managed. Flexible reserve requirements are recommended because Switzerland’s smaller market for high-quality liquid assets limits conventional options, with industry tests expected to validate applications by December.

## Maerki Baumann targets corporate stablecoin integration

Maerki Baumann’s Tech Banking team will host an executive workshop on November 19, 2026, focused on practical stablecoin integration into corporate payments and liquidity management. The session is aimed at chief financial officers and treasury leaders who need operational guidance beyond speculative investment narratives. It will cover acquisition, secure custody, and regulatory compliance, showing how stablecoins can be incorporated into existing financial processes with measurable business value. The event reflects rising demand from corporates seeking usable digital-dollar rails for treasury operations, payments, and cash management. By emphasising governance, security, and compliance, the workshop signals that stablecoins are moving from experimental balance-sheet items toward mainstream corporate finance infrastructure.

## Bank of England consults on tokenized collateral

The Bank of England opened a consultation on supervisory expectations for central counterparties that want to accept tokenized collateral, clarifying that existing rules allow this when assets satisfy liquidity, valuation, and risk standards. Draft guidance stresses that tokenized assets must remain capable of being valued, transferred, and realised even under stressed market conditions. The consultation aims to support innovation in financial-market infrastructure while preserving stability, recognising that tokenization can improve collateral mobility but also introduces operational, legal, and liquidity complexities. Responses are due by February 12, 2027. If finalised, the guidance could give market participants clearer rules for using tokenized securities, cash-like instruments, and other digital collateral in clearing and settlement.

## Water.org expands Ripple stablecoin disbursements

Water.org expanded its partnership with Ripple to use Ripple USD, a regulated dollar-backed stablecoin, for cross-border disbursements, cutting transaction fees by up to 66% and reducing settlement times from days to same-day. The integration has supported water and sanitation financing for more than one million people in Latin America. Ripple has committed $6 million in funding and a $1.25 million stablecoin grant to extend the model to Indonesia, the Philippines, and Uganda. The programme is presented as a concrete example of stablecoins lowering costs and accelerating delivery in high-fee emerging-market corridors, showing how digital settlement rails can improve humanitarian aid efficiency compared with traditional remittance channels.

## Lagarde departure leaves digital-euro succession question

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde will step down on February 7, 2027, an early exit that opens a succession contest during a pivotal period for the digital euro and MiCA enforcement. Lagarde has favoured tokenised settlement infrastructure anchored by central-bank money rather than private stablecoins, citing financial-stability risks, and personally intervened to delay Binance’s MiCA licence bid in Greece over concerns about dollar-based stablecoins. Her successor’s position on the digital euro, scheduled for a pilot in mid-2027, will strongly influence Europe’s approach to crypto regulation, stablecoin oversight, and the balance between public and private digital money. The transition may determine whether Europe prioritises sovereign digital settlement rails or accommodates market-led stablecoin growth.

## Tokenized stocks expand collateral in decentralized finance

The tokenized-stock market has expanded to roughly $3.74–$3.84 billion in distributed value, a 17.79% increase over the past thirty days, with bStocks, Ondo, and xStocks leading the sector. bStocks is the largest platform at about $1.4 billion, while monthly transfer volumes have reached $10.14 billion. A notable finance development is the integration of seven major U.S. stocks, including Apple and Microsoft, as collateral on Aave V4, allowing users to borrow stablecoins such as USDC against tokenized equity positions. This linkage deepens the overlap between equity tokenization and decentralized lending, creating new capital-efficiency opportunities while raising questions about collateral quality, oracle accuracy, liquidation mechanics, and regulatory treatment of tokenized securities.

## Stablecoin transfers accelerate at MERGE Madrid

Executives speaking at MERGE Madrid 2026 said cross-border stablecoin transfers rose 80% between June 2025 and June 2026, reflecting a shift toward digital assets for real-world transactions. Banks are increasingly positioning themselves as infrastructure providers for stablecoins and tokenized deposits, moving beyond crypto trading to support services such as Visa’s seven-day settlement and Western Union’s USDPT remittance rails. Highlighted initiatives include expansion of the Qivalis euro-stablecoin consortium and the launch of Bit2Me’s STX platform for tokenizing securities. The discussion stressed interoperability and operational integration over isolated blockchain networks, signalling that stablecoins are becoming embedded in cross-border payments, securities settlement, and enterprise liquidity management.

## MiCA deadline pushes Coinbase users toward DEXs

Coinbase’s October 30 deadline to restrict non-compliant stablecoins under the European Union’s MiCA framework disrupted the regional market and pushed traders toward decentralised exchanges. Assets affected include Tether’s USDT, PayPal’s PYUSD, and DAI, which can no longer be traded freely on Coinbase’s centralised platform. Decentralised exchanges offer an alternative route through self-custody wallets without a central intermediary, but their viability is questioned because of fractured liquidity, technical complexity, and regulatory exposure. The episode shows MiCA enforcement is reshaping access to stablecoins in Europe, encouraging migration to venues with lighter controls while highlighting the tension between regulatory compliance, market liquidity, and the practical limitations of self-custody trading.

## Stablecoins drive Sub-Saharan crypto growth

Sub-Saharan Africa’s cryptocurrency economy grew 13.4% to $101 billion, led by South Africa’s $39.3 billion market and driven largely by businesses using stablecoins to bypass high fees and delays in traditional finance. Intra-regional crypto payment corridors nearly doubled to 152, transaction volumes more than tripled year-on-year, and peer-to-peer stablecoin value rose sixfold to $14.6 billion. Dollar-denominated stablecoins are increasingly used for 24/7 transfers, financial inclusion, and cheaper remittances, with Nigeria and South Africa at the centre. The shift marks a move away from speculative trading toward practical commercial applications, as regulators work to integrate stablecoins with local fiat systems and formal payment infrastructure.

## Sibos debate centers on multi-rail digital money

At Sibos 2026, industry leaders debated how stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and central bank digital currencies can coexist within a multi-rail payment infrastructure. Key developments included the European Central Bank’s launch of Pontes for distributed-ledger settlement and Swift’s blockchain ledger enabling cross-border tokenized deposits, while stablecoins saw market capitalisation exceed $300 billion. Participants said interoperability and governance are critical as banks integrate these digital money forms with AI-driven agentic payments, which promise efficiency gains but introduce new operational, cyber, and compliance risks. The discussion reflected a broader consensus that future payments will likely rely on programmable rails, supervised digital money, and shared standards connecting banks, fintechs, and market infrastructure.

## Ripple RLUSD reaches Coinbase after Moody’s rating

Ripple’s RLUSD stablecoin expanded distribution by launching on Coinbase retail and institutional platforms, including the Base blockchain, after Moody’s assigned it an investment-grade A2 long-term credit rating. The rating reflects high-quality reserves of U.S. government securities and cash managed by BNY, strengthening confidence in the token’s backing. RLUSD has surpassed $2.4 billion in market capitalisation. The Coinbase integration allows users to trade and convert RLUSD at a 1:1 rate across multiple networks, marking a significant step in bringing regulated digital dollars to major crypto exchanges. The move ties credit-quality disclosure, custody, and exchange access together, supporting institutional adoption and cross-chain use.

## Overall Outlook

Overall, the October 30 digest shows stablecoins moving from niche crypto instruments toward regulated payment, treasury, and capital-markets infrastructure. Adoption is strongest in payments, emerging-market remittances, enterprise pilots, and bank-issued tokens, while regulatory frameworks in the United States, Europe, and Switzerland are shaping issuance, capital treatment, and market access. Risks remain around deposit protection, liquidity, compliance gaps, and competition between private stablecoins and central-bank money. The next phase will likely test interoperability, supervisory clarity, and whether stablecoins become embedded in mainstream financial rails.
