## Circle Expands USDC Into Emerging Markets

Circle plans to expand USDC into about 40 emerging and developing markets, including Africa, Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East, as demand for on-chain payments and digital dollars grows. Expansion is backed by a $400 million acquisition of Singapore-based Tazapay, which opens banking and payment networks in more than 100 markets, plus a $100 million Binance investment. USDC circulation rose 19% year over year to about $73.3 billion in Q2, with reports also citing $72.6 billion, and USDC held about 25% of the stablecoin market. Circle has federal and New York charters, while the GENIUS Act should create a clearer framework from January. BlackRock, Visa, and Standard Chartered use Arc infrastructure. CEO Jeremy Allaire said AI agents are increasing small machine-to-machine payments but raising cybersecurity risks.

## XRP’s Institutional Test and Stablecoin Path

Former Ripple CBDC adviser Anthony Welfare said the XRP Ledger’s fast, low-cost settlement and tokenization features are strong, but institutional adoption still requires liquidity, custody, compliance, and interoperability. He views stablecoins, including multi-network euro stablecoin projects that include the XRP Ledger, as a practical path to reduce payment friction and support tokenized finance. Recent institutional moves, such as Evernorth’s Nasdaq XRP treasury listing and Ripple’s RLUSD funding integration, highlight growing exposure. However, Welfare cautioned that lasting demand for XRP remains unproven, because these developments may increase infrastructure usage and tokenized payment relevance without necessarily translating into sustained token purchases or holding.

## Stablecoin Remittances Cut Transfer Costs

A Q3 2026 Borderless.xyz report found stablecoin-funded remittances cost about 84% less than traditional transfers, with a median $200 payment costing $2, or 1%, versus $12.72, or the World Bank's 6.36% Q3 2025 traditional average. Savings were particularly strong in Sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America. Stablecoin business transfers were also cheaper: a $10,000 eurozone payment cost as little as $26, compared with $135–$150 by bank wire. Settlements could occur in minutes through local networks, unlike next-day SWIFT transfers, though payout completion ranged from 73% in Costa Rica to 98.6% in Mexico, making reliability depend on corridor, size, provider, and destination rails. Hyundai Card's USDT and Lloyds–Visa's USDC pilots showed faster settlement. Standard Chartered expects the stablecoin market to reach $2 trillion by 2030 as transaction volumes rise.

## Veriserve Launches Stablecoin Quality Assurance

Veriserve launched an integrated quality-assurance service for stablecoin and tokenized deposit payment systems, targeting cryptocurrency exchanges and blockchain infrastructure developers. The offering combines generative AI, Veriserve’s TESTRA test-design agent, cybersecurity checks, and financial-sector testing expertise. It is intended to support development, AML and security compliance, regression testing, and ongoing monitoring of blockchain payment systems. The company said the service aims to improve reliability and testing efficiency as blockchain-based cross-border transfers and business payments become more widespread. The launch positions Veriserve to help institutions validate stablecoin settlement, custody workflows, and compliance controls before and after deployment.

## Sweden Urges Digital Krona Preparation

Sweden’s central bank is urging parliament to accelerate legal preparations for a possible digital krona, or e-krona. After 2023 work was paused, the Riksbank asked the Finance Committee to reassess a retail CBDC and recommended an immediate public inquiry. It cited payment resilience, the European Union’s digital euro program, geopolitical risks, stablecoin growth, payment-system competitiveness, and the dominance of dollar stablecoins in global markets. The Riksbank warned that widespread dollar stablecoin use could reduce the krona’s role and put pressure on Sweden’s payment system. Technical development would take about five years, so even rapid parliamentary approval would not allow a launch before 2030. The e-krona is intended to support Swedish and European payment infrastructure and preserve payment access during power or telecommunications disruptions.

## Stablecoin-Exposed Payment Stocks

Regulated stablecoins such as USDC are expanding beyond crypto niches into practical payment rails for cross-border transfers, AI-agent micropayments, and institutional settlement. Circle’s Tazapay acquisition and new Binance backing have highlighted the shift, while listed payment companies may benefit if stablecoins become faster, cheaper digital payment flows. Three stocks with exposure include Paysafe, Wise Group, and Payoneer Global. Each offers regulated digital payment, wallet, or cross-border payment infrastructure that can intersect with stablecoin usage and faster settlement. The equity angle suggests that stablecoin adoption may affect not only issuers and blockchain networks, but also regulated payment processors, merchant acquirers, and businesses needing efficient international payment rails.

## Cross River Bank Integrates RLUSD

Cross River Bank said it will integrate Ripple’s dollar-backed stablecoin RLUSD into its core banking system, allowing fintech clients to switch between U.S. dollars and RLUSD around the clock using the XRP Ledger. The announcement centers on stablecoin payments and settlement, with possible efficiency gains for card programs and intraday settlement rather than direct XRP demand. XRP’s limited price reaction reflects that the partnership does not require clients to buy or hold XRP to access RLUSD functionality. It also highlights that RLUSD remains a smaller stablecoin competing with larger established dollar-backed tokens. The move could still matter for XRP Ledger usage because the integration demonstrates institutional core-banking connectivity for a Ripple-affiliated stablecoin.

## Japan Banks Complete Stablecoin Proof of Concept

Japanese banks completed a stablecoin proof of concept supported by Japan’s Financial Services Agency. The evaluation covered joint issuance, cross-border payments, anti-money-laundering controls, and regulatory compliance under the Payment Services Act. The project is significant because it tests how large financial institutions can operate stablecoins within a supervised banking environment rather than only through standalone cryptocurrency platforms. It addresses operational questions such as issuance, settlement, compliance, and cross-border transfer workflows. If the results are positive, Japan’s experience could shape national rules for digital yen or stablecoin settlement, especially as regulators seek resilient payment infrastructure and controlled use of digital currencies for international payments.

## Russia Plans P2P Crypto Transfer Ban

Russia plans to ban peer-to-peer crypto-asset transfers effective July 1, 2027. Under the proposal, such transfers would need to route through approved intermediaries rather than directly between private wallets or accounts. The measure preserves an exemption for foreign-trade settlement, allowing certain international commercial transactions to continue using existing channels. The rule reflects a broader regulatory preference for supervised payment flows, aiming to improve oversight, reduce sanctions-related risks, and align crypto-asset transfers with formal financial systems. If implemented, it would change how Russian individuals and businesses move crypto assets, while still permitting trade-related settlements that regulators consider legitimate.

## Stablecoin Prefunding Reduces Working Capital

Stablecoins can lower the working-capital cost of cross-border payments by letting providers replenish prefunded accounts around the clock and closer to when payouts are made. Visa moved stablecoin prefunding from pilot to commercial production through Inyo Global, allowing eligible businesses to prefund Visa Direct cross-border payouts with USDC while recipients continue receiving fiat currency. The potential benefit depends on whether providers can actually reduce the size and duration of committed prefunding balances. Stablecoins still require liquidity management and add costs related to custody, compliance, redemption, and operations. If prefunding efficiency improves, the model could make faster international payments cheaper for businesses and payment service providers.

## France Adopts Stablecoin Conversion Tax

France’s National Assembly adopted an amendment that would tax capital gains when digital assets are converted into regulated MiCA stablecoins, even if holders do not convert into euros. The proposal would apply a 31.4% flat tax to such conversions, covering USDC and EURC but reportedly not USDT. The measure targets taxable events in stablecoin ecosystems and signals that French tax authorities want to prevent users from avoiding capital-gains recognition by moving assets into stablecoin forms. Final adoption still depends on further 2027 budget votes. If passed, the rule could affect investment strategies involving MiCA-regulated stablecoins and add a compliance consideration for French investors trading or converting digital assets.

## Tether Backs Saber Money Expansion

Saber Money, an international stablecoin payment provider, accepted undisclosed investment from Tether and CMCC Global’s Titan Fund on October 14. The funding will support expansion across Asia-Pacific and the Gulf, including local licensing, banking partnerships, and business payment services such as supplier billing and cross-border transfers. Saber reported that its payment volumes grew more than sixfold in nine months, with billions of dollars processed in more than 40 countries and over 90% of payments processed in under one minute. The investment strengthens Saber’s position in high-growth payment corridors and gives it resources to build regulated local access. It also indicates that Tether is extending its network beyond issuing USDT into payment infrastructure and cross-border business flows.

## Coinbase Powers Marqeta Stablecoin Cards

Marqeta is expanding its partnership with Coinbase to let businesses issue payment cards funded by customers’ stablecoin holdings. Under the arrangement, Coinbase will hold the stablecoins and handle currency conversions, while Marqeta issues the cards and processes payments. The service will use Coinbase’s Base blockchain for on-chain settlement, enabling cardholders to spend stablecoins at merchants that accept cards and allowing businesses to fund card programs around the clock. The companies did not disclose fees, a rollout date, or which stablecoins will initially be supported. The move combines card networks, stablecoin custody, and blockchain settlement, potentially making stablecoin-backed spending more accessible for businesses and customers while reducing reliance on traditional fiat-only card funding.

## Higher Deposit Rates Can Slow Stablecoins

A new Bank for International Settlements working paper using weekly U.S. data from January 2019 to June 2025 finds that stablecoins and bank deposits compete for the same funds. Stablecoin market capitalization grows more slowly when banks raise deposit interest rates. The substitution effect is stronger above roughly 3%, when competitive pressure causes banks to pass through Federal Reserve rate changes more aggressively. That makes mostly non-interest-bearing stablecoins less attractive and draws funds back into bank deposits. The finding suggests that bank deposit rates are an important competitive variable in stablecoin adoption. If banks offer higher yields, stablecoin issuers may need to provide incentives, reduce costs, or improve utility to retain funds.

## Mastercard and BVNK Launch Payment Platform

Mastercard and BVNK launched the Global Money Platform, a modular single API that lets banks, fintechs, payment service providers, and businesses hold fiat currencies and stablecoins, convert between them, and spend or pay out from either. The platform is the first major integration since Mastercard acquired BVNK. It supports multi-currency accounts, stablecoin-linked cards, and local bank payouts through Mastercard Move. Early customers include Nuvei and Marqeta. By combining traditional payment rails with stablecoin infrastructure, the platform allows companies to embed digital-asset functionality without managing separate account, conversion, payout, and card providers. Mastercard said the service is available now through BVNK and plans to add agentic commerce and regulated digital money, including tokenized bank deposits.

## Tokenization Liquidity and Stablecoin Mainstreaming

Tokenized real-world assets are expanding access to pre-IPO stocks, commodities, and equities, but panelists said on-chain supply is outpacing investor demand and liquidity remains fragmented across issuers and blockchains. Executives said stablecoins are more likely to become mainstream if they function as seamless everyday payment tools, while addressing user experience and cross-jurisdiction regulatory hurdles. Stablecoin market capitalization was about $306 billion, up roughly 2% year over year after growing around 74% in the preceding 12 months. The discussion highlights a gap between asset tokenization and usable liquidity: more issuers and networks are creating on-chain assets, but secondary trading, custody, and settlement still need broader integration. Stablecoins remain key to converting tokenized assets into practical payment and settlement flows.

## A7 International Launch Amid Sanctions

A Coinbase-funded think tank said emerging markets hold about two-thirds of global stablecoin supply and urged them to license local-currency tokens before dollar-pegged coins expand. The FSB warned that cross-border stablecoin oversight remains immature. The United Kingdom imposed new sanctions on Russia’s A7 Network, targeting exchanges, payment platforms, and an individual. At the same time, A7 launched A7 International, a service that converts USDT on TRON into SWIFT bank transfers for a 0.3% fee and requires KYC checks. The launch comes amid U.S. and UK efforts to limit A7’s use in sanctions evasion, while global attention rises around stablecoins, tokenized payments, and CBDC-based payment rails. The tension shows how stablecoin networks are simultaneously commercializing and facing sanctions, compliance, and cross-border regulatory scrutiny.

## Overall Outlook

Stablecoins are moving from experimental instruments into regulated payment, settlement, and capital-management infrastructure, with expansion into emerging markets, lower remittance costs, bank integrations, and new commercial platforms. The next phase will depend on liquidity, custody, compliance, user experience, and whether tokenized assets generate real demand. Competition with bank deposits, CBDC development, and cross-border regulatory coordination will shape which networks, issuers, and payment providers scale fastest. The overall trend points toward more institutionalized stablecoin usage, but also greater regulatory and operational risk.
