## FSB Flags Stablecoin Stress, Cross-Border Gaps, and AI Risks

The Financial Stability Board warned that global stablecoin oversight remains untested in severe stress, with only 11 of 28 jurisdictions having finalized frameworks in the $300 billion market. It said borderless 24/7 liabilities conflict with jurisdictionally anchored reserves and redemption rules, especially in multi-issuer structures where affiliated entities share one token but hold reserves in different countries. Fast redemptions can outpace collateral movement, obscuring holder locations and forcing reserve sales or bank-deposit withdrawals that spill into markets. It called for cross-border monitoring, data sharing, standardized metrics, reserve portability, insolvency planning, and implementation of its 2023 recommendations, noting faster, cheaper payment potential. Chair Andrew Bailey also flagged AI-driven cyber, financial-crime, and third-party risks; responsible AI governance remains central through 2027. The nonbinding report, published October 12, precedes a G20 meeting.

## Stablecoin Payments Still Depend on Bank Liquidity

Stablecoins can settle cross-border business payments quickly, but many recipients immediately convert them into bank deposits to pay employees, suppliers, and other obligations. That creates a last-mile liquidity problem: on-chain transfers still depend on traditional banks and financial markets for redemption, liquidity, and settlement. The Financial Stability Board report says 24/7 stablecoin transfers can generate systemic-risk concerns if conventional banking systems cannot keep pace with continuous digital redemptions. The next competitive advantage may belong to companies and banks that make received funds immediately usable for payroll, trade, and operating payments, rather than merely moving value into custody. The issue is especially relevant where stablecoins are being used as payment rails but corporate cash management remains anchored to fiat accounts.

## Singapore Expands CBDC and Stablecoin Safeguards

Singapore’s central bank said wholesale CBDCs will be essential to anchor monetary stability as tokenized assets and private digital money scale. The Monetary Authority of Singapore is advancing a Singapore dollar CBDC test network and preparing to settle tokenized MAS bills using wholesale CBDC, aiming for greater settlement certainty. It also said deposit tokens, regulated stablecoins, and CBDCs can serve different financial use cases rather than replacing one another. MAS outlined stablecoin safeguards including reserve backing, redemption at par, and disclosure, and suggested stronger requirements for systemically important stablecoins. It is consulting on a single-currency stablecoin framework. The approach frames wholesale CBDC as infrastructure for tokenized finance while keeping private stablecoins subject to defined reserve and redemption rules.

## Bank of Korea Uses Institutional CBDC for Bond Pilot

The Bank of Korea said it will use only institutional central bank digital currency to settle its planned government bond tokenization pilot. The instrument would be issued to financial institutions rather than the public, and private stablecoins or similar instruments would be excluded at this stage. The central bank said direct central bank issuance can provide immediate settlement finality and reduce counterparty settlement risk, making it suitable for sovereign-bond experimentation. Details remain open while the Bank consults the Economy and Finance Ministry, the Financial Services Commission, and the Korea Securities Depository on the pilot’s scope, issuance mechanics, and interoperability standards. The decision signals a preference for controlled institutional rails before allowing broader tokenized government securities or retail-facing digital money.

## Bank of England Takes No Rush for Digital Pound

Bank of England Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden said authorities will not rush to launch a digital pound. She said stablecoins and tokenized deposits can already perform similar payment functions, so the need for a public-facing CBDC depends on progress in tokenization and payment innovation. The Bank is monitoring payment-system developments and will take a wait-and-see approach rather than moving quickly toward issuance. The comment places the digital pound inside a broader competitive field of private stablecoins, bank-issued deposit tokens, and wholesale settlement solutions. It suggests the UK will calibrate digital currency only if innovation and monetary-sovereignty needs make a public option necessary, while avoiding premature duplication of capabilities that banks and licensed issuers may provide.

## EU Focuses on Stablecoin Compliance, Digital Euro, and Quantum Risk

European authorities are tightening oversight of digital assets and digital-euro risks. ESMA gave MiCA-authorized crypto firms three months to stop serving EU clients with non-compliant stablecoins, allowing only closely supervised liquidation, conversion, withdrawal, transfer, or safekeeping. ECB board member Piero Cipollone warned that delaying a digital euro could weaken Europe’s monetary sovereignty, with a pilot targeted for mid-2027 and possible issuance in 2029. Europol urged crypto companies to begin preparing for quantum-computing threats by adopting post-quantum cryptography and improving wallet security. Together, the moves show regulators focusing on both near-term market compliance and longer-term strategic risks to payment infrastructure, monetary policy independence, and cryptographic resilience.

## Asia Needs Everyday Stablecoin Experiences

Asia is theoretically well positioned for stablecoin adoption because of large remittance flows and cross-border commerce, but mainstream use remains limited because much discussion focuses on institutional infrastructure, regulation, and settlement. The conversation often neglects the everyday user, for whom fees, slow settlement, and complex onboarding remain the main barriers. Adoption will likely require user-friendly experiences that remove crypto friction, simplify payments and salary flows, and address practical financial frustrations. Institutions need to turn stablecoin foundations into practical tools for consumers, merchants, and freelancers, with clear payment options, fast conversion, and familiar interfaces. Until stablecoins solve ordinary cash-management problems, they may remain relevant mainly to institutions, traders, and cross-border payment corridors rather than becoming a mass retail payment medium.

## Global Trade Adds Stablecoins, CBDCs, and Tokenized Deposits

Global trade payment rails are becoming more fragmented as stablecoins, national CBDCs, and tokenized deposits expand. Stablecoins such as USDT and USDC are increasingly used to settle oil, vehicles, electronics, ship insurance, and other cross-border transactions, providing faster alternatives to dollar correspondent banking. National CBDC and yuan rails, including mBridge, CBETS, and the digital ruble, are building parallel payment channels for trade, while several governments test local-currency stablecoins. Tokenized deposits on bank blockchain platforms, including Citi, JPMorgan’s Kinexys, and Swift’s 24/7 registry, are introducing faster settlement for trade finance. Together these rails reduce reliance on a single dollar corridor but create new points of regulatory and bank control.

## Standard Chartered Adds RLUSD Custody and Collateral Access

Standard Chartered’s Dubai International Financial Centre branch now offers institutional custody for Ripple’s dollar-pegged RLUSD stablecoin on the XRP Ledger. The bank also joined a planned LMAX Group integration that would use collateral mirroring to let clients use the value of RLUSD as trading collateral while the tokens remain in the bank’s custody. RLUSD has about $2.45 billion in market capitalization, a small share of the roughly $306 billion stablecoin market. The LMAX trading link is still pending final documentation, testing, and regulatory approvals. The development gives corporates a regulated custodial option for a major dollar-pegged token and supports a model where stablecoin value can be deployed for trading without transferring ownership of the underlying asset.

## Sui’s USDsui Stablecoin Reaches Record Supply

Sui’s native stablecoin USDsui, issued by Stripe’s Bridge, reached a record $80 million supply and grew 20.5% over the last 90 days. The growth came as Sui’s total value locked rose more than 20% to $530 million. The report links the stablecoin momentum to Sui’s expanding use cases, including tokenized-asset standards, AI-infrastructure investments, and SUI buybacks supported by stablecoin-yield proceeds. These developments are presented as indicators that stablecoin activity may be broadening the network’s utility beyond speculative token trading. A growing native stablecoin supply can help support on-chain payments, yield products, and institutional finance, though sustained adoption will depend on continued integration, liquidity, and real-world demand.

Stablecoin Development shares closed at $1.54, down 22.61% from the previous close, after opening at $1.99 and reaching a high of $2.00 on Friday. The stock had risen sharply earlier in the week, including a 104.37% gain on a Friday close at $7.48, before falling more than 47% the following Monday. The volatility underscores how listed stablecoin-related equities can move quickly on trading flows, sentiment, and market conditions rather than a single confirmed corporate event. It also highlights the gap between a company’s exposure to the stablecoin theme and the operational or regulatory milestones that would justify sustained valuation support.

## Tempo Sees Accounting Change Helping Corporate Stablecoins

Stripe-backed blockchain Tempo says a proposed U.S. accounting change could make stablecoins more attractive to corporations by allowing qualifying tokens to be treated as cash equivalents. That would make them more practical for corporate treasury use without harming liquidity ratios or loan covenants. The proposal would require direct redemption rights with stablecoin issuers, so tokens purchased through exchanges might not qualify unless the company’s contract with the issuer supports redemption. The FASB proposal is open for comment through Nov. 19, even as the GENIUS Act begins establishing the first federal regulatory framework for U.S. payment stablecoins. If adopted, the accounting shift could narrow the gap between on-chain stablecoin balances and traditional cash management for large enterprises.

## CoinShares Brings Stablecoin Yield to Safe Wallets

CoinShares has integrated with Safe Labs and Railnet to offer stablecoin yield strategies through Safe’s Earn widget. The arrangement allows users to access professionally designed yield strategies while retaining self-custody of their assets. CoinShares designs and oversees the strategies, Railnet provides vault infrastructure, and Safe supplies the smart-account interface. Users can deposit and withdraw funds at any time, giving the product more flexibility than fixed-tenure funds, though the strategy remains subject to market and credit risk. The offering is subject to jurisdiction and eligibility limits, excludes U.S. persons, and warns that yields are not guaranteed and digital assets can lose value. It is a step toward mainstream self-custody access to institutional-style stablecoin income.

## Tereina and Circle Extend Stablecoins to SAP

Tereina and Circle are integrating stablecoin functionality into SAP Cloud ERP, enabling enterprise customers to use stablecoins within SAP’s enterprise resource planning platform. The move extends stablecoin access into core business management systems, where procurement, payment, reconciliation, and cash-management processes are handled. By embedding stablecoin transactions in SAP workflows, companies may be able to settle with stablecoins while preserving the controls, reporting, and audit trails required for large organizations. The partnership is important because it positions stablecoins not merely as a blockchain asset, but as an operational payment option inside enterprise software. It could help reduce friction for firms already managing global payments and treasury operations in cloud ERP environments.

## Uniswap Leads Stablecoin DEX Volume; Aerodrome Hits Milestone

Uniswap accounted for 47% of decentralized-exchange stablecoin trading volume, making it the leading venue for stablecoin swaps. The result underlines the continued importance of permissionless decentralized liquidity for stablecoin distribution, arbitrage, and corporate conversion. At the same time, tokenized stock products on the Aerodrome protocol reached a $2 billion valuation as trading in tokenized equities gained traction. The two metrics point to a broader market pattern: stablecoins are becoming a default liquidity layer on decentralized venues, while tokenized securities expand into on-chain products that can be settled or financed with digital dollars. Together, they suggest a growing bridge between traditional asset classes and decentralized trading infrastructure.

## Gibraltar Explores Multi-Currency Stablecoin Consortium

The Government of Gibraltar and ADI Foundation signed a memorandum of understanding to explore a multi-currency stablecoin consortium on ADI Chain. The proposed consortium would issue fully reserved sterling, U.S. dollar, and euro-referenced digital currencies, with each currency issued through a separate entity holding one-for-one high-quality liquid reserves. Those reserves would be independently attested and audited annually. The initiative would involve Gibraltar-licensed banks and institutions and aims to build regulated digital settlement infrastructure for institutional payments, cross-border payments, and international trade. Its longer-term ambition is to connect Europe, the Middle East, and Africa. The project highlights a regulated, multi-issuer model that seeks to address sovereignty and reserve concerns while creating a neutral settlement layer.

## Outlook

Digital-currency development is moving beyond policy debates into operational infrastructure: CBDC pilots, enterprise ERP integrations, custody, trade rails, and accounting treatment. At the same time, regulators are emphasizing that cross-border crisis management, reserve portability, and consumer usability are still weak points. The coming months will likely separate projects that create practical payment, liquidity, or settlement value from those that remain conceptually driven. Expect continued experimentation, but with a sharper focus on compliance, real usage, and whether stablecoins can integrate smoothly into everyday and institutional finance.
