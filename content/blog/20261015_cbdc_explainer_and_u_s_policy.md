## CBDC Explainer and U.S. Policy

A central bank digital currency is a direct liability of the central bank, recorded on its books and typically distributed through a two-tier system in which commercial banks or licensed providers operate customer-facing wallets while the central bank maintains the core ledger. Payments settle directly on that ledger, delivering a stablecoin-like transfer experience but backed by government rather than a private issuer. The United States has prohibited federal agencies from establishing a CBDC and has instead favored privately issued stablecoins. As a result, the U.S. market is largely observing and learning from other countries' CBDC designs rather than developing its own national digital currency.

## Stablecoin Volumes and Market Framing

Stablecoin transfer volumes now exceed those of Visa and Mastercard, though the comparison includes broader money movement rather than strictly card payments. The stablecoin market is valued at more than $300 billion, led by Tether and Circle's USDC. In the prevailing framework, Bitcoin serves as a scarce, gold-like store of value while stablecoins act as silver-like payment media that address crypto volatility through fiat-currency pegs. Tether's USDT is backed largely by assets such as U.S. Treasuries. The analysis argues that widespread adoption of USD stablecoins could dollarize global finance, reduce reliance on the SWIFT system, and prompt the United States and other governments to either regulate stablecoins more comprehensively or develop competing digital currency systems.

## Standard Chartered Delays $2 Trillion Stablecoin Target to 2030

Standard Chartered has delayed its forecast for the stablecoin market to reach $2 trillion to 2030 after total stablecoin supply stalled around $309 billion this year. The bank argues that usage can still grow even without new issuance if existing tokens circulate faster. It identifies four distinct stablecoin markets: emerging-market savings, developed-market payments, crypto trading, and AI-agent payments. Tether, Circle, and Ethena are positioned as leaders across these use cases, with market share determined by fit for specific applications rather than sheer token supply. The bank also notes that upcoming U.S. licensing rules, deposit insurance decisions, and accounting changes will shape corporate adoption, particularly in developed-market payments where competition among issuers is expected to intensify.

## Visa Survey and Stablecoin Settlement Growth

Visa's Money Travels 2026 survey found that 36% of U.S. respondents would use stablecoins in a baseline scenario, rising to 56% when hypothetical bank-level fraud protection and deposit insurance were included. At the same time, 56% of respondents said they had never heard of stablecoins, indicating a significant awareness gap. Behind the scenes, stablecoin infrastructure is expanding rapidly: Visa reported a $20 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate in September and support for more than 160 stablecoin-linked card programs. The company's stablecoin settlement volume increased more than 15-fold over the past year, suggesting that payment networks are increasingly adopting stablecoins for back-end settlement even when consumers do not directly interact with the technology.

## JPMorgan and Citi Develop Tokenized Deposit Services

JPMorgan and Citi are developing blockchain-based tokenized deposit services that allow institutional clients to move bank money around the clock, offering some of the speed and availability traditionally associated with stablecoins without losing deposit relationships. Tokenized deposits represent money held at regulated banks, while stablecoins are issuer-backed claims that can move across open blockchain networks. Industry figures expect the two models to coexist, with banks focused on institutional settlement and stablecoins powering exchanges, wallets, payments, and broader decentralized finance ecosystems. The development signals that major financial institutions see blockchain infrastructure as complementary rather than competitive to stablecoin networks, preserving the regulatory advantages of bank deposits while capturing the efficiency benefits of distributed ledgers.

## Qivalis Secures Dutch EMI License

Euro-pegged stablecoin consortium Qivalis, founded in 2025 and backed by 37 European banks, has received authorization from De Nederlandsche Bank to operate as an electronic money institution, clearing the main regulatory hurdle to issuing a euro-denominated electronic money token under MiCA within months. The Amsterdam-based firm plans a full-reserve model and will hold reserves across participating banks rather than solely with shareholders. It will distribute its token through a B2B2X model using Fireblocks infrastructure, reaching commercial banks, exchanges, payment service providers, and market infrastructure operators for institutional payment and settlement use. Qivalis must still submit its white paper for DNB review before launch. As the first DNB-licensed euro stablecoin consortium, the project underscores Europe's effort to build a euro-denominated on-chain settlement asset governed by European regulatory standards.

## EBA Classifies USDC as Significant Under MiCA

The European Banking Authority has classified Circle's USDC as a significant e-money token under the MiCA framework, making it subject to joint oversight by the EBA and France's financial regulator. USDC is the second-largest stablecoin by market capitalization at approximately $73 billion, underscoring continued demand for dollar-pegged tokens in Europe. The classification brings enhanced supervisory attention and regulatory requirements to the token's operations within the EU. The move comes as the European Central Bank continues to evaluate its digital euro, originally proposed in 2020, which could potentially be issued by 2029. The parallel development of a regulated euro stablecoin and a potential digital euro highlights the complex interplay between private-sector innovation and public monetary policy in the European digital payments landscape.

## Wells Fargo Joins 20-Bank Stablecoin Consortium

Wells Fargo has joined approximately 20 global banks and financial firms in a proposed bank-led stablecoin company expected to form in late 2026 and launch in the first half of 2027. The consortium plans to issue a U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoin compliant with rules such as the GENIUS Act and MiCA, initially targeting wholesale, institutional, and retail payments, with additional G7 currencies including the euro in later phases. Wells Fargo also launched tokenized deposits for corporate and commercial clients in September, using Cosmos ledger technology to enable real-time, programmable transfers. A shared U.S. deposit-token network with JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, and Citigroup also targets availability in 2027. Separately, the bank reported third-quarter net income of $5.95 billion, up from $5.59 billion a year earlier, while expanding its digital payment infrastructure.

## Stablecoin Remittances from Canada to Asia Surge

Chainalysis data show that remittance-sized stablecoin and Bitcoin transfers from Canada to India, China, and the Philippines rose 141% year over year to $2.36 billion in the 12 months ending June 2026. These three Asian corridors now account for 19.4% of Canada's outbound cross-border stablecoin value. Stablecoins represented 44% of Canadian dollar-based order-book volume in June, reflecting deeper domestic liquidity supporting cross-border payments. The report attributes the growth to high traditional remittance costs and a large foreign-born population in Canada, but notes that off-ramp friction and local regulatory conditions in destination countries will determine whether the trend continues. The surge highlights stablecoins as a practical alternative to legacy correspondent-bank channels for cross-border remittances.

## Cleveland Fed Survey: Business Appetite for Stablecoins Remains Low

A Cleveland Federal Reserve survey found little business enthusiasm for stablecoin payments in the United States, with only one of 148 respondents currently using them and just seven planning to do so. Companies cited low familiarity, lack of customer demand, and uncertainty about cost or efficiency compared with existing payment methods as primary barriers. The report concluded that cross-border payments may represent stablecoins' clearest near-term use case in the U.S. market. Broader domestic adoption would require sustained education, financial incentives, and demonstrated proof of operational benefit to convince businesses to shift from established payment rails. The findings contrast with rapid growth in institutional and settlement-level stablecoin usage, suggesting a disconnect between back-office adoption and front-of-business integration.

## France Proposes 31.4% Tax on Stablecoin Conversions

French lawmakers are debating 2027 budget amendments that would extend the country's 31.4% flat capital gains tax to conversions from cryptocurrencies into EU MiCA-regulated stablecoins, effective January 1, 2027. The change would end the current exemption for many crypto-to-crypto swaps. Chainalysis warned that taxing regulated stablecoin transactions could push traders toward offshore venues or less transparent decentralized channels outside the EU framework. The proposal also includes requirements for French residents to declare self-hosted wallets worth at least 100,000 euros, an exit-tax extension for crypto assets, and the ability to carry forward crypto losses for up to 10 years. None of the proposals has become law, and the full National Assembly still must vote on the budget's revenue section.

## Bank of England: No Rush on Digital Pound

Bank of England Deputy Governor Sarah Breeden said there is no rush to issue a digital pound, with any CBDC decision likely driven by benefits not already available through stablecoins or tokenization. The bank has long reviewed a digital pound but remains unconvinced it is necessary, as private-sector digital assets can deliver similar payment benefits and potentially interoperate smoothly with central bank money. The BoE is upgrading real-time gross settlement infrastructure to accommodate tokenized assets and has urged the development of sterling stablecoins to challenge dollar dominance in international payments. Industry executives cautioned that commercial deposits, tokenized deposits, stablecoins, and CBDCs must integrate across networks without creating new settlement barriers, underscoring the need for interoperability in the evolving payments landscape.

## Tokenized U.S. Treasuries and RWA Market Growth

Holders in Italy, Portugal, and Singapore own larger shares of geolocated tokenized U.S. Treasury value than U.S. holders, with the United States ranking fourth at 10.5%. Tokenized U.S. Treasuries grew 125% in the 12 months ending July 2026 to $15.42 billion, giving foreign retail investors a direct route into a wholesale market and adding incremental demand for U.S. government debt. Dollar stablecoins reinforce this channel by holding 99.1% of stablecoin order-book volume and being backed largely by short-dated U.S. government debt, while local currency stablecoins remain comparatively small. The broader tokenized real-world asset market reached $36.07 billion, with tokenized U.S. Treasury debt the largest category. The data suggest that on-chain U.S. debt is attracting a geographically diverse investor base that could reshape demand dynamics for government securities.

## Lynq and Better Money Complete RLUSD Settlement Proof of Concept

Lynq and The Better Money Company completed a live proof of concept in which institutional assets on Lynq were converted into Ripple's RLUSD and settled in minutes without relying on short-term credit. The firms said interest continued to accrue during the settlement period using Tassat's Yield in Transit functionality, reducing the yield drag that normally occurs while capital clears. The transaction was a small, pre-funded test between known institutional counterparties and represents phase one of a broader collaboration. Native stablecoin support on Lynq is targeted for late 2026. The proof of concept demonstrates that institutional settlement can be achieved in minutes with continuous yield, potentially addressing one of the key pain points in traditional correspondent banking and opening the door to faster, more capital-efficient interbank transactions.

## KuCoin Launches KCUSD Yield-Bearing Asset

KuCoin launched KCUSD, a yield-bearing U.S. dollar asset created by subscribing 1:1 with USDT, USDC, or USDG. The product offers up to 4% standard annual percentage rate, with promotional rates reaching 6%, no fixed lockup, and daily compounding. KCUSD is a custodial KuCoin certificate that can be redeemed for the underlying stablecoin and, for eligible VIP accounts, used as trading collateral within its Unified Trading Account while still earning yield. The product is best suited to users who already keep stablecoins on KuCoin, but it carries exchange counterparty risk and has limited external transferability. The launch highlights growing competition among exchanges to offer yield-enhancing digital dollar products that can serve both savings and trading purposes without requiring users to move funds off-platform.

## North America Agentic Commerce Expansion

North America's $3 trillion crypto economy remains the most influential globally, driven by U.S. dollar stablecoins, tokenized real-world assets, and emerging AI-agent payments. Stablecoin use has expanded for cross-border and remittance flows, while the tokenized RWA market reached $36.07 billion. The region also saw rapid growth in agentic commerce, with U.S.-built x402 protocol and stablecoin rails enabling AI agents to make frequent, low-value on-chain payments autonomously. This agentic payment layer represents a new category of transactional volume that did not exist in traditional payment systems. The combination of stablecoin rails, tokenized assets, and AI-driven payment automation positions North America as the primary testbed for next-generation digital currency applications that could reshape how businesses transact at micro-scale.

## Reserve Bank of New Zealand Pauses Retail CBDC Work

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand has paused its retail CBDC work while focusing on modernizing broader payments infrastructure. The bank's approach allows future digital money to include CBDC alongside bank deposits, potentially pointing toward tokenized commercial bank deposits settled in a wholesale CBDC environment. This pivot suggests RBNZ is not committed to a specific retail digital currency design but is keeping the option open while prioritizing interoperability and system modernization. The decision mirrors a broader trend among central banks to evaluate CBDCs in the context of private-sector innovation rather than as standalone national projects. New Zealand's approach could serve as a model for other economies seeking to balance monetary sovereignty with the flexibility of private digital asset ecosystems.

## BIS Paper Links Bank Deposit Rates to Stablecoin Growth

A Bank for International Settlements paper found that higher U.S. bank deposit rates slow stablecoin market-cap growth, with a stronger effect for USDC than for USDT. The finding suggests limited but detectable substitution between stablecoins and bank deposits, as investors shift between the two when relative yield and convenience change. The differential impact on USDC versus USDT may reflect differences in their user bases, reserve compositions, or the degree to which each is integrated with traditional banking channels. The research adds empirical evidence to the ongoing debate about whether stablecoins complement or compete with bank deposits. As deposit rates fluctuate, the stablecoin market's ability to attract and retain users may prove more sensitive to interest-rate environments than previously assumed.

## Middle East Stablecoin Association Partners with Luna PR

The Middle East Stablecoin Association, incorporated in the Dubai International Financial Centre, has appointed Luna PR as its strategic communications partner. The partnership will focus on raising regional and international awareness of stablecoin developments, explaining opportunities, risks, regulatory considerations, and infrastructure needs to policymakers, financial institutions, technology companies, and the general public. MESA is working to promote responsible and interoperable growth of digital money centered on stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and related digital assets. The appointment signals growing institutional engagement with stablecoin advocacy in the Middle East, a region that has been a hub for digital-asset regulation and innovation. The collaboration aims to bridge the gap between technical developments and policy understanding across the region's diverse stakeholder ecosystem.

## Rain Launches Embedded Stablecoin Wallets

Rain has launched embedded, non-custodial stablecoin wallets that allow partner applications to give users dedicated accounts for receiving, holding, spending, and transferring stablecoins such as USDC and USDT. The wallets support 12 blockchain networks and connect to Rain-issued cards usable at 175 million merchant locations worldwide, as well as virtual accounts, onramps, offramps, rewards, and payout services. Rain said custodial wallet functionality and stablecoin earning features are expected by the end of 2026. The launch enables businesses to integrate stablecoin payments natively into their own platforms without relying on third-party crypto wallets. The move underscores the growing trend of embedding digital-asset functionality directly into mainstream financial and commercial applications, making stablecoins more accessible to non-crypto-native users.

## Global Watchdogs Flag Digital Asset Risks

The Financial Stability Board has urged G20 regulators to coordinate oversight of global stablecoins and AI-related financial risks, warning that cross-border supervision and crisis-management mechanisms remain immature. In Australia, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission proposed extending the country's CFD retail protections for 10 years and expanding them to qualifying derivatives settled in digital assets. Separately, South Korea's Jeonbuk Bank reportedly began using Bitcoin as collateral for won-denominated corporate loans, demonstrating that banks are increasingly integrating digital assets into traditional lending frameworks. These developments collectively point to a global regulatory effort to extend existing financial oversight into the growing digital-asset sector while addressing the unique risks posed by cross-border, technology-driven payment and investment systems.

## Overall Outlook

The digital-currency landscape is entering a phase of institutional maturation. Supply growth in stablecoins has plateaued, but usage, settlement infrastructure, and regulatory frameworks are advancing rapidly. Central banks are taking a measured approach, with the U.S., U.K., and New Zealand all de-emphasizing near-term CBDC issuance in favor of allowing private-sector innovation to fill payment gaps. The convergence of tokenized deposits, stablecoins, and AI-driven agentic payments points to a multi-layered financial system where different instruments serve distinct roles. Key catalysts over the coming year will include the 20-bank stablecoin launch, France's tax decision, MiCA enforcement actions, and the continued expansion of cross-border stablecoin remittance corridors.
