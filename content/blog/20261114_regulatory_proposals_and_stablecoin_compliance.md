## Regulatory Proposals and Stablecoin Compliance

Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission proposed draft rules for stablecoin transactions, including a daily cap of 5 million Thai baht, about $147,750, per customer at each licensed digital asset operator. The measures target money laundering and cybercrime by requiring stricter wallet ownership verification, Travel Rule compliance, blockchain analytics screening, and enhanced monitoring of high-risk wallets. The proposal also imposes transparency duties on exchanges and brokers, such as disclosing market makers and setting minimum thresholds for off-platform trades. Public comments are open, with exemptions for internal transfers between a customer's own accounts, market-maker activity, and transactions approved by the Bank of Thailand.

The International Monetary Fund urged Kenya to relax new cryptocurrency regulations by allowing foreign-denominated stablecoin issuers, including Tether and Circle, to hold reserves abroad under strict supervision rather than exclusively locally. IMF officials warned that local reserve mandates could discourage global issuers from establishing a presence in Kenya, pushing users toward unregulated offshore platforms. The Fund also recommended reviewing static minimum capital requirements, cautioning that high thresholds may deter smaller crypto businesses from entering the market. The guidance aims to balance consumer protection, financial stability, and competition while keeping Kenya's crypto ecosystem supervised and accessible.

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority launched the DART framework to regulate tokenized deposits and stablecoins, positioning the city as a key hub for regulated tokenization across Asia-Pacific. The initiative gives financial institutions clearer guidance for issuing, holding, and transferring digital liabilities while aligning with international supervisory expectations. The framework arrives as global stablecoin supply faces contraction and enterprises seek compliant rails for cross-border settlement. By supporting tokenized deposits under a defined regime, Hong Kong aims to attract banks, fintechs, and institutional participants that need legal certainty for treasury operations, payments, and programmable money use cases.

The European Union advanced its digital finance agenda by establishing the European Tokenisation Standards Group to create common standards for tokenized securities, with industry nominations open until November 27, 2026. Concurrently, the European Central Bank selected 36 payment providers for a 12-month digital euro pilot beginning in late 2027, targeting a potential launch in 2029 pending legislative approval. To address illicit financial flows, the EU launched a 12-month Crypto Sanctions Task Force dedicated to preventing Russia from using digital assets to evade sanctions. Together, the measures signal Europe's effort to harmonize tokenized markets, test retail digital currency infrastructure, and strengthen enforcement.

## Central Bank Digital Currency Initiatives

The Reserve Bank of India authorized banks to integrate digital rupee wallets directly into existing mobile banking applications, replacing the previous requirement for separate standalone apps. The strategic shift aims to simplify user access and boost adoption of the central bank digital currency, which has seen muted transaction volumes since launch. While experts acknowledge that integration improves discovery and convenience, they caution that widespread consumer uptake depends on the e-rupee offering greater value than the dominant UPI payment system. The move reflects regulators' effort to make CBDC rails visible within everyday banking interfaces.

Chandigarh's transition from Direct Benefit Transfer to central bank digital currency tokens for ration subsidies created significant redemption hurdles, with only about 30,000 of 53,000 beneficiaries successfully using September subsidies. The shift to digital wallets through the PNB Digital Rupee app introduced technical barriers, including one-time-passcode failures, connectivity issues, and synchronization delays across interconnected platforms such as NIC and PFMS. These problems caused a sharp decline in subsidy utilization and reduced the active beneficiary base compared with the previous decade-long DBT system. The episode illustrates the operational risks of migrating public payment programs onto new digital rails.

Central banks are increasingly exploring issuance of central bank digital currencies to adapt to a digital economy shaped by tokenization, artificial intelligence, and changing settlement patterns. A CBDC is defined as digital money issued by a central bank, functioning as a digital claim on the central bank analogous to physical cash. The trend reflects regulators' desire to actively shape financial markets and behaviors, not merely act as gatekeepers. As private stablecoins and tokenized deposits expand, public digital money initiatives are being tested for retail, wholesale, and programmable use cases.

## Stablecoin Market Supply and Issuer Dynamics

The global stablecoin supply experienced its sharpest contraction since 2022, shedding more than $14 billion amid rising interest rates and regulatory-driven redemptions. The decline marks a shift away from rapid issuance growth and highlights how higher yields on traditional instruments can reduce demand for passive dollar-pegged assets. Regulatory scrutiny has also prompted issuers and users to reassess reserve structures, redemption mechanics, and compliance obligations. The contraction creates pressure on major issuers while opening room for yield-bearing synthetic dollars, privacy-enhanced rails, and tokenized deposits that can compete for institutional liquidity.

Total stablecoin market capitalization declined 1.74% to $288.45 billion, driven largely by redemption pressure on major issuers such as Tether and USDC. Despite the aggregate contraction, Ethena's USDe grew 7.29% as yield-bearing synthetic dollars attracted capital from non-yielding incumbents, signaling an internal rotation toward assets that generate returns. Arc chain also posted significant growth of 20.29%, highlighting divergent performance across the stablecoin sector as liquidity shifts away from passive dollar holdings. The data shows that stablecoin competition is increasingly shaped by yield, integration, and issuer reliability.

Sui's stablecoin supply increased 10.33% to $537.74 million over the past 30 days, diverging sharply from the broader market, where total US dollar stablecoin supply fell 5.34%. Circle's USDC accounted for 68.5% of the network's supply, while the native USDsui token cooled slightly from its recent peak. The expansion reflects rising decentralized application activity, steady fee generation, and gasless transfer features, even as SUI's price retraced roughly 40% from recent highs. The growth is mechanically linked to the Sui Foundation's SUI token buyback program, because yields generated from stablecoin reserves fund purchases of the native token.

Ripple's RLUSD stablecoin surpassed PayPal's PYUSD to rank seventh by market capitalization, reaching approximately $2.71 billion compared with PYUSD's $2.67 billion as of November 12, 2026. The crossover resulted from RLUSD's steady growth driven by exchange integrations and institutional adoption, while PYUSD experienced a notable supply contraction. About one-third of RLUSD's supply is held on the XRP Ledger, indicating demand extends beyond Ripple's native network. The lead remains narrow and susceptible to daily market fluctuations, showing that stablecoin rankings can shift quickly when issuer distribution, treasury demand, and redemption dynamics change.

Bitcoin fell to its lowest price since August, with analysts attributing the decline to concentrated liquidations and weakening demand across exchange-traded funds, stablecoins, and corporate treasuries. Market data highlighted shrinking stablecoin supply and sustained net outflows from spot ETFs as key factors eroding the market's marginal buying power. The drop was further exacerbated by hotter-than-expected inflation data and broader technology-sector volatility, which dampened overall risk appetite. The move underscores how stablecoin liquidity and institutional inflows have become closely linked to bitcoin's price stability and investor sentiment.

## Enterprise Settlement and Payment Infrastructure

Fireblocks disclosed that stablecoins account for 75% of its $7.5 trillion annual transaction volume, amounting to approximately $5.6 trillion in stablecoin flows. This dominance highlights their role as the primary settlement rail for institutional clients, with banks, exchanges, and asset managers moving decisively toward dollar-pegged digital assets for treasury operations, cross-border transfers, and settlement. The shift outpaces use of native crypto assets such as bitcoin and ether. The data underscores stablecoins as the working capital of the crypto economy, reducing friction in institutional transactions while concentrating operational risk among major issuers.

Stripe, in partnership with Paradigm, launched Tempo, a blockchain specifically designed for stablecoin payments, prioritizing transaction throughput over trading activity to support high-volume financial flows. The platform, valued at $5 billion after raising $500 million, facilitates real-world applications such as cross-border remittances, international payroll, and agentic commerce for major partners including DoorDash, Visa, and Anthropic. By allocating the majority of block space to simple point-to-point payments, Tempo aims to provide efficient, regulated infrastructure that leverages the speed and low cost of stablecoins for enterprise use. The launch signals growing demand for purpose-built payment chains.

SAP Pay integrated four MiCA-regulated stablecoins—euros, Swiss francs, Swedish kronor, and US dollars—into its Cloud ERP platform, enabling businesses to settle payments in these currencies. The integration is facilitated by Tereina, an SAP-backed company, and AllUnity, a joint venture involving Deutsche Asset Management, Galaxy, and Flow Traders. The initiative embeds multi-currency stablecoin settlement into corporate treasury workflows, supporting use cases such as supplier payments, foreign exchange, and intracompany treasury management. By embedding digital settlement inside enterprise software, SAP is helping companies connect treasury policy, accounting, and blockchain rails within a single operational environment.

Bottomline launched the first production-ready cross-border USDC payments on its corporate treasury hub, marking a milestone for enterprise stablecoin adoption. Simultaneously, Swift announced a blockchain-based shared ledger with 17 banks piloting tokenized cross-border payments, while European retail wallet Wero surpassed 52 million users and integrated with Worldpay to challenge card-network dominance. These developments, alongside the progression of PSD3 regulations, signal a shift toward faster, cheaper business-to-business settlement rails that reduce reliance on traditional correspondent banking. The combined efforts show financial institutions and software providers building parallel rails for tokenized value transfer.

BVNK launched a service enabling stablecoin-to-bank payouts through integration with Mastercard Move. The partnership allows users to convert digital assets into fiat currency for direct transfer to bank accounts, leveraging Mastercard's global payment network. The initiative aims to bridge cryptocurrency infrastructure and traditional banking systems for faster, more efficient cross-border settlements. The rollout supports compliance with payment-network requirements while expanding access to digital-asset liquidity. By connecting onchain stablecoin balances with mainstream payout rails, BVNK is reducing friction for merchants, payroll providers, and remittance platforms that need predictable fiat delivery for recipients.

Anchorage Digital and Visa added Anchorage's USDPT stablecoin as a settlement option for eligible partners on the VisaNet card network. Visa and its partners can use USDPT, which is fully backed by US dollars and issued under federal oversight, to settle payments. The collaboration marks an initial step, with both firms planning to explore adding more stablecoins to settlement options in the future. The partnership integrates a regulated stablecoin into card-network settlement, giving merchants and financial institutions a compliant digital-dollar option that can streamline clearing, reduce funding delays, and support real-time settlement.

Velera convened a cohort of credit unions and technology partners to collaboratively assess the viability of digital money, focusing on stablecoins and tokenized deposits. The organization reports that initial concerns regarding deposit outflows and card-interchange cannibalization have not materialized, with industry attention shifting toward business-to-business and cross-border payment applications. Emphasizing interoperability, Velera advocates for shared infrastructure to prevent fragmentation as institutions explore blockchain-enabled capabilities within regulated financial frameworks. The assessment suggests cooperative banks are testing digital money less as a retail replacement and more as an efficiency layer for member services, payments, and treasury operations.

Triple-A enabled stablecoin payments for the Singapore FinTech Association through a partnership that allows the organization to accept digital asset transactions. The initiative highlights the growing integration of stablecoin infrastructure within established financial-sector bodies in Singapore. By accepting digital assets, the association demonstrates practical use cases for regulated stablecoin settlement in membership fees, event payments, and community transactions. The arrangement also supports Singapore's broader effort to embed compliant digital-asset rails within industry institutions, normalizing stablecoin payments for organizations that need transparent, auditable, and efficient settlement.

## Network Activity and Regional Adoption

Stablecoin swaps increased significantly on Solana, rising from 6% to 19% of spot trading volume in the first half of 2026, while memecoin activity declined from 40% to 16% due to institutional adoption and a shift toward real-world assets. The total value of stablecoin assets on the network grew 51% year-over-year to $15.7 billion, processing more than $1.9 trillion in settlement value during this period. Additionally, tokenized equities emerged as a key growth driver, with Solana's share of onchain spot trading in this sector surging from 7% to approximately 97%. The mix shows maturing network usage.

Western Europe's stablecoin holdings surged 131% over the period, a growth rate more than double the global average, driven primarily by increased adoption in the United Kingdom and France. The UK recorded $2.09 billion in stablecoin transfers, reflecting growing institutional settlement and remittance flows, while French self-custody balances jumped 192% as retail investors shifted assets off centralized exchanges. The region's cryptocurrency economy reached $2.08 trillion in annual activity, supported by MiCA regulatory clarity, classification of USDC, licensing of banking consortiums for euro-based tokens, and banks developing regulated stablecoin products. The UK saw domestic stablecoin transfers rise even as broader crypto activity declined, underscoring stablecoins' growing role in regional financial infrastructure.

Privacy-preserving solution Raycash emerged to address transparency concerns by enabling confidential stablecoin payments on Ethereum through fully homomorphic encryption. The technology allows transactions to be verified onchain without revealing sensitive sender, recipient, or amount details, supporting use cases where commercial confidentiality and regulatory compliance must coexist. The development arrives as enterprises expand stablecoin settlement across supply chains, payroll, and treasury operations, where public visibility can expose competitive data. Raycash illustrates how cryptographic privacy tools are becoming relevant infrastructure for regulated digital dollars, balancing auditability with confidentiality.

## Outlook

Stablecoins are becoming regulated financial infrastructure, with enterprises, banks, and public institutions building rails for settlement, tokenization, and cross-border payments. Supply contraction and issuer competition are forcing issuers to emphasize yield, compliance, and interoperability, while regulatory clarity in Europe and Asia is accelerating adoption. The next phase will likely be defined by integration into mainstream treasury, banking, and public-payment systems.
