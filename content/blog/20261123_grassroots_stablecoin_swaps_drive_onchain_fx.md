## Grassroots Stablecoin Swaps Drive Onchain FX

Curve Finance founder Michael Egorov argues that onchain foreign exchange will scale primarily through grassroots stablecoin swaps by everyday users, not institutional bank trading desks. He cites data indicating rapid growth in cross-border stablecoin activity, with most emerging-market trades ranging between $1,000 and $10,000. This volume level, he says, is sustainable for automated market makers, whereas traditional firms find it unprofitable. In the model he describes, banks remain essential for issuance and compliance, while decentralized pools handle settlement of small-scale currency conversions more efficiently than card networks. The framing positions DeFi liquidity, not centralized treasury desks, as the main channel for retail cross-border payments.

## Coocon and Triple-A Explore Cross-Border Stablecoin Settlement

Coocon, a Korean business data platform, signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Singapore-based stablecoin firm Triple-A to explore global payment and settlement models using stablecoins. The partnership aims to connect Coocon’s domestic infrastructure with Triple-A’s global rails, focusing on cross-border remittances between Korea and ASEAN regions. Both companies plan to develop the necessary KYC and risk-management systems before moving beyond feasibility studies. No financial metrics were disclosed, and the parties are currently verifying the practical viability of the proposed models before determining commercial scale. The collaboration reflects growing interest in pairing local enterprise data with stablecoin rails for regional payment flows.

## Ripple’s RLUSD Enters Top Five Stablecoins

Ripple’s RLUSD stablecoin has entered the global top five by market capitalization, reaching a record $2.85 billion in circulating supply less than two years after its December 2024 launch. The milestone places RLUSD ahead of competitors including PayPal USD and Global Dollar, while it still trails Tether, Circle, Ethena, and Dai. Supply increased nearly 20% in three weeks even as major rivals Tether and Circle saw declines. Although Ethereum holds the majority of RLUSD’s total supply, the XRP Ledger accounts for the bulk of settlement volume, processing 57% of transactions in recent weeks. The milestone arrives amid market volatility, with Bitcoin crashing to $55,000 and XRP falling below $1.00.

## Hong Kong Explores Agentic AI for Crypto Crime Detection

Hong Kong’s de facto central bank, the HKMA, is exploring agentic AI tools to strengthen banks’ ability to detect crypto-related crimes by analyzing on-chain transfers alongside off-chain customer data. The initiative responds to the central role of stablecoins in illicit finance, with Chainalysis data indicating that stablecoins accounted for 84% of illicit crypto volume in 2025. Regulators are also leveraging the Virtual Asset Intelligence Taskforce, VAIT, to coordinate intelligence sharing among police, regulators, and licensed platforms. Pilots involving decentralized identity systems aim to reduce pseudonymity. These efforts complement Hong Kong’s newly launched stablecoin surveillance framework, which began operating after the first issuer licenses were issued in April.

## U.S. Senators Seek Relief for State Stablecoin Issuers

Six U.S. senators have urged the Treasury Department to grant temporary relief to state-regulated stablecoin issuers, allowing them to continue issuing tokens while their regulatory frameworks undergo federal certification. The bipartisan letter seeks to prevent a supply pause when the GENIUS Act takes effect on January 18, 2027, by ensuring state-licensed firms are not disadvantaged compared with federally approved applicants awaiting waivers. The request follows Treasury proposals that currently offer relief primarily to federal applicants, leaving open how issuers operating under state rules that are still under review should be treated. The intervention underscores the transition risk created by the new federal stablecoin regime.

## World Bank, Citi and Circle Pilot Stablecoin Development Finance

The World Bank Group, in partnership with Citi and Circle Internet Group, is exploring the use of U.S. dollar-denominated regulated stablecoins to improve the speed, cost-efficiency, and transparency of development finance disbursements. The initiative aims to integrate blockchain-based payment rails, specifically using USDC, with existing correspondent banking architecture so resources can be redirected toward greater development impact. The pilot will initially focus on intermediated and interoperable models in borrower countries where digital asset usage is legally permitted. It builds on the traceability benefits of the Bank’s existing FundsChain platform, signaling a cautious move toward regulated stablecoin rails for public-sector funding flows.

## Payoneer Secures Preliminary Approval for PAYO Digital Bank

Payoneer has received preliminary conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish PAYO Digital Bank, N.A., a national trust bank intended to issue the PAYO-USD stablecoin under federal supervision. The proposed institution will operate as a closed-loop system where PAYO-USD functions as an internal settlement token for cross-border payments and currency conversions, rather than as a publicly traded asset like USDC. Final authorization is required before operations can begin, and the bank will be subject to strict capital, liquidity, and compliance mandates. The structure separates it from FDIC-insured deposit-taking institutions, emphasizing its role as a settlement utility.

## Tether Invests in SCRYPT to Expand Institutional Payment Rails

On November 19, 2026, Tether announced a strategic investment in SCRYPT, a Swiss institutional trading and liquidity provider, to expand infrastructure for stablecoin payments. The partnership aims to create an integrated, end-to-end payment rail that combines fiat accounts, custody, and FX trading to facilitate faster and cheaper cross-border settlements. The move signals a shift toward bundled, institutional-grade stablecoin solutions designed to reduce operational complexity for banks and fintechs. It also highlights ongoing regulatory and compliance considerations, as payment providers increasingly seek consolidated services that combine liquidity, market access, and settlement within a single regulated framework.

## Euro Stablecoins Reach €747 Million Under MiCA

Euro stablecoins, legally defined as e-money tokens under the EU’s MiCA regulation, have grown to €747 million in circulation as of November 2026. Growth is driven primarily by licensed issuers such as Circle’s EURC and Société Générale’s EURCV. These tokens are strictly regulated: issuers must be banks or e-money institutions, maintain safe reserves, and prohibit interest payments to ensure redemption at par value. While the market has shifted away from unlicensed tokens toward compliant options, euro stablecoins remain a small fraction of the global stablecoin market compared with dollar-denominated assets. The data shows MiCA is consolidating issuance around regulated banks and fintech infrastructure.

## Trestly Launches kUSDT on Kaspa With Bridge Risks

Trestly has launched kUSDT, a dollar-pegged stablecoin on the Kaspa network, using a bridge model where collateral remains on Ethereum and BNB Chain while tokens circulate on Kaspa. The project employs a five-signer multisig structure for minting and redemption, with a notable 5 USDT minimum fee for Ethereum redemptions that affects small balances. While the code and vault addresses are open for verification, Trestly has not completed an independent audit. This raises questions about the trustworthiness of signer-gated bridges on proof-of-work chains, especially where users rely on off-chain custodial assumptions for peg stability and redemption access.

## Stablecoin Market Cap Falls to Yearly Low

The total market capitalization of stablecoins across all chains has declined to $284.21 billion, marking a yearly low and a 9.6% drop from the May peak of $314.39 billion. The decline represents a $30.2 billion decrease over the period, with the sector experiencing volatility throughout the year, including a brief rebound in September before falling further in November. Recent data shows continued downward pressure, with declines of 1.61% over the past seven days and 6.15% over the last 30 days. The market cap fell from $298.7 billion at the end of October to $288.02 billion by November 18, underscoring persistent contraction in the fourth quarter.

## Coinbase Receives Large USDC Inflow

An unidentified wallet transferred 237.3 million USDC to Coinbase, a significant stablecoin inflow that aligns with recent large-scale internal movements at the exchange. The deposit highlights Coinbase’s dominant role in the stablecoin ecosystem, where it reportedly holds approximately 30% of all USDC in circulation and reported $22 billion in average customer USDC balances in Q3 2026. Analysts view the transfer as indicative of rising institutional liquidity, noting that such flows often serve as dry powder for trading. The ultimate market impact depends on whether the funds are deployed into assets or moved only for custody logistics.

## GENIUS Act Limits Stablecoin Yield Payments

The GENIUS Act, effective January 18, 2027, prohibits stablecoin issuers from paying interest or yield solely for holding, using, or retaining payment stablecoins. Issuers are barred from rewarding passive balance retention, while exchanges and third parties may offer rewards funded by their own revenue, provided those arrangements do not constitute indirect issuer payments. The rule distinguishes permissible incentives, such as merchant discounts and referral fees, from prohibited yields, and it requires careful analysis of lending agreements where users transfer assets to earn returns. The provision aims to keep stablecoins focused on payments rather than yield-bearing instruments.

## Overall Outlook

The news points to a stablecoin market that is consolidating around regulated issuers, institutional rails, and compliance-driven infrastructure, even as total market capitalization contracts. Cross-border payment pilots, MiCA-compliant euro tokens, and federal trust-bank structures show expansion into real-world settlement, while grassroots DeFi swaps and emerging-market flows suggest demand persists outside traditional banking desks. Regulatory clarity, yield restrictions, and security audits are likely to shape adoption more than headline price moves.
