## Stablecoin Regulation and Licensing

The U.S. Treasury Department proposed rules implementing Section 3 of the GENIUS Act, setting licensing requirements for stablecoin issuers and conditions for offering foreign-issued stablecoins in the United States. Effective January 18, 2027, issuers must obtain a federal or state license to operate domestically, while digital asset service providers face restrictions on selling foreign stablecoins unless compliance and reciprocal-arrangement criteria are met. The Treasury is seeking public comment on the framework, which aims to clarify market-access rules, strengthen technological compliance with U.S. legal orders, and create a more predictable pathway for stablecoin products serving American users.

Taiwan’s Financial Supervisory Commission released draft Regulations Governing Permission and Administration of Stablecoin Issuance, allowing nonbank businesses to issue stablecoins under strict conditions. Eligible nonbank issuers must secure at least NT$300 million in capital and maintain reserve assets equal to or exceeding the value of tokens in circulation. The rules require issued tokens to be pegged one-to-one to the Taiwan dollar or a single foreign currency, while mandating anti-money-laundering compliance, internal controls, and supervision. The proposal moves Taiwan toward a formal stablecoin licensing regime, balancing innovation with consumer protection and limiting systemic risk from unlicensed issuers.

France’s National Assembly delayed the vote on the 2027 budget revenue section, leaving a proposed tax on gains from crypto-to-stablecoin swaps in the provisional text for further debate. Sponsored by Nicolas Sansu, the measure would remove current tax deferrals for exchanges involving electronic-money tokens, making such swaps taxable under the standard 31.4 percent rate beginning January 1, 2027. The government opposed the amendment because of implementation complexity, yet it remains part of the bill as lawmakers face a November 17 deadline to pass the full budget. The episode highlights Europe’s unresolved tax treatment of stablecoin conversions.

## Stablecoin Market Liquidity and Issuer Economics

Global stablecoin liquidity cooled as total supply fell to $295.9 billion, its lowest level in 13 months, with a $5.7 billion weekly decline tied to weak demand, geopolitical concerns, and security vulnerabilities reducing investor willingness to commit capital. As of November 3, 2026, dollar stablecoins worth $296.5 billion were circulating, with Tether and Circle holding 84.2 percent of supply. Tether accounted for more than half of the contraction, falling to $178.8 billion, while USDC stayed flat. The market had slipped 6% from April, from $280 billion to $263 billion, including approximately $17 billion of USDT withdrawn from major exchanges, notably Binance, and roughly 20% less stablecoin supply. Retail deposits fell, Bitcoin losses widened, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF outflows exceeded $1 billion, and insufficient liquidity reflected sector-wide caution constraining future growth.

Stablecoin issuer economics remain highly concentrated in reserve income. Dollar stablecoins worth $296.5 billion were circulating as of November 3, with Tether and Circle controlling 84.2 percent of supply, and issuers retained 100 percent of fees earned from interest on reserves. Large stablecoin yield pools offered median returns of approximately 4.14 percent, while most capital sat in low-yield environments. Higher advertised yields were typically associated with smaller pools and reward-token incentives rather than sustainable base income, creating a market where scale, not yield, dominates the economics of mainstream dollar stablecoins.

Chainalysis reported that China’s cryptocurrency ban pushed $104 billion in stablecoin payments through unregulated, peer-to-peer personal wallets between July 2025 and June 2026. With no licensed intermediaries operating in mainland China, the high transaction velocity shifts anti-money-laundering and compliance verification burdens entirely onto foreign recipients. By contrast, Hong Kong’s licensing regime processed $17.4 billion in net business inflows through regulated firms. The data shows institutional demand flowing toward compliant channels while illicit activity migrates to self-custody wallets, sharpening regulatory pressure on cross-border stablecoin transfers and raising the operational risk of accepting payments from counterparties outside licensed jurisdictions.

ProShares launched IQMM, a $17.8 billion stablecoin-ready money market ETF designed to help institutions manage reserves under the GENIUS Act by investing in cash and short-term U.S. Treasury securities. The fund underscores intensifying competition among asset managers such as Franklin Templeton and BlackRock to capture Treasury income generated by dollar-backed stablecoins through management fees. The structure offers liquidity and yield benefits for stablecoin issuers, but it introduces redemption risks: concentrated withdrawals could strain the ETF’s liquidity and pressure share prices. The product signals growing institutional demand for compliant, yield-bearing collateral solutions tied to stablecoin regulation.

## Payments, Banking, and Stablecoin Utility

Walmart-backed digital bank OnePay launched a feature allowing U.S. customers to fund checking accounts with USDC stablecoins across 12 blockchains, automatically converting deposits into dollars. The service uses Stripe-owned Bridge to orchestrate transfers, giving users an alternative to traditional ACH funding without maintaining a separate crypto wallet. OnePay targets freelancers and gig workers who receive stablecoin payments, eliminating exchange steps and conversion fees. The company plans to expand support to additional blockchains and stablecoins as part of a broader strategy to integrate digital assets with everyday banking services.

Visa and Mastercard highlighted expanding stablecoin settlement and agentic commerce during earnings calls. Visa reported an annualized stablecoin settlement run rate exceeding $25 billion, while Mastercard detailed new infrastructure products, including a combined offering with BVNK and integration with Mastercard Move, to facilitate stablecoin payments into local bank accounts and support tokenized card payments. Both companies stressed that agentic payments remain early-stage, requiring greater merchant readiness, processor compatibility, and stronger security frameworks before transactions initiated by software agents can scale safely across mainstream payment rails.

Solana’s stablecoin utility expanded sharply even as global liquidity contracted. Tracked stablecoin card spending reached $114.6 million by October 26, surpassing September’s $113.9 million record, and October’s monthly spending hit $141 million, a sevenfold year-on-year increase. The spend-only metric covers on-chain activity from nine of thirteen programs and excludes off-chain issuer data, so it does not capture all purchases. Solana’s stablecoin supply set a record $17.3 billion in late September, while broader spending measures exceeded $1.1 billion across selected programs that month, showing durable demand for Solana-based stablecoin payments amid liquidation pressures.

Swiss fintech Safirum launched CHF-S, the first publicly available Swiss franc stablecoin operating within FINMA’s regulatory guidelines and designed for retail and institutional users. The token is issued 1:1 against fiat reserves held in segregated accounts at Berner Kantonalbank and Banque Richelieu, with minting restricted to whitelisted institutional investors to meet anti-money-laundering requirements. Built on Solana, CHF-S incorporates mandatory identity verification and wallet whitelisting into its smart contracts, enabling fast, low-cost transactions for cross-border payments, treasury management, and tokenized asset settlement. Safirum generates revenue mainly through transaction fees, not interest on reserves, and positions CHF-S to reduce dependence on the U.S. dollar. The launch complements Swiss initiatives such as SIX and Twint’s sandbox-tested bank-guaranteed stablecoin, marking progress in Switzerland’s digital currency ecosystem.

Nigerian exchange Busha launched NGNb, a naira-pegged stablecoin backed by corresponding reserves and designed to address demand for accessible local-currency settlement instruments on blockchain networks. The token is currently accessible only through direct contact with Busha, with plans to expand availability to other exchanges and fintechs. Busha positions NGNb as a business-focused tool for institutional use, introducing a second naira-pegged stablecoin alongside the established cNGN. The launch raises liquidity fragmentation concerns within Nigeria’s financial ecosystem, as multiple local stablecoins compete for users, reserves, and settlement volume in a market with limited deep liquidity.

Mural CEO Sinclair Toffa predicted that AI agents could initiate 5 percent of cross-border payment transactions within the next year, driven by convergence of stablecoins, real-time payment networks, and artificial intelligence. To support this shift, Mural is developing agent-native infrastructure that embeds compliance, granular permissions, and audit trails directly into financial software to address authorization and liability concerns. The company leverages stablecoins to reduce foreign-exchange friction and improve dollar access for businesses in regions such as Latin America. Mural frames these assets as core components of a standardized global financial internet.

## Security, Governance, and Tokenized Assets

After a $6.3 billion Binance hack was attributed to North Korea, analysts examined security implications for tokenized assets moving onto blockchain rails. Tokenized securities can give issuers legal mechanisms to freeze and reissue stolen tokens, unlike native cryptocurrencies, making ownership restoration legally possible. Their protection, however, depends on issuer authority, smart-contract design, and the ability to act faster than decentralized markets. The incident underscores that tokenized markets face severe liquidity stress during attacks, because market makers often withdraw support when confidence falls, leaving legal remedies insufficient to stabilize trading conditions.

The XRP Ledger is approaching a second deadline to activate the fixCleanup3_4_0 amendment, which closes a security loophole allowing accounts with token-burning permissions to mint new tokens. The fix is critical for stablecoin integrity because it prevents unauthorized issuance by third-party services managing redemptions, protecting supply controls for assets such as Ripple’s RLUSD. Activation requires 14 consecutive days of validator support above 80 percent, and the amendment is currently projected to go live on November 4 after a previous delay caused by a temporary dip in votes. The outcome will test XRP Ledger governance resilience.

The governance group behind the Origin Dollar stablecoin, OUSD, proposed a new management fee structure and an equity earning pool. The changes are intended to support the protocol’s long-term sustainability while giving stakeholders new incentives tied to ecosystem performance. The proposal marks a strategic update to OUSD’s financial governance model, reflecting the broader industry trend of stablecoin issuers seeking durable revenue mechanisms beyond reserve interest. It also raises governance questions about how fees, equity participation, and protocol incentives should be balanced as stablecoins compete for users, liquidity, and institutional adoption.

## International Policy and Adoption

Brazil’s Itaú Unibanco became the first local bank to formally notify the central bank of its crypto trading and custody activities under new regulations, integrating digital assets into the formal financial framework. The notification signals that Brazil’s largest private bank is aligning crypto operations with supervisory expectations, providing a template for peer institutions. It also reflects the central bank’s effort to bring exchange, custody, and trading activities under formal oversight. The move strengthens Brazil’s position in Latin America’s digital finance landscape, where banks, fintechs, and regulators are competing to define compliant pathways for tokenized assets and stablecoin-related services.

Thailand’s Supreme Court ruled that Bitcoin qualifies as property under criminal law, strengthening legal protections against theft without reclassifying the asset as currency. The ruling gives prosecutors and courts a clearer basis for prosecuting crypto theft, fraud, and unauthorized access, while preserving Thailand’s position that Bitcoin is not legal tender. For exchanges, custodians, and token holders, the decision reduces legal uncertainty around ownership rights and criminal remedies. It also illustrates a regional regulatory pattern: Southeast Asian jurisdictions increasingly treat digital assets as property subject to criminal and civil law, separating ownership protection from monetary recognition.

The Reserve Bank of India urged state governments to adopt its Programmable Central Bank Digital Currency, P-CBDC, to improve the efficiency and traceability of government welfare payments. The push positions a programmable CBDC as a tool for reducing leakage, accelerating disbursement, and strengthening audit trails in public-finance systems. If widely adopted, the initiative would deepen India’s digital public infrastructure and create new rails for conditional, targeted transfers. The effort also contrasts with India’s stricter stance on private cryptocurrencies, reinforcing the central bank’s preference for state-controlled digital money over decentralized stablecoins and exchange-based settlement.

## Outlook

The digest points to a digital-currency landscape where regulation, liquidity, and payments utility are advancing together. Stablecoin markets remain supply-constrained and yield-sensitive, while adoption expands through banking integrations, card spending, cross-border settlement, and emerging agentic payments. Continued regulatory clarity, security hardening, and interoperable infrastructure will determine whether stablecoins move further into mainstream finance.
