## Overview

Stablecoin activity in the last period spans U.S. federal rulemaking, bank-led settlement initiatives, sovereign digital-currency planning, and consumer payment adoption. Key developments include Treasury’s GENIUS Act implementation proposal, major bank plans in the U.S. and Japan, India’s BRICS CBDC push, and persistent stablecoin use in Argentina. Meanwhile, market scale, card spending, presales, and exchange inflows show broadening liquidity, while regulatory debates focus on reserves, rewards, and credibility as payment systems.

## U.S. Treasury GENIUS Act Proposal

The U.S. Treasury has proposed rules to implement Section 3 of the GENIUS Act, governing who may issue, offer, sell, or otherwise make payment stablecoins available in the United States. The proposal covers U.S.-based permitted issuers and qualifying foreign issuers, defines key terms, and sets conditions for stablecoin issuance and offer/sale activities. It includes limited exceptions for non-U.S. issuers that avoid targeting U.S. persons. Comments are due by October 19, 2026. The Act will take effect on the earlier of January 18, 2027, or 120 days after final regulations from the relevant federal banking regulators. The rules are a major step toward a federal payment-stablecoin framework and could shape how banks, fintechs, and issuers compete in U.S. digital dollar payments.

## Hecto Financial and South Korea Stablecoin Rules

Hecto Financial, an official partner in Circle’s Circle Payments Network, is seen as a Korean payment-industry beneficiary if South Korea advances its Digital Asset Basic Act and related KRW stablecoin rules in September. iM Securities forecasts record annual sales of 221.3 billion won and operating profit of 25.6 billion won, attributing the improvement to growth in high-yield, membership-based simple cash-payment services. Hecto Financial plans to link CPN’s global settlement standards with its Hecto Wallet One infrastructure to support won stablecoin payments, settlement, and AI-agent commerce through the x402 protocol. The forecast shows how national stablecoin rules and payment-network integration could create a local company-level opportunity, particularly if KRW stablecoin adoption expands across commerce, settlement, and machine-initiated payments.

## Japan Banks' Stablecoin Council

Japan’s three largest banking groups—Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group, Mizuho Financial Group, and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation—plan to conduct commercial transactions using a stablecoin by March 2027. The banks are forming a voluntary council to develop governance and operating standards for the effort. They intend to act as joint settlement institutions and build shared infrastructure to keep deposits and payment activity within the banking sector rather than allowing it to migrate to nonbank stablecoin issuers. Potential applications include cross-border payments, digital-asset settlement, and programmable payments, with corporate use cases prioritized initially. The move is notable because it frames stablecoins as a defensive tool for major banks: preserving customer deposits and payment relationships while using shared industry infrastructure to set the terms for adoption.

## Sberbank, Russian Crypto Collateral, and Digital Ruble

Sberbank, Russia’s largest bank, said it plans to accept Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tether as loan collateral once the central bank permits such public circulation. The bank clarified that the coins would serve only as security, not as payment, because crypto spending remains prohibited under Russia’s new digital currency law. No loan terms, haircut, or launch date were disclosed, and Sberbank’s broader digital-asset plans depend on regulators allowing only the approved coins. Separately, the Bank of Russia has proposed permitting Bitcoin, Ether, and USDT on licensed exchanges after they meet market-capitalization, trading-volume, and history requirements. Sber executives also said there is little evidence of broad retail, corporate, or institutional demand for Russia’s digital ruble CBDC ahead of its Sept. 1 rollout.

## Stablecoin Reserves and U.S. Treasury Funding

Stablecoin reserve rules under the GENIUS Act require issuers to hold cash-like assets and, where Treasuries are used, securities maturing within 93 days. That means growing stablecoin issuance can support demand for Treasury bills and short-term financing rather than long-term bonds. Circle’s USDC reserves illustrate the boundary, with July data showing most assets in overnight Treasury repo, short Treasuries, and bank cash, while its direct Treasury holdings all matured by September. Separately, the Treasury doubled the maximum size of liquidity-support buybacks for 10- to 30-year nominal bonds, raising aggregate capacity for seven operations to at least $28 billion. However, this separate ceiling-based program does not direct stablecoin reserves into long-duration debt.

## India and BRICS CBDC Linkage

India plans to propose linking BRICS central bank digital currencies at the September 12-13 summit, building on an earlier RBI proposal to connect member nations’ digital currencies for trade and transactions. Finance ministers are expected to meet in early September to consider next steps on CBDC and fast-payment linkages. The effort could reduce settlement costs and lower the bloc’s reliance on the U.S. dollar by allowing member central banks to coordinate cross-border digital-currency infrastructure. The proposal is significant because it moves BRICS digital-currency discussions from conceptual interoperability toward concrete ministerial decision-making. It also places CBDCs alongside stablecoin and bank-led payment innovation as a possible alternative rail for international settlement, especially in trade finance and correspondent-banking substitution.

## Circle-USDC Chelsea Partnership

Circle has become Chelsea FC's principal and official front-of-shirt partner for the 2026/27 season, placing USDC branding on men's, women's, and academy kits. The deal is a major mainstream visibility milestone for the stablecoin, putting its name on a globally recognized football club across training, match, and youth-team apparel. It extends Circle's efforts to associate USDC with consumer-facing sports and entertainment, beyond payment products, corporate settlement, and exchange liquidity. For Circle, the partnership can increase brand recognition among non-crypto audiences and support broader acceptance of dollar-pegged digital assets. It also reinforces the market position of USDC as one of the two leading dollar stablecoins, even as the broader digital-currency market enters a phase of bank competition, CBDC planning, and consumer payment expansion.

## Major Stablecoin Liquidity Above $257 Billion

Tether's USDT reached a market capitalization of about $183.4 billion, while Circle's USDC stood near $74.1 billion, giving the two major dollar stablecoins a combined liquidity pool above $257 billion. The figure underlines how much of crypto's largest asset class now functions as a stable, dollar-linked store of value and settlement asset. That expanding pool is frequently used as a bridge between idle funds and new trades, presales, DeFi positions, or exchange activity. The scale matters because it gives both issuers and payment networks a broad base to support cards, merchant settlement, and cross-border payments, even as regulators and banks debate whether stablecoins should compete with or complement traditional deposit infrastructure.

## AlphaPepe Presale

The presale token AlphaPepe raised $2.54 million from more than 11,300 holders before its planned Q1 2027 DEX launch. The campaign indicates sustained retail participation in presale markets even as stablecoin liquidity and card payments expand. The token's launch timing, in the first quarter of 2027, suggests it is targeting a later trading window, while the holder count shows the raise was distributed across a broad base of participants. AlphaPepe's progress provides a retail-focused data point in digital-asset capital formation, separate from institutional stablecoin and bank initiatives, and shows continued investor interest in early-stage token offerings linked to the broader digital-currency cycle.

## ChainSpin Presale

ChainSpin's $SPIN presale has raised more than $150,000 and sold more than 30% of Stage 1. The token is priced at $0.0125 before the next scheduled presale increase, suggesting the project is using staged pricing to manage early demand and capital formation. Although the raise is small compared with the hundreds of billions of dollars in major stablecoin liquidity, it illustrates continued retail participation in early-stage digital-asset offerings. The presale also reflects a market segment that seeks exposure to new token projects before exchange listings or wider trading activity, separate from the stablecoin settlement, banking, and CBDC developments dominating the broader digital-currency news cycle.

## Delhi Lakshmi Yojana CBDC Wallet

Delhi government's Lakshmi Yojana will transfer Rs 1,000 monthly to eligible women through CBDC wallet, while Rs 1,500 will be held in recurring deposits locked until July 31, 2029, maturity reviewable after two years. Women may alternatively choose to deposit the full Rs 2,500 monthly into the recurring deposit account. The CBDC wallet funds are subject to spending restrictions, including prohibitions on alcohol, tobacco, narcotics, lottery tickets, gambling, and betting. The wallet is linked to the beneficiary's bank account, and the recurring deposit remains locked until the maturity date, with the lock-in period open to review after two years. The scheme shows how a state government is pairing a digital-currency wallet with savings discipline and targeted public benefit.

## Argentina Stablecoin Adoption

Argentina continues to show one of Latin America’s highest crypto adoption rates, with about one in five residents using crypto and downloads of its leading crypto apps nearly doubling in 2024. Dollar-pegged stablecoins became a key way for people to save and transact in dollars after capital controls and high inflation made official dollars hard to obtain. As much as 94% of peso crypto trading went to stablecoins, and a rising share of contractors were paid in USDC during inflationary periods. Even after restrictions eased and inflation fell, stablecoin use has persisted, suggesting the assets are becoming a durable payment and savings habit rather than a temporary hedge. That persistence indicates stablecoins can embed themselves in local financial behavior when currency risk and payment frictions are high.

## JPMorgan Evaluates Public Stablecoin

JPMorgan Chase said it has no current plan to issue a stablecoin but is evaluating a public-facing option separate from its institutional JPM Coin tokenized-deposit product. The bank already runs large-scale tokenized deposit operations through Kinexys, and its interest follows the GENIUS Act’s federal payment-stablecoin framework. It is exploring whether to enter digital dollar payments as U.S. regulation gives banks a clearer licensing path. Other U.S. banks and payment firms are simultaneously building shared stablecoin and tokenized-deposit networks, intensifying competition between banks and existing stablecoin issuers. JPMorgan’s evaluation could signal whether the largest U.S. bank treats stablecoins as a product opportunity rather than only a competitive threat to deposit funding.

## Bank-Led Tokenized-Deposit Networks

Wells Fargo, Bank of America and other lenders are advancing a jointly sponsored, initially dollar-backed digital token aimed at global corporate and commercial use. Separately, JPMorgan and other major banks are building a Clearing House network for tokenized commercial-bank deposits, with a first-half 2027 launch targeted. Thirty-nine state banking associations are developing BankChain Alliance, a permissioned blockchain for tokenized deposits, stablecoins, and programmable payments. Zelle operator Early Warning Services has launched ZLUSD for cross-border remittances. These bank-led initiatives combine large balance sheets, existing payment networks, and regulatory clarity from the GENIUS Act, targeting key rules and infrastructure launches from late 2026 through 2027.

## BIS Criticism of Stablecoins

BIS General Manager Pablo Hernández de Cos said at Jackson Hole that stablecoins still fail as large-scale money, citing weakness in redeemability, elasticity, interoperability, financial integrity, and AML enforcement. He noted global stablecoin supply reached $308 billion, warned that stablecoins can trade away from par across fragmented blockchains, and urged central banks to prioritize reserve-backed tokenized deposits that settle through central bank money. A BIS-linked study found major jurisdictions differ on stablecoin issuer permissions, with the United States requiring payment stablecoins to maintain one-for-one reserves in cash and short-term assets. He said stablecoins could coexist in narrower roles, such as decentralized lending, while tokenized bank deposits should carry most everyday payments.

## Tether CEO Dispute With BIS

Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said the Bank for International Settlements is rightfully concerned that stablecoins expose fractional-reserve banking’s core vulnerability, but he disputed the BIS view that tokenized deposits are the better foundation. Ardoino argued that fully reserved stablecoins hold 100% liquid assets, including U.S. Treasuries, while tokenized bank deposits are often supported by fractional reserves and only about 10% liquid reserves. He questioned why savers would choose fractional-reserve bank deposits and warned of broader financial-system repercussions if depositors moved savings into stablecoins. The comments frame a policy debate over whether future payment systems should center on fully reserved digital dollar assets or bank liabilities settled through central bank money.

## Stablecoin Exchange Inflows

Stablecoin exchanges have returned to net inflows for the first time in three months after a period of net outflows. The shift is notable because stablecoins are often used as a liquidity layer in crypto trading, and sustained inflows can signal growing exchange activity or renewed risk appetite. The report framed the change as a potential trend-reversal signal, especially as Bitcoin rebounded from a bearish trend. If stablecoin inflows continue, they could provide additional dry powder for token purchases, DeFi activity, and presale participation, potentially supporting further Bitcoin gains. The episode highlights how stablecoin flow data has become a near-real-time indicator of trader positioning and market momentum in the broader digital-currency ecosystem.

## Missouri Bank Stablecoin Rulebook

A century-old Missouri community bank now operates as Lead Bank in Kansas City and settles stablecoin transactions for customers nationwide. Its experience highlights how early adoption of digital dollar payment infrastructure helped the bank grow rather than be displaced by the new technology. The story supports the argument that Missouri banks would benefit from a clear stablecoin rulebook instead of additional regulatory roadblocks. For smaller banks, stablecoin settlement could become a revenue and customer-retention opportunity, especially if state rules provide predictable licensing, reserve, and compliance expectations. The narrative also fits a broader pattern in which banks are seeking to participate in digital payment innovation rather than cede payment activity to nonbank issuers and fintech networks.

## Stablecoin Card Spending

Stablecoin card spending has crossed $10.9 billion cumulatively, with July 2026 recording more than $1 billion in monthly spending. USDC and USDT accounted for most onchain card transactions. RedotPay said it has more than 8 million users and over $14 billion in annualized payment volume, and it expects annualized stablecoin card spending to reach $50 billion by 2028, up from roughly $12.5 billion today. Visa and Mastercard have expanded stablecoin-linked card access and settlement options, allowing merchants to accept stablecoin-funded payments without directly handling digital assets. The expansion suggests card networks are moving stablecoin payments into mainstream consumer rails, while issuer and exchange activity around USDC and USDT provides the liquidity base.

## Community Bank Opposition to Stablecoin Rewards

Community banks, represented by the Independent Community Bankers of America, are demanding a complete ban on stablecoin rewards because they say yields could draw deposits away from banks and reduce local lending. The ICBA estimates that up to $1.3 trillion in deposits could move to stablecoins and that the resulting outflow could cut local loans by $850 billion, hardening opposition to the CLARITY Act in the Senate. As a result, the bill faces a difficult September 15 vote, with many senators unlikely to provide the support needed to advance it. The dispute is central to U.S. stablecoin regulation: whether stablecoin issuers can offer interest-like rewards or must be limited to non-interesting digital dollars. It pits broader financial innovation and customer choice against bank deposit stability and local lending capacity.

## Overall Outlook

Overall outlook: U.S. federal rulemaking, bank participation, and consumer payment adoption are pushing stablecoins from speculative token into regulated infrastructure, while CBDC and reserve debates shape who controls digital settlement. The next months hinge on Treasury final rules, Senate CLARITY Act vote, BRICS CBDC discussions, and bank network launches. If community-bank opposition succeeds, reward restrictions could limit stablecoin appeal; if bank and card-network expansion continues, digital dollar payment options may broaden faster than expected.
