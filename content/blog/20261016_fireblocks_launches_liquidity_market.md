## Fireblocks Launches Liquidity Market

Fireblocks launched Liquidity Market, a platform that lets payments firms request stablecoin quotes from multiple OTC desks, compare bids side by side, and execute with the selected desk using existing credit lines and settlement terms. Fireblocks said it takes no spread on trades and does not take custody, with customers and desks settling directly. Initial quotes are available from B2C2 and Zodia Markets, and Cumberland, FalconX, GSR, Hercle, Nonco and Wintermute are expected to join. The service is aimed at giving payment providers transparent, execution-oriented stablecoin liquidity without adding a custodial intermediary or a marketplace fee, potentially lowering operational costs for firms that move stablecoins across counterparties and settlement windows.

## Episode Six Adds Verified Backing

Episode Six launched Verified Backing, a capability that checks bank-issued stablecoins and tokenized deposits against recorded backing in its Token Control Ledger before tokens are created and again before funds are released at redemption. The company says the checks run inside the issuing bank’s ledger, confirm that tokens remain genuine and outstanding, and are available to customers in markets where Episode Six operates. The move targets risk management for tokenized money by shifting verification from after-the-fact reconciliation to the exact points of token creation and redemption. It gives banks and counterparties a stronger control point for proving that each issued unit is supported before circulation and before cash is released, which is intended to reduce counterparty, settlement, and redemption risk in tokenized deposit and stablecoin programs.

## Gibraltar and ADI Explore Stablecoin Consortium

Gibraltar’s government and the Abu Dhabi-based ADI Foundation signed a memorandum of understanding to explore a Gibraltar consortium that would issue regulated stablecoins linked to sterling, the US dollar and the euro. The proposal would involve Gibraltar-licensed banks and ADI Chain infrastructure, with separate issuing entities, matching liquid reserves, independent checks and annual audits, to support institutional payments, cross-border transfers and trade across Europe, the Middle East and Africa. Implementation depends on commercial viability, founding members and regulatory approvals. Gibraltar says the project would diversify its economy and expand its role in digital finance, while the multi-currency design seeks to create a regulated stablecoin option for institutions needing predictable, auditable digital payment rails beyond traditional correspondent-banking arrangements.

## FSB Flags Stablecoin Error and Fraud Gaps

A Financial Stability Board report warns that stablecoins’ rapid cross-border settlement is outpacing protections for errors, fraud, wrong wallets, and failed redemptions, leaving businesses uncertain about who bears the loss. Instant token transfers also do not guarantee immediate conversion into spendable local currency, creating liquidity mismatches when banks and currency markets are closed. Banks and fintechs may therefore compete by offering recoverability, dispute resolution, transaction screening, and contractual protection around stablecoin payments. The finding points to a likely next differentiation layer: institutions that can promise reliable reversal paths, fraud controls, and currency-conversion support while keeping the settlement speed that makes stablecoins attractive. That could turn stablecoin adoption into a broader question about payment-liability allocation, operational resilience, and customer protection.

## Mastercard Launches Global Money Platform

Mastercard launched its Global Money Platform, the first product from its $1.8 billion acquisition of stablecoin infrastructure firm BVNK, giving banks, fintechs and enterprises a single API to hold and convert fiat currencies and stablecoins. The platform supports stablecoin-linked cards and payouts through Mastercard Move, with availability immediate. Mastercard said Move payouts will reach more than 40 currencies across more than 200 countries during 2027, with tokenized deposits planned. Nuvei, Marqeta, Ontop, Beem, and Almond FinTech are among the first integrators. The offering lets businesses embed multi-currency accounts that hold fiat and stablecoins side by side, spend stablecoin balances across Mastercard’s merchant network, and prepare for agentic commerce, positioning it as an extension of Mastercard’s payment economics, though execution, regulatory and competitive-fee risks remain.

## Visa Direct Moves Stablecoin Prefunding to Production

Visa said Visa Direct’s stablecoin-enabled prefunding capability has moved from pilot to commercial production, with Inyo Global as the first enabler for eligible clients and partners. Pilots had begun in 2025, and the now-production service allows businesses to prefund cross-border payouts through Visa Direct using USDC in near real time. The capability is intended to give companies faster and more flexible liquidity management than relying solely on traditional funding windows, particularly for cross-border payee payouts and distributed payroll use cases. For Visa, the move extends its network into on-chain settlement rails while keeping payouts inside its familiar card and transfer ecosystem. It also signals that stablecoin prefunding is becoming a practical merchant- and enterprise-facing feature rather than a limited experimental service.

## Sweden Revisits Digital Krona

The Riksbank is reconsidering a digital krona and has urged Swedish lawmakers to begin preparing legislation, citing the ECB’s digital euro progress and growing stablecoin use as reasons to reassess a CBDC. The central bank warned that Sweden’s payment system could become less competitive without its own digital currency and identified foreign-currency stablecoins, especially U.S. dollar-pegged tokens, as a longer-term risk to the krona. It estimates that introducing a digital krona would take at least five years, excluding legislative changes, and recommends starting legal preparations now even if Sweden ultimately decides not to launch a CBDC. The move frames a CBDC less as an immediate payment product and more as a regulatory and legal readiness exercise aimed at preserving payment-system competitiveness, currency resilience, and public settlement infrastructure.

## Former Crypto Adviser's Stake in Nxum Raises Concern

Bo Hines, a former White House crypto adviser who later joined Tether, reportedly held a one-third stake in Nxum, an investment firm that received $6.3 million from Tether while he was helping promote the GENIUS stablecoin law. The Wall Street Journal reported that Hines shared legislative updates with Nxum partners on Signal and urged them to criticize Republicans opposing the bill, renewing concerns about conflicts of interest and the revolving door between US policymakers and the stablecoin industry. The GENIUS Act was signed into law in July 2025 and is scheduled to take effect in January 2027 after further regulatory rulemaking. This revives scrutiny over whether former officials can use legislative access, insider knowledge, and industry relationships to influence policy while holding positions that benefit from the same regulatory outcome.

## BIS Paper Argues Money Needs Public Backstops

A BIS paper argues that money is a trust-based system anchored by central bank liabilities, with par convertibility connecting deposits to settlement money. It contends that private monetary innovations require public validation and backstops, making stablecoins’ current designs vulnerable under stress. Stablecoins seek validation from authorities, but their architectures generally lack the elasticity and institutional backstops needed to sustain par convertibility in a crisis. The paper implies that stablecoin issuers may need stronger public-sector support, better stress buffers, or clearer resolution paths if they are to preserve confidence during market turmoil. It positions central bank money as the reference point against which private digital payment instruments must be judged, especially when redemption pressure, liquidity mismatches, or confidence shocks could force rapid conversion from token form into usable settlement value.

## Arbitrum Launches USDG Stablecoin Incentive Program

Arbitrum launched a stablecoin incentive program for applications that hold at least $1 million in stablecoins and use USDG as their primary stablecoin on the network. The first 10 teams to reach $10 million in USDG total value locked will share a 2 million ARB prize pool, along with performance-based incentives and ecosystem support. The program is designed to encourage developers to build stablecoin-intensive applications on Arbitrum, deepen USDG circulation, and attract institutional or payment use cases that require predictable digital cash balances. By tying rewards to locked value and performance metrics, Arbitrum is trying to convert protocol incentives into durable stablecoin usage. The effort also signals that layer-2 networks are increasingly competing to become preferred venues for payments, treasury products, and tokenized financial applications.

## Qivalis Wins EMI Authorization

De Nederlandsche Bank authorized Qivalis, a 37-bank, 15-country euro stablecoin consortium, as an electronic money institution under the EU’s MiCA framework, allowing it to issue a euro-denominated electronic money token pegged 1:1 to the euro. Qivalis says the stablecoin will be distributed through banks, exchanges, payment providers, and market infrastructure rather than sold directly to retail customers, with launch planned within the coming months, including a target of the next month after remaining MiCA requirements are completed. The Amsterdam-based company must still publish a white paper and satisfy other requirements before issuance, and it has not announced a fixed launch date. The approval is a major regulatory milestone for Europe’s largest bank-backed euro stablecoin project, particularly as Circle’s USDC has already been classified as a significant e-money token under MiCA.

## Rain Launches Embedded Stablecoin Wallets

Rain has launched embedded stablecoin wallets that allow businesses to integrate digital-asset accounts directly into their applications for receiving, holding, spending, and transferring stablecoins. The initial non-custodial wallets support 12 blockchains and connect stablecoin balances to Rain-issued payment cards accepted at 175 million merchant locations, virtual accounts, and global payout services. The offering is aimed at developers and platforms that want stablecoin functionality without forcing users to manage separate crypto wallets. Rain plans to add custodial wallets and stablecoin balance-earning features before the end of 2026. By embedding wallets inside existing apps, Rain is trying to lower the adoption barrier for stablecoins and give businesses a direct route into card spending, virtual-account liquidity, and cross-border payout use cases.

## Plasma and Stripe Launch XPL Stablecoin Cards

Plasma (XPL) gained about 6.75% to around $0.0846 after announcing a partnership with Stripe to launch multi-currency stablecoin cards in more than 30 countries. The cards allow transactions in USD, EUR, BRL, USDC and other currencies while rewarding users with XPL cashback, expanding the token's utility and payment adoption. XPL remains above short-term moving averages but below longer-term benchmarks, with analysts expecting consolidation between $0.0817 and $0.0875 over the next two to three days. The move is significant because it ties the Plasma token to a broader stablecard ecosystem, giving users a way to spend stablecoins through familiar payment infrastructure. For Plasma, the partnership may turn XPL from a network token into a reward and utility asset tied to international consumer payment activity.

## Coinbase Extends Stablecoin Rails into Banking

Coinbase is extending its USDC stablecoin payment and settlement infrastructure into traditional banking through a September 2026 partnership with Moov, which connects more than 1,000 community banks and credit unions to payment systems. It also expanded its collaboration with Citi, linking its stablecoin payments stack to Citi’s institutional banking and merchant-acquiring systems. The deals let banks and payment providers integrate stablecoin capabilities without building their own infrastructure, while giving Coinbase access to established banking networks and a growth avenue beyond crypto trading. For community banks, the arrangement could provide ready-made stablecoin connectivity, faster settlement, and merchant payment options. For Coinbase, it reinforces a strategy of becoming infrastructure for mainstream financial institutions rather than relying primarily on exchange and retail crypto activity.

## Banks Sell Programmable Liquidity to Corporates

Major banks are competing for corporate deposits by offering tokenized deposits, faster settlement, and digital treasury platforms that reduce payment friction. Citi highlighted traction with tokenized deposits, allowing institutional clients to move funds on blockchain while keeping those funds within the regulated banking system, and described its token services as operating across multiple markets. The shift suggests corporate banking competition is moving beyond deposit rates toward programmable liquidity, automated payment controls, settlement availability, and data access. Banks can offer clients the ability to automate treasury sweeps, enforce payment policies, and improve settlement timing without moving balances into standalone crypto platforms. For CFOs, the attraction is not merely yield, but operational control: faster payment execution, real-time visibility, and tokenized assets that remain accessible under bank governance.

## France Advances Stablecoin and Digital-Asset Tax Changes

France’s Finance Committee backed a proposal to tax stablecoin swaps and adopted broader digital-asset amendments while rejecting the revenue section of the 2027 Finance Bill. The changes would require disclosure of self-custody wallets worth €100,000 or more, remove tax deferral for certain crypto-to-stablecoin conversions, and apply an exit tax to unrealized crypto gains above €800,000. Because the committee rejected the revenue part of the bill, the measures must be resubmitted. The stablecoin-swap tax is notable because it treats token exchanges more closely like other taxable financial transactions, while the disclosure and exit-tax rules target high-value self-custody holdings. Together, the proposals show France is trying to close perceived tax loopholes in digital-asset portfolios even as the broader budget remains contested.

## Thailand Sets Crypto ETF Framework

Thailand’s securities regulator issued a framework for digital currency ETFs, initially limited to Bitcoin and Ether funds, effective October 16. The Thai rules require such ETFs to hold at least 80% average net exposure to a single digital asset, list only on the Stock Exchange of Thailand, and use SEC-regulated custodians. The framework gives listed funds a structured path to offer token exposure while imposing concentration, listing, and custody safeguards. By limiting the first cohort to Bitcoin and Ether, Thailand is testing investor demand and market infrastructure with the two most established cryptocurrencies. The move also aligns exchange-listed products with domestic regulatory oversight, potentially making token ETFs more accessible to Thai investors who prefer regulated securities over direct custody of digital assets.

## USDC Receives Significant EMT Status

The European Banking Authority classified Circle’s USDC as the first significant electronic money token under MiCA, giving the EBA and France’s ACPR joint supervisory roles over Circle’s French subsidiary from November 12, 2026. Significant status raises reserve and capital requirements, requiring 60% of reserves to be held at commercial banks and 3% capital relative to reserves, compared with 30% and 2% for other EMTs. The EBA will oversee the issuer’s own funds, asset reserves, liquidity, recovery plans, and token redemption, while France’s ACPR retains responsibility for corporate governance, qualifying holdings, manager fit-and-proper assessments, outsourcing, and conflicts of interest. European central banks are now reviewing MiCA, with proposals to replace the bank-deposit floor with stricter daily and weekly liquidity limits.

## CBDC Holding-Limit Experiment Finds Trade-offs

An experiment across four euro-area countries found that retail CBDC holding limits can create a behavioral attraction effect, increasing users’ holdings when the limit is sufficiently high. An automatic “reverse waterfall” feature that converts balances into the limited CBDC increased adoption while reducing holdings. The results suggest that combining holding limits with automatic funding mechanisms can support CBDC use while helping to contain balances and potential financial stability risks. The finding is relevant for central banks designing CBDCs that need to encourage adoption without encouraging large private balances to shift out of bank deposits. It also highlights a policy trade-off: limits intended to cap financial-stability exposure can, paradoxically, make a CBDC more appealing when users believe the ceiling is generous enough to meet normal payment needs.

## Debate Grows Over Wholesale Settlement Finality

Stablecoins are expanding digital payments, but large-value wholesale transactions require settlement finality, asset quality, and interoperability that private stablecoins and tokenized deposits may not fully provide. The article argues that central bank money should remain the core settlement asset for systemic wholesale obligations because it avoids private credit, liquidity, and redemption risk and can expand elastically during stress. Tokenized deposits can support programmable commercial-bank-money flows, while stablecoins can serve specific bilateral or cross-border payment use cases, but both should interoperate with a trusted central-bank settlement layer. The argument distinguishes everyday payment utility from systemic settlement safety, suggesting that the future architecture may pair private stablecoins with public settlement finality rather than replace central bank money.

## Outlook

The items point to a stablecoin market that is becoming more institutional and payment-centric. New APIs, bank licenses, prefunding, verification, and embedded wallets lower adoption barriers, while MiCA supervision, France's tax rules, Sweden's CBDC rethink, and settlement-finality debates define the boundaries. The central question is whether private stablecoins and tokenized deposits can offer fast global payments while preserving public oversight, recoverability, and resilient settlement.
