## Tokenized Deposits and Bank-Led Infrastructure

Quant Network’s QNT token rallied more than 16% as renewed interest in real-world asset activity lifted the asset. The move followed its selection to operate the tokenized deposit network for banks in the United States and United Kingdom. Its partnership with The Clearing House is intended to set new standards for tokenized deposits, with bank access scheduled for the first half of 2027. The initiative is supported by the launch of the Multi-Ledger Rollup Layer 2.5, which enables bank tokens to serve as collateral in decentralized finance without bridges, boosting network activity and trading volume.

Crypto Finance Group successfully piloted a Swiss franc tokenized deposit network using Google Cloud Universal Ledger, showing how commercial banks can achieve instant, 24/7 account-to-account transfers without adopting public stablecoins. The pilot used GCUL’s deferred settlement mechanism to deliver stablecoin-like speed and programmability while preserving bank capital efficiency and satisfying strict regulatory requirements. The implementation offers regulated institutions a viable path to modernize payment infrastructure with permissioned blockchain technology that integrates seamlessly with core banking systems, avoiding the legal, custody, and market risks associated with open stablecoin issuance.

U.S. Bank completed a live pilot transaction for its proprietary USBDC stablecoin on the Stellar blockchain, though the asset remains restricted to internal bank entities and includes issuer-controlled freezing and clawback capabilities. Separately, a consortium of 21 major financial institutions committed to establishing a company in late 2026 to launch a multi-currency stablecoin solution by the first half of 2027. Anchorage Digital Bank also partnered with Securitize to facilitate direct minting and redemption of existing stablecoins such as USDtb, which is largely backed by BlackRock’s BUIDL fund. These moves show banks and infrastructure providers testing tokenized dollars, compliance controls, and on-chain settlement while expanding institutional access to yield-linked digital assets.

Qivalis, a consortium backed by 37 European banks, received authorization from De Nederlandsche Bank to operate as an Electronic Money Institution, paving the way for a regulated euro-denominated stablecoin. The initiative aims to provide digital settlement infrastructure for tokenized financial assets, allowing both the asset and cash legs of transactions to operate within the same programmable environment. It is part of a broader European strategy involving multiple forms of digital money, including tokenized deposits and the digital euro, to address interoperability and scalability challenges in on-chain finance. The authorization gives the project a formal supervisory footing and strengthens Europe’s push for homegrown settlement rails.

SoFi launched SoFi Earn, branded SOFIE, a tokenized bank deposit paying a promotional 4.40% APY and backed by FDIC insurance, positioning it as the first bank deposit token on its crypto platform. Each SOFIE token represents $1 held at SoFi Bank and can be converted one-to-one into the company’s stablecoin, SoFiUSD, offering a yield-bearing alternative to traditional stablecoins that currently earn no interest. The product aims to capture idle funds in the digital-asset ecosystem by combining the safety of bank deposits with the utility of on-chain assets, leveraging recent regulatory developments that support tokenized customer funds. It also signals banks’ willingness to compete directly with stablecoins for cash balances.

## Stablecoin Regulation Across Jurisdictions

Canada published draft Stablecoin Act rules, enacted March 2026, covering interprovincial and international coins; 60-day consultation ends December 23, 2026. Non-bank issuers must obtain pre-issuance registration with the Bank of Canada, keep reserves equal to supply in high-quality liquid assets at qualified Canadian institutions, file verified monthly reports, redeem within three business days, cap fees at 1%. Yield/interest banned, extending to issuer-third-party income-sharing and exchange holding rewards, transaction-based rewards allowed, direct retail redemption. Prudentially regulated institutions are exempt, so banks’ tokenized deposits remain interest-bearing and insured, coinciding bank tokenized deposits/CAD stablecoins. Rules align U.S. GENIUS Act and EU frameworks, take effect fall/late 2027, grant Finance Minister national-security powers, and centralize supervision at Bank of Canada, uncommon globally. Canadian Bankers Association supports yield ban; industry advocates prefer U.S. model permitting certain rewards.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency finalized regulations implementing the GENIUS Act, establishing federal requirements for payment stablecoin issuers effective January 18, 2027. The rules address reserves, redemptions, custody, audits, and risk management for eligible bank-related, federally qualified, state-qualified, and qualifying foreign issuers. The framework provides a clear regulatory timetable for the U.S. stablecoin sector, reducing uncertainty for issuers seeking compliance. Issuers must still satisfy Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering, and sanctions obligations through separate Treasury rulemaking, meaning full operational compliance will require coordinated action across multiple federal agencies.

The European Central Bank has recommended replacing MiCA’s fixed bank-deposit floors with liquidity buckets that require reserves to mature within one to five working days. The goal is to reduce contagion risk when tokenized deposits face mass redemptions. Global regulatory approaches converge on making reserves liquid, diversified, and geographically located to prevent rapid runs from reaching commercial banks. Models range from sovereign securities in the United States and Japan to central bank balances in the United Kingdom. For India, which lacks dedicated legislation, experts advise avoiding deposit concentration, calibrating rules to domestic market depth, and considering an interest ban alongside a GIFT City pilot before broader stablecoin adoption.

Coinbase will automatically convert balances of 14 non-compliant stablecoins, including USDT and PayPal USD, held by retail customers in the European Economic Area into USD Coin on October 30, 2026, to comply with the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation. The move follows an ESMA directive requiring authorized providers to cease services for stablecoins that fail European regulatory standards. Conversions will occur at prevailing market rates rather than a guaranteed one-to-one ratio. The initiative underscores MiCA’s role in reshaping the European stablecoin landscape, strengthening compliant tokens such as USDC while imposing tax and market risks on users who do not withdraw holdings before the deadline.

Tether is hiring a Head of Regulatory Affairs in Brussels to navigate Europe’s evolving financial landscape, responding specifically to ESMA’s recent ban on unlicensed electronic money tokens. The appointment follows the removal of Tether’s stablecoin from European crypto-exchange trading pairs, which are now dominated by MiCA-regulated alternatives such as Circle’s USDC. The role is intended to address regulatory compliance for Tether’s fiat-backed stablecoins and related digital-asset services within the European Union. The move signals Tether’s effort to regain relevance in a market where compliant tokens have gained structural advantages after MiCA enforcement reshaped trading venues and issuer eligibility.

## Market Data, Supply, and Trading Activity

Stablecoin market capitalization fell $2.19 billion during the week of October 19–25, 2026, in a weekly report. Decentralized exchange activity also contracted, with spot volume declining 12.21% and perpetual futures volume dropping 8.59% week-over-week. Protocol revenue decreased 10.57%, even though Central City recorded a significant surge in weekly revenue growth. The data point to softer on-chain trading and fee generation, while the stablecoin contraction suggests liquidity conditions weakened across major digital-asset venues during the period. The divergence highlights mixed signals: broad fee compression coexists with localized protocol growth. Investors face a quieter environment for leverage and yield.

The global stablecoin market has contracted by $12.73 billion since May 2026, falling to $300.36 billion, with Circle’s USDC accounting for most of the decline by shedding $4.69 billion over the past month. Shrinking stablecoin supply is often read as bearish, indicating reduced liquidity for cryptocurrency purchases. The situation is complicated by growth in tokenized Treasury products such as BlackRock’s BUIDL. Analysts suggest capital exiting stablecoins may not have left the crypto ecosystem entirely, potentially shifting into yield-bearing tokenized assets or circulating through regulatory conversions rather than being redeemed for fiat. The pattern points to a migration of liquidity within digital-asset markets rather than a simple exit to traditional finance.

## Product Launches, Corporate Moves, and Payments Infrastructure

HSBC launched RedCoin, an HKMA-regulated Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, for PayMe peer-to-peer payments, becoming the first licensed issuer under Hong Kong’s Stablecoins Ordinance. It serves over 3.3 million users for transfers and merchant payments, with plans for corporate, institutional, mobile-banking, and tokenized investment-product use, building on prior CBDC and wholesale deposit pilots. Standard Chartered-backed Anchorpoint delayed consumer rollout of HKDAP, citing public blockchain distribution through open intermediaries versus closed banking networks; it remains in institutional beta via authorized distributors, emphasizing cross-border interoperability and compliance, with retail targeted by end-2026. Both maintain reserves, with HSBC reporting higher circulation. Demand is strong: 97% of large enterprises use, test, or evaluate stablecoins, 24 top-50 banks consider tokenized deposits, and Visa, Mastercard, and Stripe back Open Standard to issue a dollar-backed stablecoin.

KuCoin launched a limited-time campaign offering users up to 7% annual percentage rate on stablecoin deposits through its KCUSD product. Participants must transfer USDT or USDC from external wallets to KuCoin and subscribe to KCUSD during the October 26–November 26, 2026 campaign period. The yield boost is tiered according to 30-day average daily net deposits, with the highest 7% APR available to users depositing more than 100,000 USDT-equivalent. Rewards are subject to a maximum reward principal of 1,000,000 KCUSD per user. The promotion is designed to attract stablecoin liquidity and increase platform deposits ahead of year-end competition.

Stablecoin firm Redotpay is reportedly preparing to file for a confidential initial public offering by the end of the year. The move signals the company’s intent to enter public markets and marks a notable step for a participant in the stablecoin sector. If pursued, the listing would provide a public-market benchmark for stablecoin-focused companies and could strengthen the firm’s capital position, brand visibility, and access to institutional investors. The development also reflects growing confidence that stablecoin businesses can scale beyond crypto-native exchanges and payments rails into regulated, publicly traded financial technology.

Cryptobanco is expanding its B2B crypto infrastructure to help finance teams manage the growing volume of stablecoin payments, which reached an estimated $137 billion to $153 billion in B2B settlements in 2026. The platform consolidates wallet management, bulk payouts, and transaction records to address operational challenges tied to cross-border recurring payments. By offering role-based access controls, risk screening, and multi-asset support, Cryptobanco aims to give corporations greater visibility and compliance for digital-asset transactions. The expansion reflects rising enterprise demand for stablecoin payment rails that can be integrated into treasury workflows with audit-ready controls and reduced settlement friction.

## Regional Adoption and CBDC Developments

The MENA crypto economy remained essentially flat at $443.3 billion in 2026, driven by divergent national trends. The United Arab Emirates’ crypto sector grew 32% to $104.8 billion, supported by strong institutional adoption and regulated stablecoin usage. Türkiye’s market contracted slightly, even as its peer-to-peer stablecoin volume surged fivefold to $3.56 billion, functioning mainly as a dollar-denominated savings tool amid regulatory restrictions on crypto payments. These shifts highlight the growing role of stablecoins as financial infrastructure for cross-border settlements and savings within the region’s evolving regulatory landscape, with compliance-oriented hubs gaining share while constrained markets rely on stablecoins for preservation and transfer.

Nigeria’s eNaira, launched in 2021 using a permissioned distributed ledger, failed to achieve significant adoption because its restrictive design prioritized risk containment over user incentives. Engineers from participating banks found that low usage rates produced empty float accounts, which prevented successful integration and testing of CBDC infrastructure. The project illustrates the difficulty of implementing central bank digital currencies that compete with market-driven stablecoins such as USDT, especially when privacy, accessibility, rewards, and everyday usability are limited. The eNaira experience highlights that technical compliance alone is insufficient: adoption depends on designing features that address users’ financial needs and offer tangible benefits over existing cash, bank, and stablecoin options.

European Central Bank Executive Board member Piero Cipollone said a digital euro is essential for strengthening Europe’s payment sovereignty by reducing reliance on non-European card schemes, which currently handle two-thirds of online transactions. The proposed central bank digital currency would offer offline payment capabilities and be accessible through bank-integrated apps and a standardized Eurosystem app, ensuring users maintain their banking relationships. The ECB plans to pilot real transactions in 2027, with a potential launch in 2029 contingent on advancement of EU legislation by the end of 2026. The proposal positions the digital euro as a public settlement option alongside tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins.

## Legal, Security, and CBDC Narratives

On April 23, 2026, Tether announced it had coordinated with law enforcement to freeze $344 million in USDT, bringing its total frozen assets to more than $4.4 billion. The event underscores that private-key control does not guarantee transferability for central-bank-digital-currency-adjacent assets such as USDC and EURC, because issuers Circle and Tether retain contract-level roles to blacklist addresses under sanctions and legal orders. Blocked funds remain visible on-chain, and users in eligible jurisdictions may seek reimbursement through issuer-specific policies, provided they are not classified as restricted persons under MiCA regulations. The case reinforces compliance risk for users relying on issuer-controlled stablecoins.

World Liberty Financial rejected allegations in a lawsuit filed by a Circle shareholder claiming that UAE-linked transactions involving the USD1 stablecoin harmed Circle. The plaintiff alleges these activities negatively affected Circle’s USDC stablecoin, raising questions about competitive impact and cross-border stablecoin flows. World Liberty maintains the claims are without merit, and Circle stated that it did not initiate or authorize the litigation. No court ruling has been issued regarding the dispute over established stablecoin business operations. The case illustrates how shareholder litigation can create legal uncertainty around stablecoin issuers, partnerships, and jurisdictionally sensitive transactions.

A widely circulated article advances a speculative claim that attacks targeting central bank digital currency wallets and exchanges are planned. It argues that authorities prefer centralized storage over personal cold wallets to prevent banks from losing customers, suggesting widespread cold-storage adoption would undermine the current banking model. The text provides no verifiable evidence for these assertions, leaving the claim as a narrative about monetary-control incentives rather than a documented operational threat. The framing reflects broader anxieties about privacy, surveillance, and the competitive tension between state-run digital money and self-custody tools.

## Overall Outlook

Overall, tokenized deposits, regulated stablecoins, and bank-led pilots are converging with stricter reserve, redemption, and yield rules. Regulatory timelines in Canada, the United States, and Europe are pushing institutions toward compliant issuance, while MiCA enforcement and GENIUS Act rules are reshaping market structure. Stablecoin supply contraction, DEX softness, and yield migration suggest liquidity is rotating within digital-asset ecosystems. Expect continued competition among banks, tokenized deposits, and compliant stablecoins, with legal and compliance risks remaining central.
