## Stablecoin and Tokenized-Asset Market Snapshot

October market data showed stablecoin capitalization rising slightly to $305.5 billion, with Tether maintaining its lead at $18.3 billion and USDC declining 2.80%. Tokenized real-world assets on-chain grew 2.92%, with reported totals spanning $3.45 billion to $34.5 billion, driven by private credit, which advanced 11.8%, and tokenized equities reaching $4.89 billion. Despite asset-value gains, on-chain daily trading volume fell sharply by 39.3% to $317 million, underscoring weaker turnover. The mixed picture highlights growing issuance and collateralization of real-world assets, while trading activity lagged behind. This divergence is important for institutions assessing liquidity, settlement depth, and the durability of tokenized-asset growth beyond balance-sheet expansion.

## Stablecoin Supply Contraction

Total stablecoin supply fell to $298.5 billion by November 1, the lowest level since August, after a $3.1 billion decline since October 28 and a $2.1 billion decrease earlier in the year that had taken supply to about $301.6 billion. The contraction reverses the roughly $100 billion, 49% surge recorded in 2025 and leaves supply $5.2 billion below January 1, 2026, completing a full reversal of the May peak of $315.5 billion. Losses were concentrated in Tether and Circle, whose combined supply dropped by more than $9 billion year-to-date, while smaller stablecoins added $7.2 billion and gained market share. The decline coincided with a 7% Bitcoin price drop and the implementation of the GENIUS Act, the first U.S. framework specifically governing stablecoins, signaling regulatory clarity amid short-term deleveraging.

## Standard Chartered Stablecoin Forecast

Standard Chartered forecasts the stablecoin market, currently valued at approximately $299 billion, will reach $2 trillion by 2030, a projection that had previously been expected two years earlier. The bank emphasizes Tether’s dominance, with more than 60% of the sector, backed by comparatively riskier reserve assets, while USDC maintains safer, liquid reserves under the 2025 GENIUS Act framework. The report also points to Open USD, a syndicate-based stablecoin issued by Bridge that distributes reserve yield to participating financial institutions. The forecast signals confidence in stablecoins as settlement rails for payments, treasuries, and tokenized finance, while reserve quality and yield-sharing structures become central competitive factors.

## Stablecoins as Core Financial Infrastructure

Stablecoins are moving from niche crypto tools toward core financial infrastructure, with major payment providers and banks integrating them for near-instant settlement and lower costs. Clear regulatory frameworks, including the OCC’s GENIUS Act rules, are intended to resolve past uncertainty by setting standards for reserves, transparency, and institutional accountability. These standards are expected to accelerate adoption by banks, fintechs, and enterprises seeking predictable compliance. Industry experts warn, however, that excessive regulation could stifle innovation by favoring large incumbents over smaller startups. The result may be faster institutional uptake, but also a narrower path for decentralized solutions and emerging stablecoin issuers competing on openness and programmability.

## European MiCA Enforcement and USDT

Major European platforms, including Bitpanda, Kraken, and Coinbase, have stopped selling Tether’s USDT to customers in the European Economic Area because the token does not comply with MiCA. ESMA has mandated licensed providers to cease all services related to USDT by January 8, 2027, although private individuals may continue holding it. The move accelerates the shift toward MiCA-compliant alternatives, with USDC emerging as the primary dollar stablecoin for the region. USDC offers users a licensed route supervised by the European Banking Authority, reinforcing the EU’s push to separate compliant stablecoins from non-compliant issuance. The transition may raise switching costs for traders and merchants while strengthening institutional confidence in regulated dollar-denominated digital cash.

## Stablecoin Yield and Leverage Risks

Stablecoin yield strategies that borrow to purchase additional tokens carry asymmetric risk, because rising borrowing costs can erode principal even when pegs remain intact. A hypothetical example shows that compounding interest on leveraged positions can produce net losses, revealing that lending collateralized stablecoin positions is not equivalent to earning a simple deposit return. The analysis warns that yield-chasing strategies can be vulnerable to rate shifts, liquidity constraints, and funding-cost volatility. For institutions and sophisticated investors, the key implication is that stablecoin yield must be evaluated against financing costs, counterparty exposure, and collateral requirements. As stablecoin lending expands, transparent stress-testing and conservative leverage limits will become essential safeguards.

## Ethereum Stablecoin and Tokenized-Stock Growth

Ethereum continues to anchor the stablecoin economy, hosting 54% of all stablecoins according to Token Terminal. The network is also experiencing rapid growth in tokenized stocks, which have surged 170% year-on-year to $827 million, with 86% using a 1:1 backing model. This concentration underscores Ethereum’s role as the primary settlement layer for dollar-denominated digital assets and tokenized securities. The expanding footprint of tokenized equities may attract greater regulatory scrutiny, particularly around issuer compliance, custody, disclosure, and secondary-market trading. For developers and institutions, Ethereum’s dominance creates both opportunity and pressure: its liquidity and tooling support adoption, while its scale invites closer oversight as tokenization moves beyond crypto-native assets into mainstream financial instruments.

## XRP Ledger Stablecoin Milestone

The XRP Ledger’s total stablecoin supply surpassed $1 billion for the first time in the third quarter, reaching $1.03 billion, while transfer volumes rose 39.5% to $14.09 billion. Ripple’s RLUSD dominated the milestone, capturing 98.1% of the ledger’s stablecoin liquidity as its supply increased 49.7% to $1.01 billion. The growth positions XRPL as broader financial infrastructure, especially for settlement, payments, and tokenized value transfer. Analysts caution that stablecoin expansion does not automatically translate into directional demand for XRP, since the token’s bridge-currency role depends on usage patterns, liquidity incentives, and cross-border flows. The milestone therefore signals ecosystem maturation, while leaving XRP’s price exposure dependent on how RLUSD-driven activity interacts with native ledger demand.

## Ripple Strategy: XRP and RLUSD

Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse reaffirmed that XRP remains central to the company’s strategy, positioning RLUSD as an entry point for institutional clients rather than a replacement for XRP. He noted that roughly half of all RLUSD is issued on the XRP Ledger, which supports liquidity and trading volume, while XRP’s distinctive value lies in its role as a neutral bridge asset for cross-border currency transactions. The comments followed Ripple’s final approval from the OCC to operate a national trust bank and BNY Mellon’s commencement of custody services for both XRP and RLUSD. The framing highlights a dual-track strategy: stablecoin adoption for institutional settlement, paired with XRP’s continued importance for global liquidity and foreign-exchange utility.

## Curve StableSwap on Stellar

Curve Finance launched StableSwap on the Stellar mainnet, marking its first native deployment outside Ethereum Virtual Machine-compatible networks. Built on Stellar’s Soroban smart-contract platform, the integration aims to improve liquidity for dollar-pegged stablecoins and tokenized assets amid rising institutional demand. The deployment supports permissionless pool creation and swaps, enabling more efficient trading and opening potential lending applications within the Stellar ecosystem. By extending Curve’s automated market-making infrastructure to Stellar, the project adds a non-EVM venue for stablecoin exchange and deepens interoperability between established DeFi tooling and payment-oriented chains. The move may attract users seeking low-cost, fast settlement and institutions looking for compliant, programmable liquidity rails outside Ethereum-centric environments.

## Anchorpoint HKDAP on Solana

Anchorpoint Financial, a Standard Chartered-led consortium licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, has partnered with the Solana Foundation to expand global access to its Hong Kong dollar-pegged stablecoin, HKDAP. The collaboration enables native minting and redemption of HKDAP on Solana, aiming to support retail payments, high-volume transactions, tokenized real-world asset trading, and treasury management. This is Anchorpoint’s first partnership with a public blockchain foundation, leveraging Solana’s fast settlement speeds to strengthen Hong Kong’s competitiveness in capital markets. Specific launch dates and technical details remain unconfirmed. The initiative bridges regulated financial infrastructure with public blockchain rails, extending the international appeal of the Hong Kong dollar while testing scalable issuance and redemption pathways.

## HashKey and RDUSD on HSK Chain

HashKey On-Chain and RD Technologies have formed a strategic partnership to deploy the US dollar stablecoin RDUSD on HSK Chain, an institutional-grade Ethereum Layer 2 network. The collaboration aims to expand RDUSD’s utility beyond digital asset trading into real-world business scenarios, including cross-border trade, corporate payments, and treasury management for institutional clients. The initiative leverages HashKey On-Chain’s infrastructure to support on-chain testing, settlement, and the tokenization of real-world assets in compliance with regulatory frameworks. By embedding a stablecoin within a regulated Layer 2 environment, the partnership targets enterprise users that require auditability, predictable settlement, and asset-backed liquidity. The development reflects growing demand for stablecoins that function as operational financial tools rather than merely speculative trading instruments.

## OnePay USDC Deposits

Walmart-backed financial service OnePay has launched a stablecoin deposit feature powered by Stripe that allows selected U.S. users to deposit USDC from external wallets into their checking accounts. The service instantly converts the stablecoins to U.S. dollars with no fees during the initial launch phase, supporting deposits across 12 blockchains, including Ethereum, Solana, and Base. OnePay plans to evaluate adding other stablecoins in the future. The product lowers friction for users who hold digital dollars on-chain by linking them directly to conventional bank-like accounts. It also illustrates how mainstream retail finance is beginning to treat stablecoins as accessible payment assets, potentially accelerating everyday adoption through familiar interfaces and low-cost conversion.

## Quidax and Nigeria Adoption

Quidax CEO Buchi Okoro said trust, regulation, and transparency are essential for institutional adoption of stablecoins and digital assets, emphasizing the firm’s commitment to building a compliant framework as the first exchange to receive a provisional license from Nigeria’s SEC. Speaking at an event hosted in partnership with Tether, Okoro argued that digital trust must be earned through rigorous compliance so banks and fintechs can serve customers effectively. SEC representative John Briggs supported this view, citing data showing Nigeria leads Sub-Saharan Africa in crypto adoption, with more than $92 billion in on-chain value. Briggs stressed the Commission’s determination to ensure this future is built on trust, underscoring Nigeria’s role as a major regional adoption hub.

## ECB Digital Euro and BIS Outlook

The European Central Bank is actively developing the digital euro as a central bank digital currency, with plans for a potential launch by 2035 alongside the expansion of stablecoins and tokenization experiments. The discussion frames the transition from physical cash toward digital financial systems, emphasizing that public and private digital money will likely coexist. The Bank for International Settlements is studying new infrastructures based on digital currencies and tokenized assets, reflecting a broader shift in monetary architecture. Key developments projected between 2026 and 2031 include the growth of blockchain in finance and the integration of artificial intelligence with digital assets. The timeline positions Europe as an early mover, while leaving open design, privacy, and cross-border interoperability questions.

## Cardano Stablecoin and Quantum Proposals

Cardano released its November 1 CIP roundup, introducing a new proposal to standardize compliance requirements for regulated stablecoins. The proposal is intended to streamline integration for wallet and infrastructure providers, reducing fragmentation for issuers and users. The update also includes CIP-0206, which proposes quantum-resistant signature verification functions for Plutus smart contracts using NIST-standardized algorithms, anticipating future cryptographic threats. Ongoing reviews of governance metadata rules continue to shape community coordination. Together, the proposals show Cardano’s focus on regulatory readiness, long-term security, and developer compatibility. If adopted, they could make regulated stablecoin issuance more practical on Cardano and strengthen the ecosystem’s resilience against emerging quantum-computing risks.

## Binance Hack and Withdrawal Reopening

Approximately $2 billion in assets, including nearly $1.2 billion in dollar-pegged stablecoins such as USDT and USDC, left Binance within three hours of the exchange reopening withdrawals. The movement followed a $6.3 billion hack, during which Binance said it has replaced about 95% of the stolen assets using its own funds and is now processing payouts from newly generated wallets. Withdrawals have reopened for only 15 networks so far, with remaining wallets undergoing security audits. Market reactions have been muted despite initial speculation of a price rally. The episode highlights the persistent tension between exchange liquidity, security controls, and user demand for rapid access after breaches.

## Bitcoin Privacy and KYC Pressures

Privacy discussions around Bitcoin are increasingly framed around the risks of Know Your Customer requirements and the methods used to acquire Bitcoin without identity verification. The coverage highlights how KYC regimes can expose transaction histories, reduce financial confidentiality, and push users toward privacy-focused acquisition routes. It notes that demand for non-identifying access persists among users seeking control over personal data, while regulators emphasize anti-money-laundering compliance. The tension creates a dual-track market: regulated exchanges and custodial services serve institutional and mainstream users, while privacy-oriented tools cater to those prioritizing anonymity. The development underscores that digital-asset adoption is shaped not only by price and regulation, but also by trust, surveillance concerns, and the desire for self-sovereignty.

## Overall Outlook

The outlook points to a bifurcated stablecoin market: regulatory compliance and institutional infrastructure are advancing rapidly, while short-term supply contraction and trading-volume weakness show that adoption is not linear. Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Stellar, Cardano, and Solana are all being positioned as venues for regulated stablecoins, tokenized assets, and cross-border settlement. MiCA, GENIUS Act, and central-bank digital currency initiatives are raising compliance expectations, increasing the importance of transparent reserves and auditable issuance. At the same time, privacy concerns, leverage risks, and exchange security incidents remain countervailing pressures. The next phase will likely favor stablecoins that combine liquidity, regulatory clarity, and real-world utility.
