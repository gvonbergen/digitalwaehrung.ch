## Stablecoin Liquidity Contraction

Glassnode data show the total stablecoin supply fell by about $14.2 billion in the 30 days ending November 9, the largest decline since June 2022, with dollar stablecoins down $14.86 billion through November 12 and roughly $17 billion over the past month. The contraction reflects holders redeeming tokens for U.S. dollars, draining on-chain liquidity and weakening the buying power behind Bitcoin’s rebound. Glassnode says a renewed rise in stablecoin supply would be the key signal that new capital is entering the market, although the assessment currently relies on a single provider. Such outflows hinder bull markets, which rely on net inflows.

## Stablecoin Market Cap and Tokenized Treasuries

The stablecoin market has shed $26.15 billion since its May 2026 peak, an 8.41% decline to $284.8 billion, with Tether and USDC posting monthly losses despite holding 85.8% market share. Tokenized U.S. Treasury funds contracted 5.36% over 30 days to $13.91 billion, as of November 14, led by reductions in BlackRock’s BUIDL and Circle’s USYC. This synchronized drawdown points to investors leaving on-chain dollar instruments amid traditional yield competition and lower crypto trading activity. Analysts remain split on whether the move is a risk-off exit from crypto or a rotation into traditional Treasuries and money-market funds.

## Bitcoin Price Pressure

Bitcoin slid to $66,900, its lowest level since August, as a $14.2 billion contraction in stablecoin supply and $1.6 billion in net outflows from U.S. spot ETFs drained liquidity. The shrinking pool of dollar-pegged tokens amplified the impact of forced liquidations in derivatives markets, turning a technical pullback into a broader liquidity squeeze. Analysts are watching stablecoin supply trends and ETF flows as decisive indicators of whether the crypto market is continuing to lose capital, with any stabilization in these flows needed to support a durable recovery.

## Stablecoin Order-Book Dominance

Dollar-pegged stablecoins account for 99.1% of stablecoin order-book volume, underscoring the dominance of USDT and USDC in crypto liquidity. By contrast, euro-denominated fiat trading represents only 11.8% of activity across 33 government-issued currencies, highlighting a sharp gap between fiat currency diversity and stablecoin concentration. The disparity creates structural risks for European market participants, who face dollar exposure when using stablecoins, and reinforces regulatory concerns about the euro’s limited role in digital finance and the need for more resilient local settlement assets.

## Kava Lend Exploit

An attacker drained about $3.8 million in KAVA tokens from Kava Lend by exploiting a code vulnerability in a dormant BUSD stablecoin market, inflating collateral values to borrow against them. Most stolen tokens were sold for $2.1 million on BNB Chain, causing the KAVA token price to fall nearly 40%. Kava Labs confirmed the incident was isolated to Kava Lend, not an infinite mint issue, and paused certain bridge functions as a precaution while investigating the exploit and assessing remediation steps.

## Tether–Kazakhstan Stablecoin Exploration

Tether signed a memorandum of understanding with Kazakhstan’s National Bank to explore infrastructure for a tenge-backed stablecoin and real-world asset tokenization. The collaboration operates within Kazakhstan’s regulatory framework, which separates the central bank’s digital tenge from privately issued stablecoins, and targets cross-border payments and decentralized finance use cases. The exploratory phase will assess whether a Tether-issued token can provide settlement capabilities for tokenized assets that existing local infrastructure does not efficiently support, potentially expanding Tether’s regulated footprint in Central Asia.

## SBI and Cambodia Blockchain Pilots

SBI Holdings and Cambodia’s central bank announced two blockchain pilots on November 13 to improve government bond management and cross-border payments. The first initiative tokenizes Cambodian government bonds using the Canton Network to enhance accessibility and transparency. The second tests the Japanese yen stablecoin JPYSC to streamline international money transfers between Japan and Cambodia. Both projects aim to reduce transaction costs and intermediaries, with initial demonstrations scheduled for the Black Swan Summit in Phnom Penh. The pilots also signal growing regional interest in tokenized sovereign debt and faster settlement rails.

## UK, Singapore, and Thailand FX Settlement Pilot

The central banks of the United Kingdom, Singapore, and Thailand have launched a pilot project to test cross-border foreign exchange settlement using stablecoins. The collaboration explores how digital currencies can streamline international transactions and improve settlement efficiency, reducing friction in correspondent banking and foreign exchange operations. The initiative reflects growing interest among global regulators in leveraging stablecoin technology for financial infrastructure, while providing a controlled environment to assess risks, interoperability standards, and the operational benefits of programmable settlement assets and governance models.

## RBI Digital Rupee Wallet Integration

The Reserve Bank of India has permitted banks to integrate digital rupee wallets directly into their primary mobile banking applications, aiming to improve accessibility and encourage wider adoption of the central bank digital currency. The change removes the need for users to download separate apps, leveraging the high visibility of existing banking platforms to lift transaction volumes that have remained muted compared with physical currency circulation. Experts caution that consumer discovery inside crowded banking apps may lag third-party payment platforms, but the initiative is expected to streamline funding and usage by aligning CBDC functionality with familiar digital payment ecosystems.

## Canada Stablecoin Issuance Amendments

The Canadian government proposed amendments to the Bank Act, Trust and Loan Companies Act, and Insurance Companies Act to prohibit federally regulated financial institutions from directly issuing stablecoins. Stablecoin issuance would be restricted to non-financial subsidiaries regulated under the Stablecoin Act, which received royal assent in March 2026 and is scheduled to take full effect in 2027. The framework also aims to cover foreign financial institutions not subject to domestic prudential supervision and strengthen consumer protections if an issuer or custodian becomes insolvent.

## Thailand Stablecoin Transaction Rules

Thailand’s Securities and Exchange Commission proposed new rules for stablecoin transactions, including a daily transfer limit of 5 million Thai baht per customer at each licensed digital asset operator. The draft regulations, open for public comment until November 26, aim to combat money laundering and cybercrime through wallet ownership verification, Travel Rule compliance, and stricter transparency requirements for exchanges and brokers. The proposals also prohibit brokers from using liquidity providers for stablecoin-to-baht pairs and set minimum transaction thresholds for off-platform trades.

## Systemic Risk Warning from FSB Chair

Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey, speaking as Financial Stability Board chair, warned that private credit and stablecoins can pose systemic risks earlier than traditional size metrics imply because of their interconnectedness with the broader financial system. He cited current market sizes of about $1.8 trillion for private credit and $300 billion for stablecoins, noting that transmission channels and confidence shifts can propagate stress before conventional indicators trigger alarms. Bailey urged global policymakers to enhance surveillance, improve data transparency, and prioritize international cooperation to address leverage, liquidity, and operational dependencies.

## Pakistan Freelancer Stablecoin Adoption

Industry experts say stablecoins could give Pakistani freelancers a lower-cost channel for collecting overseas payments and holding foreign-currency earnings. The use case is supported by global data showing stablecoins account for an average of 35% of earnings for freelancers paid in cryptocurrency. In Pakistan, where nearly three million freelancers generate about $1.7 billion annually, adoption of dollar-linked digital currencies remains contingent on legal permission and stronger regulatory safeguards, highlighting the tension between remittance efficiency and financial-sector oversight and investor-protection concerns.

## Goldman Sachs Project Faro

Goldman Sachs announced Project Faro, a USD-pegged stablecoin developed with Citigroup and other major banks to serve institutional and retail users globally. The initiative, alongside recent fixed-income bond issuances, underscores the firm’s strategic expansion into digital asset infrastructure and capital-markets plumbing. Analysts view these moves as reinforcing Goldman’s central role in financial technology, while treating them as minor catalysts that do not significantly alter the firm’s long-term earnings narrative. The project signals growing bank-led competition with incumbent stablecoin issuers and potential monetization of settlement rails.

## OpenAI ChatGPT Wallet Launch

OpenAI launched the ChatGPT Wallet, enabling its AI agents to complete purchases using tokenized card details from American Express, Mastercard, and Visa without involving cryptocurrency. The consumer-focused tool requires user approval for each transaction and integrates with existing merchant checkouts, prioritizing convenience and compatibility with mainstream payment rails. It is positioned against crypto-based alternatives such as Coinbase’s Agentic Wallets and Circle’s Agent Stack, which use stablecoins like USDC for automated, low-value payments. The approach shows agentic commerce is expanding beyond blockchain-native settlement.

## Circle USDC Consumer Utility Strategy

Circle CPTO Nikhil Chandhok argued that USDC should be positioned as a consumer utility focused on seamless accessibility for everyday users, rather than as a complex financial instrument. He emphasized that adoption depends on intuitive design and network utility, targeting use cases such as remittances and dollar access in emerging markets where blockchain complexity must remain invisible. The strategy aims to compete with Tether through superior user experience and regulatory compliance, especially under frameworks like MiCA, while exploring programmability for future agentic AI payments.

## DeFi Participation and RWA Concentration

A crypto analyst reported that stablecoin participation in decentralized finance declined to 10.8% from a previous peak of 15%, indicating a reduced role for stablecoins in the sector. The analysis also noted that most tokenized real-world assets rarely enter DeFi protocols. Stablecoins and RWAs on Ethereum account for 53.2% of total tracked asset value across networks, representing $185.8 billion out of $349.2 billion, underscoring Ethereum’s concentration while suggesting broader DeFi activity remains narrow and capital efficiency and participation depth are limited.

## Arbitrum Stablecoin Inflows

Arbitrum, an Ethereum layer-2 network, has seen a notable rise in stablecoin inflows, underscoring the critical role of stablecoins in decentralized finance applications. An earlier 24-hour snapshot recorded a $43.6 million increase in the network’s stablecoin market capitalization, a metric distinct from total inflows. The growth highlights continued demand for scalable dollar-denominated settlement assets on layer-2 networks, even as broader stablecoin supply contracts, and suggests liquidity is rotating toward chains offering lower fees and faster execution for DeFi users and composability.

## Aptos Stablecoin and RWA Growth

Stablecoin supply on the Aptos blockchain rose by more than 17% to approximately $779 million in the past week, diverging from the broader market, which contracted 1.6% to roughly $286 billion. USDT accounts for about half of this supply, while the network also hosts significant tokenized real-world assets, including BlackRock’s BUIDL fund valued at $157.37 million. Despite the recent growth, total supply remains below previous highs, DeFi activity metrics stay modest, and the Aptos token is trading near its all-time low.

## Overall Outlook

Stablecoin contraction is the dominant near-term signal, tying Bitcoin’s weakness to liquidity loss, ETF outflows, and tokenized treasury shrinkage. Regulatory pilots, bank-led stablecoins, and consumer-facing wallets show continued institutional adoption, while systemic-risk warnings and exploits reinforce the need for tighter oversight. A durable recovery likely requires stablecoin supply to stabilize and DeFi participation to broaden.
