## Stablecoin Market Contraction

Stablecoin market capitalization has shrunk by about $12 billion in 30 days, its fastest decline since the 2022 Terra-Luna collapse, as traders take profits amid Bitcoin’s four-month low. Analysts say roughly 90% of the loss is concentrated in Tether’s USDT and Circle’s USDC, whose balances on exchanges have fallen sharply. Capital appears to be leaving the crypto sector entirely, not rotating between tokens, with contraction concentrated on Ethereum-based trading venues. Last week alone, the market lost $4.15 billion, even as decentralized exchange spot volumes rose 9% and perpetual futures volumes increased 14.67%, underscoring that higher trading activity is not translating into stablecoin demand.

Solana’s total stablecoin market capitalization contracted by about $919 million over 30 days, falling to $13.958 billion. The decline was driven mainly by sharp reductions in USDT and several other stablecoins, which outweighed a 6.3% increase in Circle’s USDC. USDC now represents 52.4% of the network’s tracked stablecoin market, cushioning the aggregate contraction. The data highlights an uneven chain-level shift: specific assets expanded while overall value fell. This mirrors the broader market pattern of selective flows, where users rotate within a network, exit to fiat, or reduce exposure across venues, showing that stablecoin demand on Solana is increasingly concentrated around the largest issuer.

## Brazil Stablecoin Regulation

A Central Bank of Brazil official warned that converting reais into stablecoins for cross-border payments, known as “stablecoin sandwiching,” may constitute money laundering because it circumvents foreign-exchange reporting. The statement reinforces Resolution 561, effective October 2026, which bars virtual assets from serving as settlement instruments in international payments and requires settlement through foreign-exchange operations or non-resident real accounts. Regulators have also finalized 24-hour holds on transfers of $10,000 or more to limit fraud, while a proposed 3.5% tax on cross-border stablecoin transactions remains under consideration. The central bank’s FX department head said legally obtained funds can be treated as laundered if used to buy stablecoins. Brazil’s tokenization association argued the practice is not inherently illegal and warned broad classifications could harm legitimate business.

## Russia Rouble Stablecoins

Sberbank executive Alexander Vedyakhin expects rouble stablecoins issued in Russia to gain significant market share in cross-border trade next year, after a new legal framework permits issuance as digital financial assets. The development responds to the market’s need for efficient settlement tools under Western sanctions, which previously pushed Russian companies toward foreign-issued stablecoins such as A7A5. Sberbank is working with the central bank to establish secure infrastructure, stressing robust safeguards against cyberattacks and social-engineering attacks. The initiative positions rouble-denominated stablecoins as a sanctioned-trade settlement option, reducing reliance on non-domestic tokens while creating new regulatory, technical and privacy challenges for banks, payment operators and cross-border counterparties.

## Taiwan Stablecoin Rules

Taiwan’s Financial Supervisory Commission has proposed draft regulations under the Virtual Asset Service Act that prioritize banks and bank-led ventures for stablecoin issuance, requiring non-bank applicants to meet a NT$300 million capital threshold. The framework mandates full reserve backing at fair value, with assets restricted to low-risk instruments such as government bonds and bank deposits. Issuers must also satisfy strict customer identification and anti-money laundering compliance. Public consultations on licensing and management rules run through December 16, with supporting regulations expected as early as the first quarter of 2027. The proposals aim to bring stablecoin issuance under prudential oversight while limiting systemic risk from poorly collateralized tokens.

## Project Simbolon

The Monetary Authority of Singapore and Deutsche Bundesbank successfully tested Project Simbolon, a cross-border EUR/SGD settlement proof of concept using wholesale central bank digital currencies. The trial coordinated payment-versus-payment settlement across independent central bank ledgers with zero-knowledge proofs, avoiding direct technical integration and reducing one-sided delivery risk. Deutsche Bank joined the initiative, expanding the pilot to test interoperability between CBDC settlement platforms involving major financial institutions. Authorities emphasized the experiment is a controlled test, not a live corridor, and that legal finality, governance of relayers, operational requirements and faster proof generation must be resolved before commercial deployment. The project illustrates how tokenized ledgers can improve FX liquidity management while preserving national ledger sovereignty.

## HKMA-Swift Tokenized Deposits

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority and Swift have expanded collaboration to test tokenized deposits for cross-border transactions, building on the Project Ensemble Sandbox. Major banks, including Bank of China (Hong Kong), Standard Chartered and HSBC, are exploring interoperability between Swift’s global ledger and the settlement layer of Project Ensemble. The pilot aims to bridge international and local payment systems, enabling real-time, programmable cross-border payments across multiple currencies. Recent Project Ensemble upgrades already allowed 24/7 settlement of tokenized deposits using tokenized central bank money. The initiative is intended to show how separate payment systems can operate together, improving efficiency for institutions that need both Swift connectivity and on-chain settlement rails.

## DBS-Kinexys USD Tokenized Deposits

DBS and J.P. Morgan’s Kinexys have launched a commercially available interoperability solution for 24/7 cross-border transfers of tokenized USD deposits between permissioned blockchain environments in the United States and Singapore. The service connects DBS Token Services with Kinexys’ account-based permissioned ecosystem, allowing institutional clients to deploy on-chain USD liquidity for cross-border operations. Ant International is the first client, using the rail for treasury and liquidity management outside traditional banking hours. The launch advances Project BLOOM, led by the Monetary Authority of Singapore, by demonstrating live use cases for interoperability between distinct bank-operated tokenized money ecosystems and meeting growing demand for efficient Asian cross-border payments.

## Circle Earnings and Arc

Circle reported strong third-quarter earnings and revenue results, beating Wall Street expectations. Despite the positive financial performance, the company’s stock has declined significantly because of slowing growth from lower reserve returns, higher distribution costs and regulatory uncertainty in the stablecoin market. Circle is expanding its ecosystem by launching Arc, a blockchain designed for stablecoin transactions and tokenized real-world assets. The company is positioning itself for future growth as financial institutions increasingly adopt stablecoin payments, seeking to offset pressure on reserve income by broadening product reach, improving settlement infrastructure and deepening relationships with institutional users.

## Finteum FX Settlement

DLT-based FX platform Finteum has surpassed $1 billion in daily intraday FX swap volume and secured investment from Circle to explore stablecoin settlement using USDC and EURC. Finteum is introducing timed overnight FX swaps to reduce settlement risk and aims to support Payment-versus-Payment settlement for CBDCs, tokenized deposits and other stablecoins. The initiative aligns with G20 and CPMI objectives to lower FX settlement risk, while Finteum plans to integrate Circle’s Arc blockchain alongside existing multi-chain solutions. The expansion shows how institutional FX infrastructure is converging with stablecoin rails, creating liquidity channels that can settle currency trades atomically and potentially reduce counterparty exposure.

## HKEX e-HKD Margin

Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing will launch a service allowing clearing participants to use the digital Hong Kong dollar, e-HKD, for after-hours margin deposits in derivatives trading starting December 14, 2026. The initiative is supported by Bank of China (Hong Kong), HSBC and Standard Chartered Bank. It enables participants to fund positions outside conventional banking hours, improving collateral timing and flexibility for managing overnight exposures. The CBDC will be treated as cash collateral, with settlement banks handling digital transfers on behalf of participants. The rollout marks a practical institutional application of wholesale central bank digital currency, linking market infrastructure, collateral management and tokenized money.

## Monument Bank Tokenized Retail Deposits

Monument Bank has launched tokenized retail deposits on the Midnight public blockchain, offering clients 12-month fixed-term savings products in pounds sterling with an AER of 5.20%. The product is fully protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme and operates within the bank’s existing regulatory framework, ensuring client personal data remains private and is not exposed to cryptocurrency price volatility. The launch marks the first time a regulated UK bank has used a public blockchain to offer tokenized deposits. Future phases plan to include tokenized investments in real-world assets, extending the model beyond deposits while preserving consumer protections.

## Honduras CBDC Research

Cosmos Labs and Peersyst Technology signed a memorandum of understanding with the Central Bank of Honduras to conduct academic and technical research into a potential central bank digital currency, instant payments and cross-border transfers. The collaboration includes workshops, use-case development and demonstrations using Cosmos blockchain technology and the Inter-Blockchain Communication protocol. No live pilots have been announced, and the bank has not committed to issuing a CBDC. The work builds on prior public consultations about payment-system upgrades, stablecoin regulation and financial inclusion, documented in the central bank’s 2025 annual report. It complements broader Latin American efforts to study interoperability between digital central bank money and tokenized assets.

## Luno Nigeria Africa Payments

Luno Nigeria CEO Ayotunde Alabi said stablecoins can improve the speed and cost-efficiency of cross-border payments in Africa, but cannot fix fragmented financial systems without stronger regulatory cooperation and integration among national payment networks. He noted that intra-African payment costs exceed the global average because of weak currency interoperability and settlement delays. Alabi advocated shared settlement frameworks modeled after the euro to improve liquidity and capital movement. Luno is expanding institutional infrastructure through Crypto as a Service, stablecoin issuance via BlockTower and the Global Settlement Network, GTXN, to support cross-border transactions across jurisdictions. The comments highlight the gap between tokenized rails and the policy coordination needed for pan-African settlement.

## Toss Bank Solana Remittance

Toss Bank completed the first phase of a proof of concept for cross-border remittances using dollar-pegged stablecoins, such as USDC, on the Solana blockchain. The test demonstrated that transfers could be completed in tens of seconds, far faster than the one-to-three days typically required by the traditional SWIFT network. The bank also verified anti-money laundering safeguards, including OFAC-sanctioned address blocking, and plans to align the service with South Korea’s emerging stablecoin legislation. The pilot shows how regulated banks are evaluating stablecoin rails for remittance efficiency, subject to sanctions screening and forthcoming domestic rules.

## Wholesale CBDC Infrastructure

Wholesale central bank digital currencies are emerging as critical infrastructure for tokenized finance, providing a trusted settlement layer that enables atomic settlement of tokenized assets such as bonds and collateral. Recent initiatives, including the Eurosystem’s Pontes system and the Bank for International Settlements’ Project Agorá, show how tokenized central bank reserves and commercial bank deposits can operate on shared platforms to maintain monetary stability and interoperability. The primary focus is shifting from consumer-facing retail CBDCs toward resolving architectural challenges in the settlement layer, ensuring scalable systems as financial assets move onto distributed ledgers. The trend reflects a pragmatic approach to digital money, prioritizing institutional plumbing over broad retail rollout.

## Cambodia Initiatives

The National Bank of Cambodia is advancing multiple initiatives spanning government bond tokenization, a yen-denominated stablecoin remittance system and cross-border payments with Rwanda. The programmes combine sovereign debt digitization, stablecoin-based remittance infrastructure and regional payment connectivity, reflecting an effort to modernize settlement channels while addressing cross-border frictions. The yen-denominated stablecoin project is notable for linking remittance flows to a major international currency, while the Rwanda connection tests interoperability across neighboring jurisdictions. The initiatives remain at varying stages of development, and the central bank has not announced full production deployment for all components. Together, they illustrate Cambodia’s use of tokenization and stablecoin rails to improve access, efficiency and regional integration.

## India e-Rupee Wallets

The Reserve Bank of India is encouraging banks to integrate e-rupee wallets into main banking applications to boost adoption of the digital rupee, as circulation figures remain low. The move aims to make the CBDC easier to access for retail users by embedding it within familiar mobile banking interfaces, reducing friction around downloads, onboarding and everyday payments. Low circulation has highlighted the challenge of translating central bank money projects into broad usage, especially when cash and UPI remain entrenched. By pushing integration into mainstream apps, the RBI seeks to strengthen the digital rupee’s role in payments, improve visibility and test whether user experience improvements can drive meaningful transaction growth.

## SDEV Stablecoin Development

Stablecoin Development Corporation, formerly NovaBay Pharmaceuticals, is down 8.04% after analyst downgrades and weak relative price strength, trading at $0.755 as of November 16, 2026. The company, which focuses on accumulating and deploying the SKY protocol token for blockchain-based services, has fallen 25.28% over the month, reflecting significant volatility and negative trailing earnings. Despite AAII’s “Deep Value” grade, the stock exhibits very weak momentum, with a four-quarter relative price strength of -34.00%, indicating underperformance against the broader market. The decline underscores the speculative gap between companies pursuing stablecoin-linked strategies and the fundamentals of the underlying protocol token.

## Overall Outlook

Stablecoin liquidity is shrinking as regulatory scrutiny rises, while central banks and banks are building tokenized settlement rails for FX, remittances and collateral. Adoption is likely to advance first in institutional wholesale settings, with retail and cross-border stablecoin use dependent on legal clarity, interoperability standards and sanctions compliance. Market conditions suggest continued volatility in stablecoin flows and tokenized-deposit pilots.
