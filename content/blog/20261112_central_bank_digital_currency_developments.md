## Central Bank Digital Currency Developments

Central bank digital currencies remain a global policy priority, with 146 countries reported to be developing them. Fully launched retail CBDCs remain rare, limited to the Bahamas, Jamaica and Nigeria, while the digital euro is scheduled for potential issuance in 2029. The technology supports retail payments for citizens and wholesale applications such as cross-border settlement, promising faster and cheaper transfers. Yet widespread adoption has been slowed by privacy concerns, design trade-offs, interoperability questions and the need to balance innovation against monetary-policy stability. These tensions continue to shape pilots, legislation and public trust in state-backed digital money.

European Central Bank Executive Board member Piero Cipollone warned that legislative delays in the EU could postpone the digital euro’s targeted 2029 launch. He urged negotiators to reach a deal by year-end, cautioning that a later introduction would deepen Europe’s dependence on foreign payment providers and weaken the competitiveness of European companies. The ECB plans a 12-month pilot beginning in late 2027, contingent on adoption of the necessary regulation within the Single Currency Package. The warning underscores the political and technical urgency of completing the legal framework before the planned issuance window, as policymakers seek a sovereign digital payment option amid rising foreign competition.

Commercial Bank of Dubai successfully executed end-to-end wholesale central bank digital currency transfers with the Bank of China through the Central Bank of the United Arab Emirates’ Jisr platform. The transaction marks a significant advance in cross-border payment capability between the UAE and China, demonstrating how tokenized settlement can move value across jurisdictions with reduced intermediaries. The pilot highlights growing momentum for wholesale CBDC corridors that link major financial centers. By testing real-world transfer flows, the initiative supports efforts to improve cross-border efficiency, lower settlement risk and reduce reliance on legacy correspondent-banking arrangements, while also raising governance and privacy questions for participating institutions.

Analyst Humberto Andrade examined the relationship between instant payment systems and central bank digital currencies, arguing that fast domestic payment rails reduce dependence on foreign card networks but do not eliminate reliance on foreign stablecoins or dollarized settlement. He framed CBDCs as a strategic choice between tokenized commercial bank deposits and retail CBDC issuance. A retail CBDC path, he said, requires deliberate policy measures to prevent commercial bank disintermediation and protect privacy. The analysis positions CBDCs not merely as payment speed upgrades, but as instruments of monetary sovereignty, shaping how economies balance efficiency, privacy, bank funding stability and reduced foreign settlement dependencies.

## India’s CBDC Rollout and Public Payments

Indian Overseas Bank launched the IOB Digital Rupee application, enabling customers to conduct peer-to-peer and peer-to-merchant transactions using India’s central bank digital currency. The app supports UPI interoperability and integrates programmable CBDC functionality to facilitate purpose-specific disbursements of government grants and direct benefit transfers. The initiative marks a significant step in the bank’s digital transformation, offering a secure and innovative payment option alongside existing digital ecosystems. By embedding programmability into retail CBDC use, the bank aims to improve targeted public spending, reduce leakage and expand practical acceptance of the digital rupee among everyday users and merchants.

Delhi government officials reported that 94% of applications for the Lakshmi Yojana remain unscreened because of delays in establishing Project Management Units, with only a small fraction of the 751,961 received applications processed so far. The scheme’s initial central bank digital currency payment mechanism encountered technical problems, including wallet activation and fund-access failures. To maintain benefit delivery, the government temporarily distributed the full ₹2,500 monthly amount through fixed deposits until January 2027. Authorities acknowledged that the CBDC-based arrangement needs stronger technical and operational processes, coordinated with banks, the Reserve Bank of India and the National Payments Corporation of India.

## U.S. Political Leadership and Digital-Asset Policy

Pro-crypto U.S. Representative Tom Emmer was elected vice chair of the House Republican minority caucus, securing the party’s second-highest leadership position ahead of the anticipated Democratic takeover of Congress. Emmer, a leading advocate for digital assets, has championed legislation such as the CLARITY Act and co-founded the bipartisan Congressional Crypto Group to advance stablecoin and market-structure bills. His leadership role includes coordinating legislative strategy, while his policy work emphasizes opposing government-controlled central bank digital currencies and clarifying regulatory definitions for blockchain infrastructure providers. His elevation places a pro-digital-asset voice in a senior Republican leadership position at a critical moment for U.S. crypto policy.

## Stablecoin Liquidity and Market Conditions

Total stablecoin supply contracted by $14.2 billion over the 30 days through November 9, falling just under $280 billion and marking the sharpest decline since June 2022. Tether’s USDT led with a $6.6 billion drop over 60 days, while gold-backed tokens stayed relatively stable. The drain, reflecting capital exiting crypto, coincided with $1.6 billion in net U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF redemptions and loss-taking selling, leaving Bitcoin near $73,000 and below short-term holder cost bases. Market analytics providers said leverage had been flushed out, highlighting growing regulatory and competitive pressures from institutions such as Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and the Federal Reserve. Amid the contraction, major banks backed a $270 million initiative to compete with stablecoin issuers, while the supply drain delays the return of new money needed for a sustained rally.

## Ethereum Stablecoin Share and Institutional Infrastructure

Ethereum’s stablecoin market share dropped to a historic low of 46% as $32.5 billion in supply migrated to lower-fee competitors such as Tron and Solana, signaling a structural shift in liquidity preferences. The decline coincided with Ethereum’s price falling to $2.06K amid intensified selling pressure driven by hotter-than-expected U.S. inflation data and fears of aggressive Federal Reserve rate hikes. Despite bearish conditions, institutional infrastructure is expanding: Standard Chartered launched prime brokerage services for Ether, and Deutsche Bank initiated custody services supporting stablecoins such as USDC under European regulatory frameworks. The episode shows market stress coexisting with continued institutional build-out.

## Bank-Led Stablecoin Infrastructure

A consortium of 21 major global financial institutions, including Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Santander, Deutsche Bank and UBS, committed approximately $270 million to fund Project Faro, a joint venture developing a U.S. dollar-backed stablecoin. Ten global systemically important banks are among the backers, covering operating costs for the first five years. The venture targets launch in the first half of 2027, plans to expand into euro-denominated tokens and other G7 currencies, and aims to comply with the U.S. GENIUS Act and EU MiCA regulations. It seeks to compete with Tether, Circle, JPMorgan’s JPM Coin and the Qivalis euro consortium by creating an interoperability layer that bridges tokenized deposit networks, functioning as a neutral transit medium for cross-border and intra-network payments, distinct from consumer-focused stablecoins such as USDC.

PNC Bank launched a pilot service allowing eligible corporate clients to receive USDC stablecoin payments via the Ethereum blockchain, automatically converting them 1:1 into U.S. dollars. The initiative expands PNC’s digital capabilities beyond cryptocurrency trading by leveraging Coinbase infrastructure to enhance cross-border payment efficiency. It strengthens the bank’s Treasury Management franchise and positions PNC to capitalize on growing demand for blockchain-based financial solutions. By combining stablecoin receipt with instant dollar conversion, PNC offers corporations a way to use digital-dollar rails while reducing direct exposure to token volatility and streamlining reconciliation for international settlement.

Fypher launched FYUSD, a U.S. dollar-backed stablecoin issued by BitGo Bank and Trust under the GENIUS Act framework, targeting institutional users in Asia with an initial $10 million vault deployment. The token functions primarily as a settlement instrument, while yield is generated through opt-in on-chain strategies provided by Concrete, a protocol developed by Blueprint Finance. Ether.fi serves as the primary depositor for the pilot program. The launch marks a strategic shift toward active capital allocation within Fypher’s broader on-chain financial platform, combining regulated issuance, yield optimization and institutional settlement rails for Asian market participants.

Samsung Wallet partnered with Coinflow and Bastion to enable U.S. Galaxy device users to convert stablecoin holdings into dollars and withdraw them to bank accounts or debit cards. The integration allows eligible users to move funds from Samsung Wallet through payment rails such as ACH, push-to-card and real-time payments, following the platform’s October 2026 launch of its stablecoin feature. Bastion serves as the licensed custodian for the stablecoins, while Coinflow manages conversion and routing of funds through its “Network of Networks” infrastructure. The partnership brings stablecoin off-ramps directly into a mainstream consumer device, linking digital assets to familiar banking and card payment channels.

Visa added the Monad blockchain to its stablecoin settlement program, giving participating issuers and acquirers an additional multi-chain option for moving funds outside traditional banking hours. The expansion follows Visa’s report of a $20 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate, representing more than 15 times year-over-year growth. Concurrently, the Monad Foundation announced plans to launch a Private Settlement feature enabling institutions to maintain transaction confidentiality while retaining access for auditors and regulators. The development shows stablecoin settlement moving deeper into mainstream payment infrastructure, with privacy-preserving rails designed to meet institutional compliance requirements and support high-volume merchant settlement.

## Institutional Digital Asset Services

Deutsche Bank launched a regulated digital-asset custody service for corporate and institutional clients in Europe, supporting secure storage and transfer of Bitcoin, Ether, USDC, EURC and the euro-based token EURAU. The service uses multi-layer key-management controls and excludes retail investors, targeting corporate and investment-bank clients across Europe. The bank also signed a memorandum of understanding with Deutsche Asset Management to explore tokenized financial products, including money-market funds, crypto-asset exchange-traded commodities and stablecoins. Future plans include expansion into Asian markets in 2027 and services for tokenized deposits, stablecoin conversions and digital bond issuance, positioning Deutsche Bank as an integrated institutional digital-asset provider for custody, settlement and liquidity management.

## Consumer Adoption, Financial Stability and Regulation

The European Central Bank’s Consumer Expectations Survey revealed that stablecoin ownership among euro-area consumers remains limited, with 5% of consumers holding them as of August 2025. U.S. dollar-denominated tokens such as USDT and USDC dominate these holdings, which are concentrated among crypto investors and skew toward smaller amounts, although a minority holds more than €10,000. The report warns that current risks to financial stability are low, but broader adoption—especially for cross-border payments—could introduce vulnerabilities related to de-pegging events, reserve-asset liquidations and currency substitution. The findings highlight the euro area’s cautious stance toward dollar-linked digital money.

The Financial Stability Board urged Turkey to strengthen its oversight of crypto markets and establish dedicated rules for stablecoins. While acknowledging Turkey’s progress in introducing licensing requirements for crypto service providers, the board highlighted the absence of a specific stablecoin framework and recommended improved monitoring of financial-stability risks. It also called for enhanced supervision, enforcement and international cooperation with foreign regulators to address existing gaps. The guidance reflects growing concern that rapid crypto adoption, especially dollar-pegged tokens, can create supervisory blind spots, currency-substitution pressures and cross-border spillovers that require coordinated responses.

The collapse of Paraguay’s Ueno Bank, which affected nearly 60% of the country’s adults, triggered a political crisis and prompted many citizens to turn to dollar-pegged stablecoins such as USDt to preserve liquidity and hedge against the devaluing local currency. Tether executives promoted stablecoins as a lifeline, but analysts said the shift reflected immediate payment needs during bank resolution rather than a permanent mass migration from traditional banking. Experts emphasized that stablecoins can provide an alternative source of liquidity during systemic failures, yet they lack the deposit guarantees and regulatory protections inherent in conventional banks. The episode illustrates stablecoins’ role as emergency liquidity infrastructure, not a full substitute for regulated banking.

## Outlook

Overall, digital-currency developments show bifurcation: CBDC pilots and bank-led stablecoin infrastructure are advancing, while retail stablecoin liquidity contracts and adoption faces privacy, operational and regulatory constraints. Government CBDC use is expanding in India and cross-border corridors, but technical failures and legislative delays expose implementation risks. Stablecoins are becoming embedded in treasury, settlement and consumer off-ramps, increasing institutional competition and regulatory scrutiny. The outlook depends on liquidity recovery, clearer legal frameworks, successful pilots and the ability of banks, regulators and technology providers to balance innovation with financial stability.
