## Stablecoin Adoption and Market Effects

An IMF working paper shows emerging economies account for about 75 percent of all stablecoins, and nearly 99 percent are dollar-denominated, underscoring digital dollarization driven by savers seeking refuge from local currency instability and capital controls. The trend contrasts with China’s e-CNY, which has recorded strong domestic transaction volumes but limited international demand, reflected in the renminbi’s small share of global reserves and cross-border payments. The spread of dollar-linked stablecoins in developing markets reinforces U.S. monetary dominance through decentralized user choices, while China’s state-led digital currency projects have not achieved comparable global reach.

A study covering 28 economies from 2021 to 2024 found that higher exposure to digital money, including stablecoins, is associated with lower exchange-rate volatility against the U.S. dollar. Stablecoins can improve payment efficiency and serve as bridges for international transactions, the research notes, but they do not inherently strengthen domestic currencies or guarantee purchasing-power stability. The findings distinguish temporary payment usage from lasting dollarization, warning that the latter may weaken local monetary policy transmission even when currency fluctuations decline in practice.

Portfolio managers are being urged to assess how dollar-backed stablecoins, central bank digital currencies, and tokenized deposits reshape liquidity, settlement, and short-term Treasury demand. Stablecoins can influence funding markets and Treasury purchases, while their credit risk and governance differ materially from state-issued CBDCs. The analysis notes that tokenized assets may speed settlement, but faster rails do not eliminate counterparty, operational, or market risk. Managers should monitor reserve quality, issuer transparency, regulatory changes, and the interaction between private digital money and central bank systems as adoption expands across global finance.

## Regulatory and Policy Developments

Nigerian banks, asset managers, and fintech firms are weighing stablecoin services for cross-border payments and business transactions, supported by infrastructure providers such as Quidax that focus on compliance and regulatory readiness. The push reflects expanding digital-asset adoption, yet dollar-pegged stablecoins raise concerns for monetary policy, financial stability, and anti-money-laundering supervision. Nigeria’s Securities and Exchange Commission and the International Monetary Fund have stressed stricter oversight, stronger investor protections, and clearer rules as institutions enter the market. The debate centers on balancing innovation with safeguards against digital dollarization and illicit finance risks.

Bank of Ghana is developing a Stablecoin Directive to regulate digital asset activity and ensure innovation supports monetary stability and the Ghana cedi. Governor Dr. Johnson Pandit Asiama said the framework, created with the Securities and Exchange Commission and Financial Intelligence Centre, aims to mitigate risks from foreign-denominated stablecoins while strengthening consumer protection and safe integration with traditional finance. The central bank seeks to balance regulation and innovation, using tokenization to improve cross-border payments, trade settlement, and access to capital for small businesses.

Thailand’s central bank is preparing regulations to place stablecoins, Buy Now Pay Later services, and large gold purchases under closer supervision, with implementation planned between November 2026 and March 2027. The expanded framework includes stricter oversight of non-bank lenders and payment firms, building on recent measures that reduced cash withdrawals by more than 57 percent. Bank of Thailand officials say the steps aim to address household debt, improve funding for small and medium-sized enterprises, and combat illicit finance within a broader financial stability agenda.

Canada’s Stablecoin Act, enacted to govern government-backed stablecoins as payment instruments, is accelerating work by major banks on shared infrastructure for tokenized deposits. The law creates momentum toward institutional digital money, but the sector faces persistent friction from fragmented provincial rules and the absence of standardized national requirements. Compliance complexity is raising costs and slowing innovation relative to international peers. Coinbase Canada’s chief executive argues that a unified national framework is needed to retain talent, reduce regulatory uncertainty, and align Canadian tokenized-deposit development with global standards.

The Singapore FinTech Festival 2026 will host dedicated sessions on stablecoins, real-world asset tokenization, and programmable money, reflecting the industry’s move toward regulated digital settlement rails. On November 18, regulators from Europe, Thailand, and Japan are scheduled to debate stablecoin frameworks and the supervisory implications of tokenizing assets. The following day, executives from HSBC, Paxos, and Partior will examine which forms of money, including tokenized deposits, should occupy layers of the financial stack. The agenda signals growing convergence between central bank initiatives, bank-led tokenization, and public blockchain infrastructure.

## Hong Kong Tokenized Finance

Hong Kong’s monetary regulator has expanded the EnsembleTX pilot under Project Ensemble to support 24/7 interbank settlement of tokenized central bank money and tokenized deposits, replacing time-limited Real-Time Gross Settlement for participating institutions. The initiative now includes 12 banks and 20 pioneer users, enabling mirrored interbank payments, continuous corporate treasury operations, and transactions with tokenized money market funds. It has completed secondary-market trials of tokenized Exchange Fund Bills. Regulators plan additional tests involving regulated stablecoins, whose issuers must meet strict standards under the new Stablecoins Ordinance, to assess interoperability, redemption, and settlement efficiency in always-on bond trading and repo environments, alongside broader initiatives on AI governance and cross-border payment connectivity.

Standard Chartered has launched 24/7 tokenized deposit settlement services for corporate clients in Hong Kong, operating independently of Ripple’s XRP bridge asset. The services use digital representations of bank deposits to improve settlement efficiency and transparency. The debut follows HSBC’s completion of the first international tokenized deposit transfer via SWIFT, showing banks using blockchain rails that can remove neutral bridge currencies in domestic and netted cross-border transactions. As bank-issued digital tokens and stablecoins gain traction, they are increasingly competing with Ripple’s RLUSD stablecoin, potentially reducing structural demand for XRP as a settlement tool. The services are positioned as viable instruments for corporate treasury operations, underscoring competition between private bank tokens and bridge-asset settlement models.

HSBC’s Hong Kong dollar-backed RedCoin stablecoin, one of the first regulated Hong Kong dollar stablecoins, attracted 50,000 users in its first week via the PayMe app, reaching HK$87.75 million in circulation with full cash reserves as of November 2. The token enables peer-to-peer transfers and merchant payments on a private blockchain with 24/7 settlement capabilities, with plans to expand access to mobile banking and introduce tokenized investment subscriptions. Bank CEO Georges Elhedery attributed rapid retail adoption to Hong Kong’s supportive regulatory environment and outlined ambitions to scale the token to millions of users.

Anchorpoint Financial, a Standard Chartered-backed entity holding Hong Kong’s FRS01 stablecoin issuer license, has partnered with the Solana Foundation to expand its Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, HKDAP, onto the Solana blockchain. The agreement enables native issuance and redemption of HKDAP on-chain to support retail payments and settlement of tokenized assets. It marks Anchorpoint’s first collaboration with a public blockchain foundation and follows a controlled institutional rollout. The partnership aims to use Solana’s high-speed settlement capabilities to broaden access to regulated digital money in Hong Kong.

## Payments, Partnerships, and Consumer Products

OKX has introduced a Mastercard-powered virtual stablecoin spending card for eligible U.S. customers, enabling fee-free purchases directly from USDC, USDT, and USDG balances without fiat conversion. Issued by WebBank, the card offers zero transaction and foreign-exchange fees, cashback rates of up to 10 percent, and yield opportunities on USDG holdings subject to tier requirements and spending caps. It is available in most U.S. states, excluding New York, Massachusetts, and several territories, and integrates with Apple Pay and Google Pay for contactless payments.

SBI Holdings and Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group have signed a capital and business alliance to explore financial services using the yen-denominated stablecoin JPYSC. Sumitomo Mitsui will acquire a 5 percent stake in SBI Startale Marketing, the joint venture supporting the initiative. The partnership focuses on research and proof-of-concept work for payment and remittance applications, addressing legal, technical, security, and commercial requirements. It combines SBI’s financial network with Startale Group’s blockchain expertise to promote JPYSC adoption and develop on-chain finance opportunities.

HashKey Cloud and Utila have formed a strategic partnership aimed at accelerating stablecoin adoption among institutional investors. The collaboration is intended to strengthen infrastructure and services for firms seeking to integrate digital assets into treasury, trading, and settlement operations. By combining cloud, custody, and institutional trading expertise, the partners plan to reduce operational barriers and expand access to compliant stablecoin-based workflows. The move reflects growing demand from professional investors for enterprise-grade tools that connect regulated digital money with existing financial systems.

## Infrastructure, Security, and Cross-Border Pilots

The International Monetary Fund has recommended embedding automated compliance logic into wholesale central bank digital currency platforms to reduce friction in cross-border payments among Common Monetary Area countries. Its Project Sunbird report argues that programmable controls can help institutions meet sanctions, reporting, and counterparty checks without slowing settlement. The proposal fits a wider push to make tokenized financial infrastructure interoperable, efficient, and supervisable. For regional payment systems, the findings suggest that compliance automation, rather than isolated pilots, may be key to scaling CBDC-linked transfers while preserving policy control and reducing operational bottlenecks.

The XRP Ledger is preparing to deploy the fixCleanup3_4_0 update, which addresses a vulnerability that allowed unauthorized minting of issued tokens, including stablecoins. The flaw did not affect XRP’s native supply; it affected accounts authorized only to burn tokens, which could under certain conditions issue new tokens. The update is supported by 33 of 35 trusted validators and is scheduled for its earliest rollout on November 4, pending the required two-week consensus period. The fix is intended to close an issuance-control gap before it can be exploited further.

XRP Ledger stablecoin supply grew 25.2 percent to $1.03 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with RLUSD accounting for about $1.01 billion of the total. The expansion highlights RLUSD’s dominant market share and rising transfer volumes on the ledger. The period also saw significant growth in tokenized real-world assets, which reached $498.7 million, alongside increased inflows into XRP investment products. Together, the figures point to expanding institutional use of the network for stablecoin settlement, tokenized asset activity, and liquidity.

The European Central Bank has signed service agreements with two groups of network providers to support the network backing for its digital euro pilot project. The selected providers include Nexi Payments and Deutsche Telekom Business Solutions, as well as Telefónica de España, Telefónica Global Solutions, and Indra Soluciones Tecnologías de la Información. The agreements mark a practical step in preparing infrastructure for a potential retail central bank digital currency. They also show the EU’s effort to distribute technical responsibilities across established payments and telecommunications firms while maintaining supervisory control over the pilot.

The People’s Bank of China and the Bank of the Lao PDR have launched the China–Lao PDR Cross-Border Digital Payment Connect, linking China’s retail central bank digital currency, e-CNY, directly to Laos’ domestic payment network. The interoperability model lets Chinese users pay Lao merchants through QR codes with immediate settlement in local kip, using existing infrastructure to reduce friction and preserve monetary sovereignty. The initiative is a notable case study for CBDC integration with conventional rails, emphasizing regulatory oversight, trust-building, and technical connectivity.

North Korean state hackers reportedly stole $6.36 billion from Binance, exploiting Ethereum’s EIP-7702 delegation feature to drain hot wallets in under 20 minutes. Stablecoin issuers including Circle and Tether coordinated to freeze about $3 billion in USDC and USDT within 70 minutes, highlighting the recoverability of tokenized deposits compared with native assets such as Bitcoin and Ether. The remaining $2.4 billion in unfreezable crypto is expected to be laundered, underscoring a structural asymmetry in asset protection between centralized stablecoins and decentralized cryptocurrencies.

## Overall Outlook

Digital currency policy is shifting from experimentation toward regulated, production-scale settlement infrastructure. Stablecoins are deepening dollar usage abroad, tokenized deposits are gaining institutional traction, and CBDC pilots are becoming more interoperable. Security incidents and compliance gaps, however, are likely to drive stricter reserve, licensing, and cross-border rules. Competition among bank tokens, stablecoins, and central bank platforms is expected to intensify.
