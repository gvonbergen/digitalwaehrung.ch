## Institutional Compliance and Regulatory Frameworks

**Standard Chartered Use-Based Compliance**

Standard Chartered is advocating for “Use-Based Compliance” in institutional stablecoin adoption, arguing that controls should match the economic purpose of each transaction rather than the available technology. The bank distinguishes use cases such as cross-border business-to-business payments and digital asset investment, saying institutions can apply risk-contextualized monitoring and governance aligned with Financial Action Task Force and Monetary Authority of Singapore expectations. The approach prioritizes customer intent and real-world risk over blanket surveillance. It is intended to help banks scale stablecoin-enabled services safely while leveraging existing trust frameworks, and it supports regulatory clarity for institutional digital asset payments.

**ABA Comments on GENIUS Act Rules**

The American Bankers Association submitted comments to the Treasury Department urging revisions to proposed rules implementing the GENIUS Act, specifically opposing entry of unregulated stablecoins into the U.S. market via intermediaries. The association recommends defining key terms to prevent legal evasion, holding foreign issuers to standards equivalent to domestic regulated entities, and enhancing interagency coordination to protect bank activities from liability. Its proposals aim to ensure clear regulatory lines, safeguard stablecoin holders during issuer status changes, and prevent competitive distortions. The submission reflects banks’ concerns about systemic risk, operational exposure, and the need for consistent oversight as stablecoins move closer to mainstream payment infrastructure.

**Treasury Stablecoin Rule Scrutiny**

The U.S. Treasury’s proposed rule on payment stablecoins is drawing scrutiny over how it defines issuance and regulates foreign issuers’ access to U.S. customers. Comment letters from banks, technology firms, and regulators show divergent views on preventing offshore stablecoins from reaching American users through intermediaries. Some stakeholders urge stricter controls, while others advocate frameworks that recognize cross-border payment mechanics. Participants are pressing Treasury to clarify issuer liability, protect regulated entities from competitive disadvantages, and distinguish between direct sales and the use of foreign stablecoins as intermediate assets in transactions. The debate will shape how U.S. rules interact with global stablecoin markets and compliance obligations.

**Singapore Foreign Stablecoin Framework**

Singapore’s Monetary Authority has proposed a framework allowing foreign stablecoins into its market under stricter regulatory conditions, including mandatory trace-freeze-burn capabilities and stress testing. The rules enable joint issuance by local and foreign companies and recognize certain overseas-licensed stablecoins, aiming to attract international payment firms while ensuring high compliance standards. Banks may be required to establish separate non-bank entities to issue stablecoins, reflecting the regulator’s focus on mitigating risks associated with digital payment tokens. The proposal positions Singapore as a balanced jurisdiction for stablecoin activity, combining market openness with safeguards for financial stability, consumer protection, and anti-money-laundering controls.

**French Stablecoin Tax Proposal**

French lawmakers postponed the parliamentary vote on a budget proposal containing stablecoin taxation measures to November 3, after the National Assembly narrowly approved an amendment taxing gains from swapping appreciated cryptocurrencies into MiCA-compliant, single-currency stablecoins at 31.4% starting January 1, 2027. The measure applies to French tax-resident individuals and removes the current tax deferral by triggering immediate recognition on swaps into qualifying e-money tokens, even without fiat conversion. Supporters frame it as closing loopholes, while critics cite tax fairness amid cost-of-living protests and transaction-tracking complexity. Lawmakers also rejected a proposal allowing ten-year carryforward of crypto losses, preserving existing same-year offset rules. Further parliamentary votes are required.

## Stablecoin Payments, Adoption, and Market Data

**Asia-Pacific Consumer Stablecoin Adoption**

Allium and FXC Intelligence report Asia-Pacific stablecoin usage is heavily skewed toward consumer-facing transactions, with business-to-consumer payments accounting for 5.3 times their share in traditional cross-border flows. Business-to-business transactions represent a smaller relative portion of stablecoin activity than in conventional payments, yet the region leads globally in this consumer-centric adoption pattern. The data attributes the trend to currency volatility and established crypto trading markets in leading APAC nations, including Taiwan, Turkey, and Indonesia. The findings suggest stablecoins are being adopted first where consumers need fast, low-cost access to dollar-linked assets and payments, shaping regional demand and policy priorities.

**Stablecoin Transaction Volume and Adoption**

Stablecoin transfer volume reached $891 billion in the third quarter of 2026, more than quadrupling from early 2023, with transfers averaging over $400 million per hour and 73% occurring outside standard U.S. banking hours. A study by Coinbase and The Block Pro Research projects quarterly volumes reaching $1.5 trillion by 2027, driven by lower remittance fees and faster settlement for businesses. Seven of the ten largest U.S. banks have tested stablecoins or tokenized deposits, and 30% of mid-market companies are using or piloting them for cross-border activity. Researchers estimate moving 80% of international transactions to stablecoin networks could add $155 billion to global GDP annually, while cautioning adoption may reduce bank lending and reshape the agentic economy.

**Emerging-Market Enterprise Stablecoin Use**

Mid-sized enterprises in emerging markets are increasingly adopting stablecoins for cross-border trade to bypass the high costs, delays, and foreign-exchange risks inherent in traditional correspondent banking systems. By leveraging stablecoin settlement rails, businesses achieve near-instantaneous, 24/7 transaction finality with lower fees and greater transparency compared with legacy wire transfers. The shift is supported by specialized infrastructure platforms that bridge digital-asset rails with local fiat banking, enabling compliant and efficient global commerce. This adoption pattern underscores stablecoins’ role in reducing friction for smaller firms that lack the scale to negotiate favorable correspondent-banking terms, while also raising supervisory questions around FX management and consumer protection.

**Ghana-Asia Trade Settlement Pilot**

Stanbic Bank Ghana and Hong Kong-based RD Technologies are exploring a bank-led cross-border trade settlement solution using stablecoin technology to facilitate business-to-business payments between Ghanaian importers and Asian suppliers. Under the proposed model, Stanbic would manage customer-facing operations and compliance, while RD Technologies would provide digital-asset infrastructure and access to its Asian settlement network. The initiative aims to reduce the time, cost, and complexity associated with legacy payment systems, pending regulatory clearance and technical validation. It illustrates how emerging-market banks and technology providers are partnering to digitize trade finance, targeting corridor-specific settlement efficiency, compliance control, and reduced reliance on correspondent banking chains.

## Product Launches and Infrastructure Expansions

**Mastercard Stablecoin Security**

Mastercard has launched Stablecoin Security, a risk intelligence service combining payment data with blockchain analytics to assess fraud, scams, and sanctions exposure before stablecoin settlements are finalized. Delivered via API, it generates transaction-context risk scores that financial institutions can integrate into existing compliance workflows, tracing fund origins and identifying links to restricted parties. The service initially deploys in North America with partners Borderless.xyz and Keyrails, with global commercial availability planned for 2027 through integration with BVNK, acquired in August 2026. Mastercard has not disclosed performance metrics, and institutions remain responsible for their own anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing decisions.

**Wells Fargo Stablecoin Consortium**

Wells Fargo plans to launch a U.S. dollar-denominated stablecoin in the first half of 2027 as part of a consortium with 20 other financial institutions, aiming to enhance cross-border and institutional payments. The initiative complements the bank’s recently launched tokenized deposit platform, which significantly reduces settlement times for corporate clients, and aligns with broader efforts to expand digital finance capabilities through potential partnerships, including those with Kraken. The strategy offers a long-term growth avenue by improving payment efficiency and international reach, but success depends on regulatory developments and the pace of commercial adoption. It signals major banks’ intent to compete with payment-focused stablecoin issuers while preserving regulated balance-sheet infrastructure.

**JPM Coin Expansion to Ethereum**

J.P. Morgan expanded availability of its USD-denominated deposit token, JPM Coin, to the Ethereum mainnet, marking its second public blockchain after an initial launch on Base. The expansion allows institutional clients to transfer onchain cash alongside existing assets and collateral, leveraging Ethereum’s liquidity to reduce friction in digital asset transactions. It supports programmable treasury functions and reflects the growing integration of tokenized deposits into public, verifiable financial infrastructure. The move enhances interoperability and settlement capabilities for institutional clients, signaling that banks are increasingly willing to anchor regulated deposit tokens on major permissionless networks to improve accessibility and composability.

**tx Launches USTX Stablecoin**

RWA platform tx launched USTX, a dollar stablecoin issued by fintech provider Brale, to serve as the native currency for its wallet and planned tokenized stock marketplace. Unlike synthetic wrappers, USTX is backed by a 1:1 reserve of cash, bank deposits, money market funds, and short-term U.S. Treasury bills, with minting and redemption facilitated through USDC deposits. The launch aims to attract real-world asset liquidity to tx’s chain while ensuring compliance with the GENIUS Act, which restricts issuers from paying yield solely for holding payment stablecoins. The product links stablecoin infrastructure with tokenized equity trading and onchain asset markets.

**Circle Introduces cirBTC**

Circle argues that institutions require stablecoin liquidity to use Bitcoin effectively as collateral in onchain credit markets, introducing cirBTC to bridge native BTC with smart contract networks. By minting 1:1 backed cirBTC, institutions can borrow USDC against Bitcoin holdings without selling the asset, creating a functional collateral stack where BTC serves as long-term value and USDC provides immediate working capital. The mechanism enables programmable borrowing, trading, and settlement across platforms such as Arc and Ethereum, allowing institutions to access dollar-denominated liquidity while maintaining Bitcoin exposure. The proposal positions stablecoins as essential plumbing for institutional crypto lending and multi-chain treasury operations.

**Saylor Proposes STRC Stablecoin Model**

Michael Saylor announced intentions to transform STRC into a tokenized stablecoin pegged to the U.S. dollar, aiming to convert Bitcoin’s capital appreciation into stable, interest-bearing money. He argues STRC’s current structure as part of a corporate capital hierarchy creates conflicts of interest that hinder its ability to maintain a peg. The proposal calls for a decentralized, peer-to-peer stablecoin model that pays interest and competes with traditional banking. It is framed as a response to regulatory constraints such as the GENIUS Act, which limit domestic stablecoin utility and encourage innovation toward offshore, Bitcoin-backed systems offering higher yields.

## Research on CBDCs, Stablecoins, and Deposits

**CBDC Governance Paradox Study**

A paper in the Virginia Journal of International Law argues that challenger central bank digital currencies face significant relational, technological, and legal complexities that create a governance paradox, making it unlikely they will soon displace U.S. dollar hegemony. The analysis highlights tensions among state authority, private-sector participation, interoperability standards, and legal enforceability, which complicate the design and deployment of non-dollar CBDCs. Its conclusion tempers expectations that digital currency innovation alone will rapidly rebalance the international monetary system. The paper suggests dollar dominance rests not only on financial depth and network effects, but also on institutional, legal, and geopolitical factors that challenger currencies must address before achieving comparable global use.

**Euro Stablecoins and Tokenized Deposits**

A Banca d’Italia study compares euro-denominated stablecoins with tokenized commercial bank deposits and concludes that bearer-form tokenized deposits are economically equivalent to stablecoins. The research argues that both instruments can serve similar payment and settlement functions when represented on shared infrastructure, making their differences more technical than substantive. It recommends consistent regulatory treatment for the two categories to avoid arbitrage, preserve trust, and support financial stability. The finding is relevant to policymakers designing frameworks for digital money, because treating functionally equivalent assets differently could distort competition, complicate supervision, and create gaps in consumer protection. The study strengthens the case for technology-neutral regulation.

**ECB CBDC Holding Limit Study**

A European Central Bank working paper suggests that implementing a sufficiently high holding limit for a central bank digital currency can encourage adoption. The study notes that most CBDC projects anticipate caps on individual digital wallet holdings to prevent excessive outflows of deposits from commercial banks. The recommendation reflects the central challenge of balancing innovation with financial stability: limits that are too low may discourage use, while limits that are too high may accelerate bank disintermediation. The finding implies that successful retail CBDC design will depend heavily on calibrated thresholds, public trust, and complementary measures that preserve bank funding while enabling digital cash use.

## Overall Outlook

The digital-currency landscape is moving from experimentation toward embedded financial infrastructure, with stablecoins serving as payment rails, collateral bridges, and settlement assets across consumer, enterprise, and institutional markets. Regulatory clarity, interoperability, and risk controls are becoming decisive factors as banks, regulators, and technology providers compete for cross-border payment share. Near-term adoption is likely strongest where stablecoins reduce frictions, while CBDCs and tokenized deposits advance in parallel, shaped by stability safeguards and national policy choices.
