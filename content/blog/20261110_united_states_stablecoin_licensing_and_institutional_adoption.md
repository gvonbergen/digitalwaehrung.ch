## United States Stablecoin Licensing and Institutional Adoption

The US Treasury has proposed rules implementing Section 3 of the GENIUS Act, creating licensing requirements for stablecoin issuers operating in the United States. The framework defines when a stablecoin is considered issued in the US and sets conditions for digital asset service providers offering foreign-issued stablecoins, including compliance with US lawful orders and reciprocal jurisdictional agreements. Scheduled to take effect January 18, 2027, the rules aim to provide clear market access standards for payment stablecoins. Treasury is seeking public comment before finalizing the policy, signaling that issuers, banks, and technology providers will face a structured federal licensing regime.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency released final rules implementing the GENIUS Act, establishing regulatory and licensing requirements for payment stablecoin issuers. The clarity has prompted fintechs such as Modern Treasury and Ramp to seek digital asset trust charters. Banks are increasingly debating whether to partner with, compete against, or build their own stablecoin infrastructure. These shifts will shape the FinovateSpring 2027 agenda, with sessions examining competition between stablecoins and tokenized deposits and the impact of agentic AI on payment liability and customer relationships.

Coinbase received final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to operate as a nationally chartered trust bank, a designation that permits federally supervised custody services under a framework excluding traditional lending and deposit-taking. The approval enables expanded custody operations, potential stablecoin issuance, support for tokenized assets, and stablecoin-based services for traditional financial institutions. It also allows Coinbase to assist other crypto firms with token issuance and management, deepening ties with banks such as Citi that want digital asset capabilities without building infrastructure. These moves mark Coinbase’s deeper integration into payments and custody, occurring amid significant declines in its stock price.

PNC Bank launched a limited pilot program enabling eligible Corporate & Institutional Banking clients to receive incoming USDC stablecoin payments on the Ethereum blockchain. Powered by Coinbase Payment products and APIs, the service automatically converts received USDC one-for-one into U.S. dollars and deposits the proceeds into designated PNC accounts. The initiative expands PNC’s existing relationship with Coinbase, including prior crypto trading services delivered through an institutional crypto-as-a-service platform, and marks the second collaboration following their 2025 partnership. The rollout aims to improve treasury-management efficiency, faster access to funds, and cross-border payment speed while maintaining traditional banking safeguards.

## European Stablecoin Markets and Regulatory Frameworks

The EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation establishes a harmonized framework for crypto-assets, imposing enhanced prudential requirements on issuers of asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens to ensure reserve adequacy and redemption capabilities. Stablecoin issuers must maintain high-quality reserves, honor holder claims, and comply with strict governance and disclosure obligations, distinguishing them from financial instruments governed by securities law. Fully applicable as of late 2024, MiCA aims to reduce market risks and standardize supervision across the EU by clarifying classification rules and operational controls for all in-scope digital assets.

The Qivalis consortium, comprising 37 European banks, secured Dutch regulatory authorization to issue a euro-backed stablecoin intended to challenge dollar dominance in the €450 million euro stablecoin market. The project is awaiting final white-paper review under MiCA regulations, while member banks including BBVA and DZ BANK are already using Ripple Custody for digital asset management. The launch timeline remains unconfirmed as the consortium resolves outstanding custody decisions and operational readiness requirements. The initiative reflects European banks’ effort to build a supervised euro-denominated stablecoin alternative that meets MiCA standards and supports institutional distribution.

The total supply of euro-pegged stablecoins reached a record high of €751 million, surpassing the previous peak set in February 2022. Circle’s EURC and Société Générale’s EURCV dominate the market, accounting for 83% of total supply with a combined value of approximately €623.33 million. This concentration highlights the significant market share held by these two tokenized assets within the euro stablecoin ecosystem, underscoring both growing institutional adoption and limited issuer diversity. The milestone suggests euro-denominated stablecoins are gaining traction under MiCA, while remaining vulnerable to issuer-specific risks if liquidity and redemption practices concentrate among a small number of tokens.

Western Europe’s crypto economy grew 1.2% to reach $2.08 trillion between July 2025 and June 2026, with stablecoin holdings surging 131% and lifting the region’s share of global balances to 5.1%. The EU’s MiCA regulation is fostering a more integrated market, evidenced by rising euro-denominated trading and expansion of euro-backed stablecoins, while the United Kingdom leads in domestic stablecoin transfers. Amid fiscal pressures, French users significantly increased self-custodied stablecoin balances by 192%, reflecting a broader trend of users diversifying into digital assets as economic uncertainty mounts.

## Cross-Border Payments, CBDCs, and Global Infrastructure

Prime Minister Mark Carney is advocating for linking Canada’s Real-Time Rail payment system with Europe’s TARGET Instant Payment Settlement (TIPS) system, a move that coincides with European central bankers advancing plans for a digital euro. The initiative is part of the broader Canada–EU Alliance for the Future, which aims to enhance cross-border financial integration between the two regions. By connecting instant-payment rails, the proposal seeks to reduce friction in trade and settlement, aligning with Europe’s digital euro work and reinforcing Canada’s strategy to participate in next-generation cross-border payment infrastructure.

Liv by Emirates NBD has partnered with Universal Digital Intl Ltd and Aquanow ME FZE to integrate USDU, the UAE’s first dollar-backed stablecoin registered with the Central Bank of the UAE, into its banking app. The token is issued under the Payment Token Services Regulation, backed one-for-one by US dollar reserves held at UAE banks, and distributed through Aquanow’s regulated infrastructure. This integration allows Liv users to manage digital asset portfolios alongside traditional banking services within a single secure environment, strengthening the UAE’s regulated stablecoin ecosystem and giving customers compliant access to dollar-denominated digital payments.

Swift is launching a blockchain-based real-time cross-border payment scheme in 2026, with Citi becoming the first major bank to go live, signaling a significant shift by incumbents toward faster settlement rails. Concurrently, Stripe is developing a stablecoin prototype that integrates its Bridge acquisition, targeting mainstream business-to-business cross-border payments amid projected market growth to $409 billion by 2034. These developments highlight the rapid convergence of traditional banking infrastructure with digital assets, as stablecoins and tokenized deposits move from experimental phases into core global payment systems.

Indian Overseas Bank has launched the IOB Digital Rupee app, enabling customers to transact using India’s Central Bank Digital Currency, e₹, through secure peer-to-peer and peer-to-merchant transactions. The application supports UPI interoperability and includes programmable CBDC functionality to facilitate purpose-specific disbursements for government grants and Direct Benefit Transfer programs. The launch marks a significant step in the bank’s digital transformation, offering a secure and innovative payment option that complements the existing digital ecosystem while expanding access to India’s state-backed digital currency.

## Emerging-Market and Regional Regulatory Developments

The Central Bank of Nigeria issued regulations effective November 5 bringing stablecoin issuers and peer-to-peer exchanges under its purview, rescinding the prior blanket P2P ban. Dollar-pegged retail stablecoins must be issued by locally incorporated licensed entities with specific token approval, with existing operators receiving a conditional 180-day transition and market access only under express interim permission. Foreign-issued stablecoins used solely in approved wholesale cross-border settlements are exempt from retail offerings. Transactions involving USDT and USDC are subject to foreign-exchange controls, and P2P or commercial transfers must be linked to a Bank Verification Number, prohibiting anonymous intermediary accounts. Issuers face capital requirements of 10 billion naira for naira stablecoins and 15 billion naira for foreign-currency stablecoins, physical offices for naira issuers, and strict overcollateralization and liquidity mandates.

China and Oman signed a five-year currency swap agreement that includes cooperation on central bank digital currencies, leveraging China’s advanced digital yuan project to enhance cross-border payments. The arrangement signals expanding international collaboration on digital currency infrastructure, particularly between China and Gulf partners seeking more efficient settlement channels. By embedding central bank digital currency cooperation into a bilateral swap framework, the agreement supports trade settlement, liquidity management, and potential future interoperability, reflecting the growing role of state-backed digital currencies in reshaping cross-border finance.

Vietnam introduced stringent regulations mandating rapid incident reporting and detailed custody disclosures for crypto exchanges, driven by recent high-profile hacks and the impending launch of a domestic exchange. The rules aim to strengthen investor protection, improve transparency, and reduce operational risk in a market that has experienced security breaches. For exchanges, the requirements increase compliance burdens around cyber-incident notification timelines and custody arrangements, while giving regulators better visibility into fund segregation and platform resilience. The move signals Vietnam’s intent to regulate digital-asset infrastructure ahead of formal market development.

Ukraine’s draft crypto legislation cleared a parliamentary committee, establishing the securities regulator as the primary overseer of digital-asset markets. The central bank opposes the current draft due to concerns over foreign token supervision and licensing monopolies, highlighting institutional disagreement over how to balance investor protection, market access, and financial stability. The proposal reflects Ukraine’s effort to create a formal regulatory perimeter for crypto activities, including token issuance, trading, and custody, while unresolved disputes may affect licensing, cross-border token offerings, and the central bank’s role in oversight. The outcome will shape the country’s digital-asset policy trajectory.

## Overall Outlook

Stablecoin regulation is converging toward licensed, reserve-backed, and interoperable systems, with the US, EU, UAE, Nigeria, and Asian jurisdictions imposing capital, disclosure, and market-access conditions. Banks, fintechs, and exchanges are moving from pilots into production, while CBDC-linked rails and tokenized payments are becoming core infrastructure. Market leaders will be those able to combine compliance, custody, and cross-border settlement.
