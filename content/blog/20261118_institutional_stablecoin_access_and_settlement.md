## Institutional Stablecoin Access and Settlement

DBS and Circle Internet Group have partnered to expand institutional access to USDC across Asia by letting eligible clients directly mint and redeem the stablecoin through DBS. The collaboration builds on the Monetary Authority of Singapore’s Project Bloom and gives institutions near-instant, 24/7 settlement between US dollars and USDC. By strengthening stablecoin infrastructure, the initiative aims to give treasurers more flexibility for settlement, liquidity management, and balance-sheet operations. It also reduces dependence on secondary-market liquidity, which can be fragmented and volatile, by creating a direct issuance channel that links regulated banking rails with a dollar-denominated digital asset.

The World Bank Group has partnered with Circle Internet Group and Citi to test how USDC and blockchain infrastructure can modernize development fund disbursements. Using Circle’s minting capabilities and Citi’s banking connections, the pilot aims to replace slow, costly correspondent banking with faster, more transparent real-time payments. The exploration focuses on an intermediated, interoperable model in borrower countries where digital asset use is permitted, extending the Bank’s FundsChain platform. If successful, the initiative would redirect more resources toward development impact by lowering transaction frictions, improving traceability, and demonstrating that regulated stablecoins can operate inside mainstream international finance.

Fireblocks has expanded its integration with Tempo, the payments-focused blockchain incubated by Stripe and Paradigm, to deliver enterprise-grade stablecoin payments to more than 2,500 institutional clients. The upgrade supports fee sponsorship, deposit matching through TIP-20 token standards, and seamless transfers inside Fireblocks’ existing infrastructure, enabling cross-border payouts and treasury operations. The move reflects the scale of stablecoin activity on the platform, where stablecoins account for 75% of Fireblocks’ $7.5 trillion annual transaction volume and monthly stablecoin flows exceed $300 billion. For institutions, Tempo integration lowers operational friction by embedding programmable settlement rails into custody, compliance, and workflow systems already used for digital asset operations.

Polygon launched a new API that lets businesses deploy end-to-end stablecoin payment solutions in weeks, using its Ethereum layer-2 infrastructure to provide low-cost, efficient, and regulatory-aware digital settlement. The product targets companies seeking alternatives to conventional processors while managing compliance requirements, and it is designed to raise on-chain activity by making stablecoin acceptance, routing, and reconciliation easier to integrate. The launch highlights Polygon’s effort to position itself as payments infrastructure rather than only a scaling network, competing with established card, banking, and remittance rails. For merchants and platforms, the pitch is faster settlement and reduced fees, with blockchain tools packaged behind familiar APIs.

Stablecoin Development Corp. filed a prospectus supplement to sell up to $100 million of additional common stock under its existing at-the-market agreement with Virtu Americas. The new capacity brings the company’s total available shares for sale to about $135.7 million, combining $35.7 million remaining from the original prospectus with the added $100 million. As of November 16, 2026, the company had sold roughly $64.3 million under the initial agreement. The filing shows continued capital-raising by a public stablecoin-linked issuer, giving the company more flexibility to fund operations, acquisitions, or treasury activities while exposing investors to dilution and market-price risk under the ATM structure.

## Regulatory and Policy Signals

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said banks’ traditional monopoly on payments is breaking down as blockchain-based stablecoins and tokenized deposits compete with legacy networks. He argued that these digital instruments can operate continuously and meet specialized business needs alongside traditional rails, allowing new providers to enter while banks use established customer relationships. Waller stressed the need for clear regulatory frameworks to manage risks such as lost private keys and failed “zombie” blockchains. He also asserted that central bank money must remain the foundational trust layer as the financial system evolves, balancing innovation with monetary stability and consumer protection.

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission proposed a framework allowing technology companies to issue won-backed stablecoins, provided banks collectively retain majority ownership of issuing entities. Under the model, individual tech firms could hold larger stakes than any single bank, combining blockchain expertise with traditional financial oversight to encourage competition and innovation. Kakao, Naver, and Toss are reportedly developing infrastructure for such tokens, aiming to reduce reliance on dollar-denominated assets and support cross-border payments. The proposal is part of South Korea’s forthcoming Digital Asset Basic Act, which remains under parliamentary deliberation, signaling a regulated path for domestic stablecoins while preserving bank control over key issuance structures.

The Swiss Bankers Association urged authorities to create a dedicated regulatory framework for domestic stablecoins, warning that Switzerland risks dependence on foreign digital payment systems if it lacks locally issued, strictly regulated tokens. The association said a lack of domestic issuance could lead to disintermediation and weaker oversight, while overly strict rules could push stablecoin issuance abroad. Its call follows a proposed revision of the Financial Institutions Act intended to introduce licensing categories for fiat-backed stablecoin issuers and align Switzerland with international standards such as the EU’s MiCA. The debate centers on preserving Swiss banking competitiveness, financial sovereignty, and investor protection.

The Central Bank of Honduras signed a no-cost academic memorandum of understanding with Cosmos Labs and Peersyst Technology to explore an instant cross-border payment system and research a possible central bank digital currency. The collaboration focuses on technical demonstrations and use-case development to assess how blockchain can improve interoperability, payments infrastructure, and financial access. It explicitly does not commit the central bank to issuing or implementing a CBDC. The agreement also corrects earlier reports that inaccurately said instant payments were excluded, underscoring the bank’s interest in testing digital rails for modernization while maintaining policy flexibility.

Hong Kong and Swift are testing tokenized deposits to help banks achieve transaction speeds comparable to stablecoins, using distributed ledger technology to make settlement faster and more efficient within traditional banking. The initiative highlights growing industry interest in combining blockchain capabilities with established financial infrastructure, especially as real-time payments and digital settlement compete for institutional flows. If successful, tokenized deposits could give banks a way to retain settlement advantages without surrendering control to third-party stablecoin issuers. The tests also reflect a broader pattern of incumbent financial institutions adopting blockchain rails as internal plumbing rather than launching consumer-facing crypto products.

## Stablecoin Infrastructure and Funding

Stablecoins are increasingly becoming commodity-like instruments, shifting competition toward the infrastructure that supports them, including tokenized deposits, payment networks, liquidity services, compliance tools, and currency conversion. JPMorgan, DBS, and Visa are advancing tokenized deposit and stablecoin settlement solutions, while venture-backed firms such as Codex and Griffin are attracting capital to build essential services. The trend suggests digital dollars will not eliminate intermediaries; instead, revenue will be redistributed among new players that control distribution and routing. Banks are expected to use tokenized deposits to preserve funding advantages, while infrastructure providers compete for the plumbing that makes stablecoins usable across markets, enterprises, and regulated payment systems.

Codex, a Delaware-based stablecoin payments network, closed a $30 million Series A led by Pantera Capital, with Dragonfly and Coinbase Ventures participating, valuing the company at $130 million post-money. The round is one of the year’s larger raises for stablecoin infrastructure and signals institutional interest in issuer-agnostic rails that settle across multiple stablecoins. Codex targets cross-border corridors linking Asia with Latin America and sub-Saharan Africa, using AI-driven automation to facilitate currency exchange and transfers without upfront deposits. The company says the capital will expand its network and competitive capabilities, though it has not disclosed product specifics, supported blockchains, or a detailed use-of-funds roadmap.

Brazilian stablecoin infrastructure firm Avenia raised $25 million in a Series A extension led by Lightspeed Faction, bringing total funding to $44.6 million since founding. The company issues BRLA, a Brazilian real-backed stablecoin, and processes about $1.2 billion in monthly transactions. It plans to use the capital to launch merchant acquiring and a Visa card program, strengthening its position in Brazil’s fast-growing digital payments market. Avenia is also pursuing acquisitions of licensed financial institutions to support regulatory compliance and expand services across Brazil and Latin America. The raise shows investor appetite for local stablecoin rails tied to real-economy settlement, card issuance, and regulated financial services.

Tether Capital invested in Stables, a payment infrastructure provider that processes more than $2 billion in annual USDT transactions across over 160 markets, to expand USDT payment capabilities in Asia and the Middle East. The investment aims to streamline cross-border business payments by connecting local banking systems with USDT settlement through a single application programming interface, reducing costs and delays tied to traditional transfers. The move supports Tether’s broader effort to increase USDT adoption for everyday commercial use in emerging markets. By leveraging Stables’ existing network, Tether is strengthening distribution for digital dollar payments, giving businesses faster and more accessible settlement options across regions with fragmented banking infrastructure.

Tether and CoinW launched Tether’s Ark, a suite of yield-bearing Earn products that converts non-yielding tokenized gold into productive assets. Users can deposit USDT or Tether Gold, XAUT, to earn annualized yields of up to 12%, paid in XAUT, linking stablecoin liquidity with gold’s traditional safe-haven appeal. The products are designed for crypto-native and conservative investors seeking automated channels for dollar-cost averaging into digital gold, along with diversified, risk-adjusted strategies. The collaboration extends Tether’s ecosystem beyond pure settlement, using tokenized gold as a yield-bearing asset and creating new demand pathways for both USDT and XAUT within centralized crypto platforms.

Invesco launched the Invesco Stablecoin Reserves Onchain Fund, IROXX, a GENIUS Act-compliant money market fund designed to give stablecoin issuers transparent, tokenized reserve management. Live on Ethereum and built on Superstate’s FundOS platform, the fund is managed by Invesco’s Global Liquidity Team and invests in short-duration U.S. Treasury securities. It offers a $1.00 net asset value per share, requires a $1 million minimum investment from approved investors, and maintains SEC Rule 2a-7 compliance. Shares can be managed through smart contracts, with daily dividend reinvestment and transactions in both USD and USDC, bridging traditional asset management and blockchain infrastructure.

Maple Finance Chief Executive Sidney Powell outlined an aggressive growth strategy targeting $10 billion to $15 billion in assets under management by the end of 2027, driven largely by expansion into tokenized stocks and stablecoin-backed lending. The firm aims to attract traditional financial institutions by offering loans collateralized by tokenized real-world assets, reducing reliance on cryptocurrencies, while pursuing credit facilities with banks to fund operations. The pivot toward tokenized deposits and equities comes during a leadership shakeup, with co-founder Joe Flanagan and Chief Operating Officer Ryan O’Shea departing over disagreements about the company’s direction. The plan shows Maple seeking institutional scale through regulated-adjacent assets rather than crypto-only exposure.

## Payments Rails, Adoption, and Market Data

The competition between crypto rails and real-time payments is shifting from consumer displacement toward infrastructure integration. Systems such as India’s UPI and Brazil’s Pix dominate domestic transactions, so stablecoins are increasingly functioning as cross-border settlement layers beneath card networks, banks, and remittance incumbents. These incumbents are adopting stablecoins for efficiency rather than launching consumer-facing crypto products. As a result, the strategic focus is moving toward tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins serving as plumbing in the payments stack, especially for business-to-business flows, programmable settlement, and markets where banking access is limited. The trend favors embedded finance over standalone crypto consumer experiences.

Eastern Europe’s cryptocurrency activity declined 7.9% to $642.1 billion in the year ended June 2026, driven largely by a sharp drop in Russian crypto flows. Despite the regional contraction, Ukraine received $12 billion in cross-border stablecoin transfers, mainly denominated in U.S. dollar-pegged tokens, with substantial contributions from diaspora communities. Retail-sized transfers rose 8.3% across the region, indicating a shift toward smaller, stablecoin-heavy transactions. In Poland, stablecoins accounted for roughly 90% of crypto ATM activity. The data shows that geopolitical disruption can suppress aggregate crypto volumes while reinforcing stablecoins as practical tools for remittances, humanitarian support, and everyday digital dollar access.

Tokenized stocks deposited into decentralized finance protocols have surged 24-fold to $300 million, representing 6.3% of the total $4.8 billion tokenized stock market capitalization. The adoption trajectory mirrors that of stablecoins in March 2020, when they first crossed the $4.8 billion threshold before expanding into broader lending, trading, and collateral utilities. As with stablecoins, growth is being driven by increasing usefulness in decentralized finance, where holders can borrow against tokenized equities and generate yield. The trend signals that tokenized real-world assets are moving beyond experimental listings toward deeper financial applications, creating new demand for custody, oracle pricing, compliance, and liquidity layers.

Japan’s three megabanks—MUFG, SMFG, and Mizuho—plan to jointly issue and launch trading of a yen-backed stablecoin in March 2027 to improve cross-border payment efficiency and promote international yen usage. The initiative aims to challenge the dominance of dollar-denominated stablecoins, which account for more than 97% of the market, by leveraging Japan’s comprehensive regulatory framework. Adoption faces significant hurdles, including low profit margins compared with dollar-based issuers and the need to encourage users to retain tokens rather than quickly convert them into fiat. The project illustrates how major banks are using stablecoins as a strategic response to dollar settlement dominance, not simply as a consumer product.

SBI Holdings and the National Bank of Cambodia presented interim findings from their cross-border yen stablecoin remittance trial at the Black Swan Summit on November 15-16, 2026. The joint disclosure marked the first public update on the pilot, which aims to streamline remittances between Japan and Cambodia by using stablecoin technology to reduce costs and settlement times. The trial remains ongoing, and the presentation focused on early results and regulatory integration, with final decisions on full production and scaling yet to be announced. The effort shows how regulators and financial firms are testing stablecoin corridors in real-world settings, prioritizing compliance, operational reliability, and measurable efficiency gains before committing to broad deployment.

A Zelle executive confirmed during Solana’s Breakpoint event that the Solana blockchain underpins Zelle’s ZLUSD stablecoin, which is explicitly designed for bank-to-bank transfers rather than consumer use. Issued by a national trust bank, the token aims to facilitate confidential cross-border payments and has been promoted to foreign institutions for global partnerships. Current issuance data shows minimal activity, with about $85,000 in outstanding tokens backed largely by bank deposits and a Goldman Sachs money market fund. Separately, Zelle plans to launch ZelleUSD, a U.S. dollar-backed stablecoin issued by Anchorage Digital Bank on Solana, to enable international peer-to-peer payments with enhanced privacy features.

## Outlook

The stablecoin market is moving beyond issuance competition toward settlement, infrastructure, and regulatory positioning. Institutional pilots, tokenized deposits, and reserve funds suggest that regulated digital dollars are becoming embedded plumbing for global finance, while funding rounds show capital chasing interoperability, compliance, and cross-border rails. The next phase will likely be shaped by jurisdictional frameworks, bank-led issuance models, and whether stablecoins capture margins as payment tools or remain infrastructure used by incumbents.
