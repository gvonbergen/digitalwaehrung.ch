## Stablecoin Issuance and Network Integrations

Anchorpoint Financial plans to expand its Hong Kong dollar-backed stablecoin, HKDAP, from an Ethereum-only beta to Solana, enabling native issuance and redemption for retail payments and asset trading. The collaboration with the Solana Foundation supports near-instant settlement, though no launch date was announced. HKDAP, par value HK$1.00 and backed by reserve assets, is issued by Anchorpoint, a joint venture licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority in April 2026. A memorandum of understanding with Anchorpoint Financial Limited, a consortium including Standard Chartered Bank, leverages Hong Kong’s Stablecoins Ordinance to create a compliant gateway for retail payments and real-world asset tokenization. Integrating with Solana’s $14 billion stablecoin market, it expands global access to Hong Kong dollar liquidity through a regulated private-sector framework distinct from mainland China’s digital yuan pilots.

Polygon has expanded its Open Money Stack to include support for Solana, enabling businesses to manage bank deposits, stablecoin holdings, and withdrawals through a single application programming interface. The integration targets Solana’s network, which processes more than $2 trillion in quarterly stablecoin transfers, allowing companies to convert fiat currencies into stablecoins and move assets across blockchains using Polygon’s regulated payment services. The expansion follows Polygon’s acquisition of Coinme, which provides United States money transmitter licenses to incorporate regulated money movement capabilities into the platform. By combining bank access, cryptocurrency conversion, wallet management, and cross-chain settlement, Polygon aims to streamline financial operations for fintech and enterprise users. The initiative positions Open Money Stack as infrastructure for corporate treasury management, global remittances, and bridge between traditional banking rails and on-chain assets.

## XRP Ledger and Ripple Developments

The XRP Ledger’s native stablecoin supply grew 25.2 percent to $1.03 billion in the third quarter, driven by RLUSD’s 49.7 percent increase to $1.01 billion, which now constitutes 98.1 percent of the network’s stablecoin market. Stablecoin transfer volume on the ledger expanded 39.5 percent to $14.09 billion, reflecting rising usage for payments and settlement. Tokenized real-world assets on the network also advanced, rising 29.2 percent to $498.7 million. The data shows RLUSD becoming the dominant stablecoin on XRP Ledger, while activity and asset tokenization continue to grow. These trends support the ledger’s positioning for institutional participation, especially as compliance-focused upgrades and partnerships aim to deepen its role in real-world asset markets.

The XRP Ledger is proposing an upgrade to tighten permissioned token rules, aiming to close loopholes that allow unapproved users to hold regulated assets such as stablecoins and tokenized stocks. The initiative seeks to attract institutional players by enabling issuers to conduct necessary compliance checks, including anti-money laundering screenings, thereby supporting the network’s growth in the tokenization sector. The proposal must secure 80 percent validator support to proceed to mainnet adoption, positioning the ledger to better compete in the real-world asset market. If approved, the change would strengthen issuer control over eligible holders and improve regulatory readiness for regulated digital instruments.

Ripple is expanding its presence in the banking sector by securing final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish Ripple National Trust Bank, which will manage RLUSD reserves and provide digital asset custody services. The company simultaneously announced strategic partnerships with Coinbase, Robinhood, and BNY to broaden RLUSD distribution, integrate it into institutional settlement and cash management operations, and enable tokenized trade finance. These developments highlight a growing trend where stablecoin adoption relies heavily on regulatory oversight, robust distribution networks, and seamless integration with existing financial infrastructure. The moves strengthen RLUSD’s institutional pathway while embedding Ripple’s stablecoin more deeply into custody, trading, and enterprise finance workflows.

## Stablecoin Market Activity and Settlement Data

Adjusted monthly stablecoin transfer volumes rose 41.4 percent in 2026, reaching $325 billion by September, while the total stablecoin market capitalization remained relatively flat at $306 billion. Infrastructure liquidity transfers accounted for the largest share of activity, and Asia-Pacific led regional payment volumes, driven by high transaction frequency rather than larger individual payments. Concurrently, tokenized real-world assets grew 51.3 percent to $38.6 billion, with United States Treasury debt emerging as the dominant asset class within this expanding sector. The data suggests stablecoin growth is increasingly tied to settlement, liquidity routing, and collateralized asset tokenization, with Asia-Pacific benefiting from dense payment flows and the United States anchoring tokenized asset markets.

Paybis data reveals that settlement volume on its platform grew by 163 percent over a 13-month period, with stablecoins accounting for more than 99 percent of tracked crypto settlement activity. USDC emerged as the dominant settlement token, with its market share surging from approximately 21 percent to 75 percent as it overtook other stablecoins to become the leading choice for institutional payments. The analysis highlights that EUR-to-USDC is the primary conversion corridor, representing about 59 percent of all fiat-to-crypto volume, underscoring a decisive industry shift toward stablecoins for efficient transaction settlement. The findings show how currency pairs and liquidity preferences shape stablecoin adoption.

## Institutional Adoption and Payment Infrastructure

Stablecoin Development Corp reported $2.1 million in staking revenue for the third quarter of 2026, driven by rewards on approximately 2.32 billion SKY tokens, which represent roughly 10 percent of the Sky Protocol’s total supply. The company posted net income of $50.4 million, largely due to a $58.8 million unrealized gain from a 49 percent rise in SKY’s market price, while maintaining a debt-free balance sheet and expanding its governance structure. As an on-chain holding company focused on stablecoin-related assets, SDEV continues to accumulate SKY tokens to provide public-market exposure to the Sky Protocol ecosystem. The results underscore how tokenized treasury strategies and protocol governance participation can shape public-company earnings in digital-asset markets.

PhotonPay, a payments infrastructure firm, has closed a $40 million Series C funding round led by Blockchain Capital to integrate stablecoin liquidity with traditional fiat banking rails. The company plans to use the capital to upgrade multi-asset wallets, expand global card issuing, and develop an execution layer for AI-driven agentic payments. This initiative aims to bridge on-chain assets with local settlement systems, enabling businesses to convert digital holdings into spending power across more than 200 countries. The financing signals investor demand for infrastructure that connects stablecoin markets with everyday payment systems, especially as autonomous payment use cases require programmable liquidity, compliance-ready rails, and broad merchant reach.

Visa reported that fiscal 2026 stablecoin settlement volume exceeded $25 billion, marking a tenfold year-over-year increase, while its stablecoin-linked card programs have expanded to more than five million cards across over 50 countries. The company is actively broadening its digital asset infrastructure by joining the Canton Network as a Super Validator and gaining validator roles on Tempo and Circle’s Arc, maintaining a flexible approach that allows partners to select their preferred blockchains and stablecoins. In response to this growth, Macquarie raised its price target for Visa stock to $465, citing an implied upside of approximately 13.3 percent. The figures and validator roles show Visa positioning itself as a multichain settlement hub rather than committing to one network.

Visa is exploring the launch of stablecoin-based remittances in the Philippines to address the high fees and friction associated with traditional cash-based transfers, which currently account for a significant portion of the nation’s gross domestic product. A recent Visa survey reveals that adoption is hindered by low awareness and trust, with only 9 percent of respondents trusting stablecoins for remittances due to misconceptions about their function and security. Despite the lack of a firm rollout timeline, Visa aims to leverage Filipinos’ desire for digital payment education and new financial options while navigating the country’s regulatory landscape. The initiative reflects how global payment networks are testing stablecoin corridors in high-volume remittance markets.

## Regulatory and Central Bank Developments

The European Central Bank confirmed strong stakeholder support for the Appia roadmap to build a tokenized financial ecosystem using central bank money, while also selecting network service providers to connect national banks to the digital euro platform by 2029. Concurrently, the Bank of England launched a consultation on accepting tokenized collateral in central clearing, applying existing regulatory standards to these assets and seeking feedback on future eligibility for stablecoins. A new report from the Bank for International Settlements emphasized that central banks must adapt their roles to anchor trust as payment systems shift toward digital, cross-border transactions involving tokenization. Research by Orbital Research also found that stablecoins do not automatically reduce foreign exchange costs, with savings varying significantly based on transaction direction, timing, and regional liquidity conditions.

In October 2026, the United States Office of the Comptroller of the Currency released the final GENIUS Act rule, setting an effective date of January 2027. The rule establishes federal standards for US stablecoin issuers, including a $5 million capital floor and reserve requirements. Concurrently, US regulators clarified that tokenized securities are treated as traditional securities for capital purposes, removing accounting disincentives and driving a significant increase in institutional holders. Brazil banned non-bank foreign exchange providers from using stablecoins to settle offshore payments, while HSBC launched a retail Hong Kong dollar stablecoin under new local regulations. These developments, alongside Mastercard’s integration of stablecoin payouts, expansion of multilateral central bank digital currency initiatives like BLOOM, and new United Kingdom frameworks, signal a shift toward mainstream adoption by licensed financial institutions.

South Africa’s crypto economy grew 52 percent to $39.3 billion by June 2026, driven largely by cross-border stablecoin transfers and business payments. In response, Treasury and the South African Reserve Bank have proposed draft rules requiring cross-border crypto transactions to pass through authorized local providers, aiming to mitigate regulatory arbitrage and money laundering risks. The proposal has faced significant industry pushback, prompting the government to prepare a revised second draft that aligns with Financial Stability Board recommendations. The episode illustrates how fast-growing crypto markets can trigger regulatory tightening, while authorities seek a balance between consumer protection, financial integrity, and maintaining competitiveness for stablecoin-enabled cross-border payments.

## Banking Sector and Emerging Use Cases

Stablecoins, with a global market capitalization exceeding $300 billion, pose both risks and opportunities for the European banking sector, particularly regarding funding stability, credit provision, and regulatory concentration. While widespread adoption of euro-denominated stablecoins could shift banks’ funding bases toward more volatile wholesale deposits and increase spillover risks, banks are actively competing through innovation, such as tokenized deposits and issuing their own stablecoins. As regulatory frameworks like the European Union’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation and the United States Clarity Act evolve in 2027, the banking sector is expected to leverage these tools to maintain its central role in payments rather than ceding ground to fintech issuers. The outlook points to competition between banks and stablecoin issuers over settlement, liquidity, and tokenized deposit markets.

Stablecoins are emerging as the preferred payment medium for AI-driven agentic payments because traditional banking rails rely on human-centric verification methods, such as know-your-customer checks and biometric authentication, which AI agents cannot satisfy. Unlike conventional banks with restrictive operating hours and multi-day settlement times, stablecoin blockchains offer 24/7 accessibility, instant settlement, and low fees through simple wallet key ownership, eliminating the need for intermediaries. Their pegged value provides the price stability necessary for autonomous transactions, avoiding the volatility risks associated with other cryptocurrencies. However, widespread adoption is currently limited by the lack of mainstream merchant acceptance, indicating that agentic payments will require a hybrid infrastructure of both blockchain-based and conventional rails to fully mature.

## Overall Outlook

Overall, stablecoin developments in late October 2026 point to a more regulated, interconnected market where issuance, settlement, and tokenized assets are increasingly embedded in mainstream finance. Growth is strongest where compliance, liquidity, and enterprise rails align, while central banks and regulators shape the boundaries of adoption. Competition among banks, fintechs, and payment networks is likely to accelerate interoperability and tokenized deposit innovation.
