## Brazil: Open BRL Stablecoin Settlement Infrastructure

Brazil’s stock exchange operator B3, together with Santander, Itaú Unibanco, BTG Pactual and three other financial institutions, plans to launch Open BRL, a real-pegged stablecoin fully backed by deposits, with Banco B3 issuing the token and managing reserves at one real per unit, and B3 managing technology. Targeted for Q1 2027 after an early-2026 delay, the project is designed as shared infrastructure for fund transfers, interoperability and settlement of tokenized shares and securities, not primarily a retail payment product. It is intended to complement Pix and STR with near-instant on-chain settlement. Governance will coordinate with the Central Bank and CVM, while unresolved issues include blockchain choice, DREX linkage, undisclosed partner identities, pending Banco Central do Brasil approval and central bank rules not yet finalized.

## Japan and South Korea: Central Bank Policy and Stablecoin Rules

Bank of Japan Executive Director Kazushige Kamiyama urged stricter reserve oversight, matching reserve requirements and stronger links to central-bank money for yen stablecoins, preserving financial stability, “singleness of money” and preventing fragmentation. He said tokenizing central-bank money is only one DLT Sandbox option, with no retail CBDC decision, and signaled supervising private issuers under Payment Services Act. Central-bank money must remain settlement asset ensuring par exchangeability among digital money, including tokenized deposits. His remarks outline draft reserve and redemption standards, with no reserve framework, ahead of MUFG, Mizuho and Sumitomo Mitsui’s March 2027 joint stablecoin. He warned of risks: smart-contract vulnerabilities, liquidity mismatches, automated collateral liquidations, interoperability, cybersecurity and legal certainty. BOJ tests these issues via DLT Sandbox, CBDC pilots and Project Agorá, including real-value cross-border transactions, after JPYC suspension.

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission formally proposed that banks must collectively own more than 50% of any won-pegged stablecoin issuer under the Digital Asset Basic Act. The plan imposes strict capital and reserve requirements, including full backing for outstanding tokens and monthly audits, while capping digital asset platform operators’ ownership at 20% to address Bank of Korea concerns about monetary policy and capital controls. Won stablecoins would be classified as digital payment tokens. The framework seeks to balance financial stability with innovation by allowing fintech companies to be the largest individual shareholder within bank-led consortia.

South Korean financial institutions are rapidly forming alliances to launch won-denominated stablecoins after the government introduced a digital asset bill granting bank-led consortiums priority issuance rights. Major groups including Hana Financial, KB Financial and Shinhan Financial are advancing consortium plans by partnering with banks, technology firms and payment providers to establish distribution and business models. The legislation permits banks to form majority-owned consortiums while allowing technology companies to hold the largest single stake, a structure that has accelerated negotiations previously stalled by regulatory uncertainty. These alliances signal that Korea’s stablecoin market is moving from legislative design toward executable commercial partnerships.

The Bank of Korea is expanding deposit-token pilots under Project Hangang and plans a 2027 trial of tokenized government bonds linked to a wholesale central bank digital currency. The initiative extends the central bank’s experimentation from retail-facing concepts to institutional settlement, testing how tokenized deposits and sovereign debt can circulate more efficiently on digital rails. Together with stablecoin regulation proposals, the pilots show Korea’s effort to build tokenized financial infrastructure that supports payments, securities settlement and cross-border use cases while preserving the central bank’s role. The 2027 bond trial is a key milestone for assessing wholesale CBDC compatibility with existing government securities markets.

## Europe and Global Regulatory Frameworks

The European Banking Authority published a report analyzing the deployment of e-money tokens and stablecoins in decentralized finance, finding that stablecoins serve as the primary settlement asset for liquidity, collateral and settlement. It warned of significant consumer-protection concerns and regulatory arbitrage risks. Unauthorized tokens such as Tether’s USDT dominate activity outside the MiCA framework, while authorized tokens including USDC face scrutiny for potential interest-payment violations. The EBA said DeFi structures can be used to circumvent MiCA rules, exposing consumers to leveraged losses, operational failures and limited redress. With EU consumer adoption limited, it recommended the European Commission assess additional requirements for crypto-asset service providers and AI tools facilitating DeFi access during the MiCA review.

Incoming European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde expressed a preference for tokenized bank deposits over stablecoins and Bitcoin. She views tokenized deposits as more suitable digital money for the Eurozone, citing concerns about the stability and regulatory implications of private stablecoins. The stance underscores the ECB’s strategic focus on leveraging existing banking infrastructure for the digital currency landscape. It also highlights a policy divide between private-token innovation and bank-led digital money, with regulators favoring instruments that preserve central-bank settlement anchors, supervisory visibility and the transmission of monetary policy within regulated deposit-taking institutions.

Ukraine’s parliament passed Bill 10225-d, a long-delayed measure that legalizes and taxes digital assets by aligning the country’s framework with the EU’s MiCA regulation. The rules are set to take effect in July 2027, giving businesses time to adapt licensing, reporting and consumer-protection standards. The law places Ukraine closer to European digital-asset norms, potentially improving access for investors and service providers while creating a clearer tax regime. It also reflects a wider regional trend of jurisdictions adopting MiCA-style frameworks to reduce regulatory gaps, combat illicit finance and integrate digital assets into mainstream financial supervision.

Stablecoin platforms offering foreign-issued coins to U.S. customers face compliance deadlines beginning January 18, 2027 under the GENIUS Act, requiring them to verify issuers’ ability to comply with U.S. lawful orders. A broader prohibition on non-permitted issuers begins in July 2028, but platforms must immediately establish due-diligence files and obtain written representations from foreign issuers to avoid penalties. The Treasury Department’s proposed rules emphasize that meeting the 2028 Section 18 requirements, which involve Office of the Comptroller of the Currency registration and reserve standards, is a separate process that must begin in 2027. The timeline creates a phased compliance burden for cross-border stablecoin distribution.

## Payments, Liquidity and Market Dynamics

Visa, NBX, Tieto and IDT Finance announced Visa Flex Card, expected to launch in 2027, enabling users to spend traditional currency and stablecoins through one credential. Powered by Visa Flexible Credential, the card lets customers set preferences for specific balances, such as local currency for everyday purchases and US dollar stablecoins for international payments, reducing unnecessary currency conversions. The collaboration aims to integrate stablecoins into daily spending by providing seamless access to diverse funding sources. Initial debit functionality will cover Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Finland and Germany, giving consumers a single interface for multi-currency payments while lowering friction for cross-border commerce.

Stablecoin payment volumes rose 45% year-over-year to $482 billion, driven by major financial institutions including Visa, Mastercard and Stripe integrating stablecoin rails into core infrastructure. These firms are moving from pilot programs to full-scale production, using stablecoins for faster, cheaper cross-border settlements that bypass traditional correspondent banking networks. The transition is supported by evolving regulation, notably the EU’s MiCA framework, and by interoperability among instant payment systems. The data positions stablecoins as essential, largely invisible settlement infrastructure for global finance, with institutional adoption becoming central to mainstream payment flows.

New research from Orbital found exchange withdrawal fees can add up to 10% to the cost of small stablecoin purchases, especially on networks such as Tron. The study analyzed 24.8 million USDT and USDC withdrawals and found 70% of merchant payments originated from exchanges rather than personal wallets, exposing consumers to hidden costs. It concluded that routing payments through lower-fee chains, such as BNB Chain, could cut total withdrawal fees by as much as 98%. The findings suggest merchants should offer compatible low-cost network options to improve affordability, reduce transaction friction and make stablecoin payments more practical for small purchases.

Consumers in emerging markets are increasingly adopting stablecoins for everyday transactions, driven by the need to protect savings from currency devaluation and reduce high fees associated with international transfers. Research indicates that a majority of respondents in countries including the Philippines, Indonesia, Kenya and Argentina are willing to use digital assets for routine purchases and household expenses. The findings point to practical demand for stablecoins as a complement to traditional payment methods, emphasizing their utility in preserving purchasing power and facilitating affordable cross-border payments. Adoption is strongest where local currencies are volatile and remittance channels remain costly.

The 30-day change rate in stablecoin market capitalization reached approximately -12% as Bitcoin retreated from earlier highs, signaling a significant contraction in liquidity across crypto markets. Stablecoins serve as a primary indicator of available funds able to absorb trading activity, so a shrinking pool makes it increasingly difficult for the market to handle new selling pressure. Analysts say a sustainable bullish reversal requires the contraction to ease, positive monthly growth to return and an improvement in Bitcoin’s price structure. Until those conditions appear, the market remains vulnerable to further declines, with liquidity conditions acting as a key gate for risk appetite.

Stablecoin Development Corporation shares rose 14% in premarket trading on broad-market gains, with no company-specific announcements explaining the surge. As a micro-cap firm offering investment exposure to digital assets and stablecoin systems, SDEV’s performance is driven by investor risk appetite rather than operational improvements. The company reported $2.1 million in quarterly revenue and holds $7.2 million in cash, covering approximately nineteen months of operating expenses. Future funding needs may lead to share dilution, highlighting the tension between market-driven price moves, limited scale and the capital demands of operating a stablecoin-exposure vehicle.

## Tokenization, DeFi and Institutional Pilots

Ripple is approaching activation of an institutional lending protocol on the XRP Ledger designed to unlock about $837 million in stablecoins currently idle on the network. The proposed framework includes Single Asset Vaults and a Lending Protocol that let investors earn passive income by providing fixed-term loans to payment companies and other borrowers. The initiative is intended to expand the XRP Ledger’s DeFi ecosystem, which currently accounts for only $30.5 million in total value locked, by enabling collateral-free lending mechanisms. If implemented, it would give XRP Ledger users a more formal route to deploy stablecoin liquidity, strengthening yield opportunities and deepening on-chain financial services.

Cosmos Labs Co-CEO Maghnus Mareneck argues tokenized deposits give banks advantages over stablecoins because funds remain on the issuing bank’s balance sheet, supporting FDIC insurance eligibility and interest-bearing products. He says the main barriers are organizational and regulatory, not technical: banks must move ownership to product teams with budgets and build cross-institutional interoperability standards. Mareneck cites Wells Fargo’s upcoming 24/7 tokenized deposit rollout as proof of concept, stressing success depends on integrating with core banking systems and connecting to existing networks rather than building isolated private ledgers. The framing positions bank deposits, not stablecoins, as the likely bridge between regulated finance and tokenized settlement.

Swiss National Bank research examined the trade-offs of issuing wholesale central bank digital currency on public permissionless blockchains. The analysis weighs the benefits of open, interoperable settlement infrastructure against privacy, governance, scalability and operational-control risks that arise when central-bank money moves onto publicly accessible networks. The work contributes to the broader debate over whether central banks should rely on private chains, permissioned ledgers or hybrid architectures for tokenized deposits and wholesale settlement. Its findings are relevant as institutions test shared ledgers and cross-border tokenized payment systems, and they highlight that blockchain choice is not neutral for monetary policy, market access and system resilience.

Swiss National Bank launched the “Swiss Financial Ledger,” an exploratory project with eight financial institutions to assess the feasibility of a shared distributed ledger for settling domestic and cross-border transactions using tokenized central bank money and securities. The initiative is designed to test whether common ledger infrastructure can improve settlement efficiency, reduce fragmentation and support interoperability across banks, market participants and payment systems. It complements research on public permissionless blockchains by focusing on practical deployment questions, including governance, privacy, finality and integration with existing financial plumbing. The project signals Switzerland’s continued interest in tokenized settlement architectures that connect central-bank money with securities markets.

The World Bank Group partnered with Citi and Circle to test how regulated U.S. dollar stablecoins can improve the speed, cost-efficiency and transparency of development finance disbursements. The pilot examines stablecoins as settlement instruments for public-sector funding flows, where intermediaries, foreign-exchange friction and slow correspondent channels often raise costs and reduce traceability. By using a regulated dollar token, the partners aim to shorten transfer chains, provide clearer audit trails and make disbursements more predictable for recipients and institutions. The project connects stablecoin infrastructure to international development finance, complementing broader institutional adoption of stablecoin rails for cross-border payments.

## Outlook

Overall outlook: Digital-currency policy is shifting from pilots toward production infrastructure, with banks, exchanges and central banks testing tokenized deposits, wholesale CBDC rails and stablecoin settlement. Regulatory frameworks are tightening around reserves, ownership, consumer protection and cross-border compliance, favoring bank-backed instruments in many jurisdictions. Stablecoin usage is expanding institutionally and in emerging markets, but liquidity contraction and unresolved technical choices mean adoption will advance unevenly through 2027.
