## Project Faro: Banks pool $270 million for dollar stablecoin

Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Santander, Deutsche Bank, and 17 other financial institutions have committed about $270 million to Project Faro, a joint venture to issue a regulated U.S. dollar-pegged stablecoin. The consortium plans to incorporate the venture in late 2026 and launch the token in the first half of 2027, targeting cross-border payments and digital asset settlement. Participants intend to pool compliance infrastructure and distribution networks to compete with Tether, Circle, USDT, and USDC, treating stablecoins as distinct from tokenized deposits. The project must satisfy the U.S. GENIUS Act’s reserve, redemption, and capital rules while resolving multi-bank governance and licensing challenges. It plans euro-denominated tokens later and is engaging rival initiatives Open USD and Qivalis.

## PNC and Anchorage embed stablecoin rails in corporate banking

Stablecoin providers are increasingly embedding blockchain payment rails inside corporate banking relationships, including partnerships involving PNC Bank and Anchorage Digital. The model lets businesses receive stablecoins such as USDC that are automatically converted into dollars, giving access to efficient settlement mechanisms without requiring companies to manage digital wallets, custody, or rewrite treasury policies. By making the underlying digital assets largely invisible to end-users, the strategy aims to capture the economics of cross-border B2B transactions while meeting corporate demands for liquidity, operational simplicity, and familiar banking interfaces. This approach positions stablecoins as settlement infrastructure rather than standalone consumer products.

## PNC pilots Coinbase USDC settlement for corporates

PNC Bank, the eighth-largest U.S. bank by assets, is piloting a partnership with Coinbase that enables corporate clients to receive Circle’s USDC and automatically convert it into U.S. dollar deposits. The arrangement allows businesses to use on-chain settlement for faster payments, especially cross-border transfers, without managing crypto wallets, custody, or digital asset balance sheets. For PNC, the pilot demonstrates that stablecoins can serve as efficient settlement rails while the bank retains core deposit relationships. It offers a practical bridge between blockchain technology and traditional banking services, reducing operational friction for clients seeking faster settlement without adopting crypto-native tools.

## Bottomline completes first live USDC cross-border payments

Bottomline has completed its first live cross-border stablecoin payments using USDC issued by Circle, executed through its Global Payments Hub and Cash Management platform. The milestone shows that stablecoins can be integrated into established enterprise payment workflows, allowing treasury teams to manage digital assets alongside traditional fiat currencies within existing approval, control, and audit systems. Bottomline aims to provide near real-time settlement for cross-border transactions while preserving interoperability between decentralized finance rails and conventional banking infrastructure. The deployment is an early proof point that stablecoin settlement can move from pilot programs into production corporate finance environments.

## Treasurers drive stablecoin adoption, Paxos says

Paxos says corporate treasury departments have become the primary drivers of stablecoin adoption, replacing innovation teams that previously led pilots. Treasurers are using digital currencies for faster cross-border payments and improved working capital management. Paxos attributes the surge to clearer regulatory frameworks, including Singapore’s MAS Stablecoin Framework and the European Union’s MiCA, which give CFOs confidence to integrate stablecoins into treasury policies. B2B stablecoin payments grew 733% year-on-year in 2025, yet they remain a negligible fraction of global transactions. Adoption is still constrained by financial software integration and accounting standards, indicating that treasury demand is strong but operational plumbing is lagging.

## Merchant pay-in patterns diverge from on-chain volume

A report from NOWPayments and Glassnode finds that stablecoin commerce patterns diverge sharply from aggregate on-chain volume. USDT on Tron and BNB Chain accounts for 86% of stablecoin pay-in value, even though those networks handle only 14% of total on-chain stablecoin volume. Merchant pay-ins also peak between 19:00 and 21:00 UTC, several hours after general on-chain activity peaks. The findings suggest that merchant settlement behavior, not speculative trading, drives much of visible stablecoin usage. For businesses, the data provides practical guidance for optimizing payment infrastructure, routing, and settlement timing to align with real commercial flows.

## Supply slows as stablecoin payment volume accelerates

Stablecoin supply growth slowed to 0.4% in the first nine months of 2026, reaching about $305 billion, while payment volume rose 45% to $482 billion. The divergence reflects higher stablecoin velocity, with existing balances circulating more frequently, and a shift toward smaller, higher-frequency transactions for cross-border and business-to-business settlements. Experts warn that raw blockchain transfer volumes must be separated from genuine economic payments, because the latter depend on verified use cases rather than trading activity, arbitrage, internal transfers, or automated liquidity movements. The data suggests stablecoins are becoming more transactionally active even as issuance growth moderates.

## Noah pitches stablecoins as global payment rails

At Web Summit Lisbon, Noah CEO Shah Ramezani described stablecoins as infrastructure for instantaneous global payments, bypassing traditional correspondent banking networks to connect local payment systems. Noah is capitalizing on growing adoption of digital dollars, particularly in emerging markets, and says it has secured more than 40 regulatory licenses across over 150 markets. Ramezani also highlighted expansion into autonomous transactions driven by AI agents, noting partnerships with major incumbents such as Western Union to integrate stablecoin rails into traditional financial services. The strategy positions Noah as a compliance-focused bridge between crypto settlement rails and mainstream global payments.

## Circle and BNY highlight stablecoin-cash management link

At the World Digital Assets Forum 2026, Circle executive David Katz argued that USDC can reduce cross-border payment risks by enabling simultaneous transfer of value and instructions, improving corporate cash management. He cited Circle’s partnership with Bank of New York, which acts as custodian for USDC reserves, as evidence of growing collaboration between stablecoin issuers and traditional finance. BNY emphasized the security and speed of existing banking networks, underscoring a coexistence model rather than outright displacement. Katz also noted that South Korean firms are preparing for a potential won-denominated stablecoin market ahead of final regulatory frameworks, signaling regional appetite for compliant digital dollars.

## South Korea stablecoin bill needs clear AML rules

South Korean lawyer Jung Young-ki argues that stablecoin legislation under the proposed Digital Asset Basic Act must include clear anti-money laundering rules to support broader adoption. He recommends designating stablecoin issuers as AML-obligated entities with verified compliance capabilities, while clearly dividing responsibilities between issuers and distribution participants such as exchanges. Jung also calls for defined asset freezing procedures, alignment with the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act, and public-private cooperation frameworks. His proposals aim to create a viable stablecoin ecosystem by reducing legal uncertainty and ensuring that compliance obligations are distributed coherently across the market.

## Yield ban prospects remain low, analyst says

TD Cowen analyst Jaret Seiberg estimates only a 10% probability that Congress will enact a ban on stablecoin yield offered by exchanges over the next two years, despite banks’ efforts to close the regulatory loophole left by the GENIUS Act. The statute prohibits issuers from paying interest, but does not explicitly restrict third-party platforms from offering rewards, a gap bankers argue allows stablecoins to compete unfairly with traditional deposits. Seiberg sees low odds because of procedural hurdles, Democratic opposition, and concerns about retail investor optics, though he acknowledges a narrow path if the issue is decoupled from broader crypto market structure bills.

## Arbitrum leads stablecoin DEX growth, ARB price lags

Arbitrum led all Layer 2 networks in weekly stablecoin DEX volume growth, adding $64.2 million, more than triple the combined total of the next seven chains. The surge coincided with the launch of NullMask, a new privacy protocol on the network that enables private transactions and swaps, potentially driving further user adoption. Despite the significant increase in stablecoin activity, ARB’s price rally to $0.188 showed limited underlying buying pressure, with the token trading near $0.1795 at the time of reporting. The contrast suggests that stablecoin DEX growth can be activity-driven without immediately translating into strong token demand.

## BVNK opens Open USD access for businesses

BVNK has launched Open USD stablecoin access for business customers, expanding enterprise-facing access to the Open USD ecosystem. The launch adds a commercial distribution channel for a project that traditional banks involved in Project Faro are also engaging with as a rival initiative. It indicates growing competition among stablecoin providers to serve business users with dollar-linked tokens, complementing bank-led ventures, treasury adoption, and embedded settlement pilots. The development reinforces that stablecoin competition is increasingly focused on regulated business access, payment rails, and enterprise integration rather than retail trading alone.

## Overall outlook

Stablecoins are moving from speculative experimentation into enterprise settlement, with banks, treasury teams, and payment providers competing to embed dollar rails into mainstream finance. Growth is increasingly measured by payment volume, merchant flows, and compliance-ready infrastructure, not just supply. Competition will hinge on GENIUS Act compliance, AML frameworks, yield rules, and regional stablecoin markets. The next phase should favor providers that can combine fast settlement with accounting, audit, and banking integration.
