## U.S. Stablecoin Regulation and Bank Supervision

The U.S. Treasury Department proposed rules implementing the GENIUS Act, creating licensing requirements for stablecoin issuers operating in the United States. The framework defines when a stablecoin is considered issued in the United States and sets conditions for digital asset service providers offering foreign-issued payment stablecoins. Those providers must honor U.S. lawful orders and operate under reciprocal jurisdictional arrangements. Under the proposed timeline, core provisions take effect January 18, 2027, while unlicensed issuers would be barred from offering payment stablecoins to U.S. residents beginning July 18, 2028. Treasury is seeking public comment before finalizing regulations.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency said it is monitoring risks that stablecoin adoption poses to community bank deposits, noting that the impact on lender funding remains uncertain. The agency highlighted significant variance in estimates of deposit outflows as payment innovations advance. The oversight follows prior GENIUS Act guidance that tailored issuer requirements and bank examinations to institutions’ business models, risk profiles, sizes, and complexities. The OCC’s focus underscores the tension between payment innovation and deposit stability, especially for smaller banks whose funding bases may be more exposed to shifts toward stablecoin-linked services.

The Bank Policy Institute urged the OCC to provide greater transparency around stablecoin reward arrangements, arguing that the current case-by-case approach to implementing the issuer yield prohibition lacks clear decision-making processes. The lobbying effort highlights regulatory ambiguity over third-party incentives, which banks fear could affect deposit stability once the GENIUS Act rule takes effect in January 2027. The dispute centers on distinguishing legitimate product features from prohibited yield. Industry participants are calling for public supervisory guidance to clarify compliance boundaries and reduce uncertainty for banks and stablecoin-linked service providers.

## Asia-Pacific Stablecoin Launches and Frameworks

HSBC launched HSBC RedCoin, a Hong Kong dollar-backed stablecoin, on October 23 under a license granted by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. The token is distributed through PayMe, an app serving more than 3.3 million users, enabling peer-to-peer transfers and merchant payments in a staged rollout expected to complete by the end of the week. Reserves backing RedCoin stand at HK$81.58 million. HSBC plans to expand access through its mobile banking app by year-end, introducing tokenized money market funds with a minimum subscription of HK$100, tokenized gold, and third-party funds. The launch, built on private distributed ledger technology, challenges U.S. dollar-linked stablecoins, which account for about 99.4% of the market, and positions HSBC against Anchorpoint.

Anchorpoint, a stablecoin joint venture led by Standard Chartered, plans to launch a retail stablecoin on a public blockchain by the end of 2026. The initiative is part of a strategy to integrate regulated digital money into everyday payments, emphasizing sustainable market development, education, and cross-border payment connectivity. The company is proceeding cautiously with a business-to-business-to-consumer model rather than pursuing rapid consumer distribution. The retail plan follows the recent release of Anchorpoint’s institutional product and coincides with HSBC’s launch of the Hong Kong dollar-backed RedCoin, underscoring growing competition among licensed institutions in regulated stablecoin markets.

South Korea plans to amend its Foreign Exchange Transactions Act to regulate stablecoins as payment instruments. The proposal aims to close regulatory loopholes and improve monitoring of cross-border crypto transactions, reflecting the government’s intent to treat stablecoin activity within existing foreign-exchange controls. The move is part of broader regional efforts to bring stablecoins into formal financial frameworks, alongside Singapore’s BLOOM initiative and Hong Kong’s implementation of tokenized settlement infrastructure. For stablecoin issuers and exchanges, the amendment would increase compliance obligations around issuance, redemption, transfer reporting, and cross-border flows.

Singapore’s Monetary Authority expanded its BLOOM initiative by adding Bank Negara Malaysia and the Bank of Thailand as policymaker members. The collaboration aims to develop common standards for cross-border digital money, facilitating acceptance, exchange, and settlement of tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins across institutions, payment networks, and countries. Industry participants, including Mastercard, Visa, Ripple, and the World Bank Group, will work with central banks to create implementation frameworks that reduce costs and align commercial arrangements while respecting local regulatory differences. The expansion strengthens regional interoperability and oversight for digital assets.

Hong Kong is moving from asset-tokenization pilots to full-scale implementation by integrating tokenized Hong Kong dollar deposits into settlement workflows. The transition signals a shift from experimental use cases toward production-ready financial market infrastructure, supporting faster settlement and broader access to tokenized assets. The development is part of the city’s regulated digital-asset agenda, which includes stablecoin issuer licenses under the Stablecoins Ordinance. It complements HSBC’s RedCoin rollout and positions Hong Kong as a hub for institutional and retail stablecoin adoption, with banks and payment providers testing how tokenized deposits can streamline transfers, collateral, and cross-border settlement.

## European Central Bank and Sovereignty

European Central Bank Executive Board member Piero Cipollone argued that the digital euro is essential for European sovereignty because it would reduce the continent’s dependence on non-European third-party payment providers. He noted that two-thirds of euro-area card transactions are governed by external companies, creating strategic vulnerabilities that the planned pilot is intended to address. The issuance of the digital euro remains contingent on the conclusion of legislative negotiations in Brussels. The ECB hopes to secure regulatory adoption by the end of the year, positioning the project as a core element of Europe’s monetary and payments strategy.

## Market Infrastructure and Adoption

Solana added $22 million in euro stablecoins in the week ending October 25, 2026, surpassing the combined growth of all other tracked blockchains and reinforcing its second-place market share behind Ethereum. The network now hosts compliant issuers such as Circle, Société Générale Forge, and AllUnity, supported by cost-efficient infrastructure and Europe’s MiCA regulatory framework. Stablecoin holder addresses have surged to 14 million, with total on-chain supply exceeding $15 billion. Despite this expansion, SOL fell 13% over 30 days as decentralized exchange volume declined 9% and DeFi TVL remained static, suggesting available stablecoin capital has not translated into sustained network usage or price recovery.

Total stablecoin deposits in decentralized finance protocols reached $24 billion, a 560.8% increase over three years, as users deployed dollar-pegged tokens into lending platforms for yield. USDC leads with a 38.2% share, equivalent to about $9.2 billion, followed by Tether and USDe, which together account for roughly two-thirds of deposits. Activity is concentrated in major protocols including Aave V3, Morpho, Sky Protocol, and Spark. Deposits represent only about 8% of the $298.4 billion stablecoin market, leaving room for expansion, but recent lending pools recorded approximately $99.8 million in net withdrawals amid protocol-risk concerns.

Mastercard representatives publicly praised Ripple’s RLUSD stablecoin during the XRPL Commons Hackathon in New York City, highlighting its role in autonomous AI-driven payments. The endorsement reinforces the companies’ existing partnership, which includes a collaborative study on using RLUSD for blockchain-based settlement of traditional credit card transactions. Mastercard continues to expand its stablecoin ecosystem by integrating regulated tokens such as RLUSD, USDC, and PYUSD into settlement options. The development shows major card networks advancing tokenized settlement infrastructure, pairing stablecoin liquidity with programmable payment rails and positioning regulated digital dollars for broader use in global commerce.

Arbitrum launched a stablecoin growth strategy centered on the USDG token, backed by a 2 million ARB incentive pool to attract protocols and expand decentralized finance liquidity. The initiative aims to integrate USDG as a primary asset within applications, targeting sustainable usage in lending, trading, and payments rather than relying solely on short-term capital inflows. The announcement triggered a rally in ARB, reflecting optimism about Arbitrum’s DeFi ecosystem. Analysts remain cautious, questioning whether the program will generate lasting demand or merely temporary mercenary liquidity that disappears once incentives expire.

Euronet partnered with Fireblocks to enable banks and fintech companies to issue stablecoin-funded payment cards, expanding the integration of digital assets into mainstream banking services. The collaboration responds to growing demand for practical cryptocurrency applications that facilitate everyday transactions through familiar financial infrastructure. By linking stablecoin balances to card issuance, the initiative allows institutions to offer digital-asset payments without requiring consumers to adopt new wallet experiences. The development highlights the convergence of payments, custody, and banking rails, as regulated stablecoins increasingly become usable instruments for point-of-sale spending, merchant settlement, and corporate treasury operations.

## Central Bank Digital Currencies

Russia’s Central Bank reported that its digital ruble central bank digital currency has surpassed expectations, reaching 270,000 accounts since its September launch, driven largely by strong interest from small and medium-sized enterprises. To encourage adoption, the institution plans to introduce subscription services, test offline payments, and enable direct ATM withdrawals. National institutions are also integrating digital ruble payroll options to facilitate broader usage of the currency. The rollout reflects Russia’s push to build state-controlled payment rails amid international financial constraints, while testing how a CBDC can support retail, corporate, and government transactions.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is exploring blockchain technology and digital currencies to create alternative economic infrastructure that could help Israel withstand potential international sanctions. The initiative builds on existing efforts, including the approval of the shekel-pegged stablecoin BILS and ongoing assessments of a digital shekel by the Bank of Israel. Experts note that these tools can facilitate domestic payments and settlement, but cannot guarantee access to foreign exchange, suppliers, or global markets if sanctions restrict financial relationships. The proposal illustrates how geopolitics is shaping central bank and sovereign digital currency strategies.

## Overall Outlook

The week’s developments show stablecoins moving from speculative crypto rails into regulated banking, settlement, and cross-border infrastructure. Licensing regimes, tokenized deposits, and CBDC pilots are converging, while yield, sovereignty, and sanctions concerns remain key drivers.
